## Survodutide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** synthetic, fatty-acid-modified peptide for once-weekly injection under the skin; development code BI 456906.
- **Origin:** developed by Boehringer Ingelheim in collaboration with the Danish company Zealand Pharma.
- **Mechanism of action:** dual agonist at the glucagon receptor and GLP-1 receptor (without a GIP component).
- **Approval EU:** no (as of October 2026).
- **Approval USA:** no (as of October 2026).
- **WADA:** as a substance not approved anywhere, prohibited under S0 "Non-approved substances" (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **B** – randomized Phase 2 and first Phase 3 trials published, no approval.

### How it is supposed to work

Survodutide combines two gut and pancreatic hormones: GLP-1, which promotes satiety and enhances insulin secretion, and glucagon, which increases energy expenditure and stimulates the liver to break down fat. While tirzepatide relies on GIP plus GLP-1 and retatrutide combines all three signals, survodutide omits the GIP component and instead emphasizes the glucagon effect. The manufacturer is thus targeting the liver in particular: the fatty liver disease MASH is the second main area of development alongside obesity.

Survodutide was selected from 19 similar candidates because it engaged both receptors in a balanced way in animal experiments. In obese mice, body weight decreased by about a quarter (Thomas 2024, PMID 38560764). Studies in mice suggest that the appetite-reducing effect in the brain runs mainly via the GLP-1 receptor; the glucagon component acts rather in the body, for example in the liver (Zimmermann 2026, PMID 41638399). These are animal findings; in humans, the effect on energy expenditure is currently being measured in a dedicated trial (NCT06745284).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 10 human studies; in addition, there is the publication of the Phase 3 trial SYNCHRONIZE-2, found through a targeted follow-up search. On ClinicalTrials.gov, 29 trials were registered, including the Phase 3 program SYNCHRONIZE (obesity) and LIVERAGE (MASH).

#### Obesity and type 2 diabetes

The Phase 2 trial in obesity (le Roux 2024, PMID 38330987) showed dose-dependent weight loss of up to 14.9% after 46 weeks. The high discontinuation rate was striking: only 61% of participants on survodutide completed the 46 weeks of treatment, but likewise only 60% on placebo. Gastrointestinal side effects occurred in 75% on survodutide and 42% on placebo. Dose escalation in this trial was comparatively fast.

In the Phase 3 trial **SYNCHRONIZE-1** (le Roux 2026, PMID 42253238), the dose was escalated more slowly. After 76 weeks, participants on the higher dose lost 13.0% of their body weight, and 5.4% on placebo. This is below the values that tirzepatide or retatrutide achieved in similarly designed trials. It should be noted that the main analysis included all persons – including those who discontinued treatment or in whom dose escalation was extended. Gastrointestinal complaints affected 80.9% and 89.7%, respectively, compared with 47.9% on placebo; there were no deaths.

**SYNCHRONIZE-2** (Wharton 2026, PMID 42820639) studied 752 people with obesity and type 2 diabetes. Weight loss after 76 weeks was 8.2% and 9.8%, respectively, compared with 3.9% on placebo; HbA1c fell from 7.4% by 0.8 to 0.9 percentage points.

In the Phase 2 trial in type 2 diabetes, survodutide at a low dose lowered HbA1c after 16 weeks about as much as semaglutide 1 mg (about −1.46 and −1.47 points, respectively); the higher doses led to more weight loss than semaglutide (Blüher 2024, PMID 38095657). However, the semaglutide arm was open-label and the trial was short.

#### Fatty liver (MASLD and MASH)

This is where the real strength of the data so far lies. In the Phase 2 trial in biopsy-confirmed MASH with fibrosis (Sanyal 2024, PMID 38847460), liver inflammation improved without worsening of fibrosis in 47%, 62% and 43% of the three dose groups compared with 14% on placebo. An improvement in fibrosis by at least one stage was seen in 34–36% compared with 22%. The dose-response curve was not linear: the middle dose performed best.

The Phase 3 trial **SYNCHRONIZE-MASLD** (Kaplan 2026, PMID 42252333) with 216 adults showed, after 48 weeks, a reduction in liver fat of at least 30% in 84.2% on survodutide (placebo 24.3%), measured by MRI. According to the stricter analysis, which also takes treatment discontinuations into account, it was 68.5% compared with 28.6%. The authors themselves cite the short duration and recruitment only in the USA and Spain as limitations.

In an open-label Phase 1 trial, survodutide was also studied in people with liver cirrhosis, including advanced stages. Blood levels did not differ substantially from those of people with healthy livers; liver fat, liver stiffness and body weight decreased over 28 weeks (Lawitz 2024, PMID 38857788). This trial was small and uncontrolled.

### Animal and laboratory studies

The preclinical data come predominantly from the manufacturer's laboratories: receptor profiles in cell lines, weight loss in mice with diet-induced obesity and blood glucose reduction in diabetic mice (Thomas 2024, PMID 38560764). Studies in mice and in human tissue are available on the site of action in the brain (Zimmermann 2026, PMID 41638399). Independent animal studies are rare.

### Risks and side effects

**Documented in studies:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints** are particularly common with survodutide. In the MASH trial, nausea occurred in 66% (placebo 23%), diarrhea in 49% (23%) and vomiting in 41% (4%) (Sanyal 2024, PMID 38847460). In SYNCHRONIZE-1, up to 89.7% reported gastrointestinal complaints (le Roux 2026, PMID 42253238). The frequency is high compared with other incretin drugs, even though most complaints were mild to moderate.
- **Serious adverse events** were similarly frequent in the MASH trial, at 8% on survodutide and 7% on placebo.
- **Discontinuations:** In the Phase 2 obesity trial, only around 60% completed the treatment phase – in both groups.

**Still open:** Increases in heart rate are known for glucagon and GLP-1 agonists; a dedicated trial on cardiac safety was conducted (NCT06200467). According to the registry, the large cardiovascular safety trial SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT has been completed; at the editorial deadline, the results had not yet been published in a peer-reviewed journal. Long-term effects of the glucagon component on muscle, bone and amino acid metabolism have not been studied.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved; use only in clinical trials. Gray-market offerings are not legal medicines.
- **USA:** not approved. The FDA has designated survodutide a "Breakthrough Therapy" for the treatment of MASH, which can speed up the review process (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Sport:** WADA class S0 (non-approved substance), as of 2026, please check for updates.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed (registered trials, as of October 2026):**

- **SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT** (NCT06077864): cardiovascular safety trial with 5,531 participants, completed according to the registry; design described in Kosiborod 2024 (PMID 39453356).
- **LIVERAGE** (NCT06632444) with 1,800 participants with MASH and fibrosis, and **LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis** (NCT06632457) with 1,590 participants with MASH cirrhosis – the decisive trials for approval in hepatology, with planned completion dates between 2029 and 2031.
- Further Phase 3 trials: type 2 diabetes (NCT07754461), women with obesity (NCT07850050), cardiac structure in obesity (NCT07850063), persons without prior treatment (NCT07855900).
- Phase 3 trials in China (NCT06214741) and Japan (NCT06176365) have been completed.

**Assessment:** As a pure weight-loss drug, survodutide is likely to have a hard time against tirzepatide, semaglutide and the upcoming triple agonists – weight loss in SYNCHRONIZE-1 was lower and gastrointestinal side effects were frequent. Its niche could become fatty liver disease, especially advanced stages up to cirrhosis, for which there are hardly any treatment options. Whether this succeeds will be decided by the LIVERAGE trials.

### Conclusion

Survodutide is a dual glucagon and GLP-1 agonist that, in Phase 3 trials, reduced body weight after 76 weeks by up to 13.0% (without diabetes) and 9.8% (with type 2 diabetes), respectively. The effects on the liver were more pronounced: in the Phase 2 trial, liver inflammation improved in up to 62% compared with 14% on placebo. Gastrointestinal complaints are very common. Survodutide is not approved; whether it has a future as a liver drug will be shown by the ongoing Phase 3 trials.
