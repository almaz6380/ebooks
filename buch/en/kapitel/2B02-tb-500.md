## TB-500 and Thymosin Beta-4

Online, "TB-500" and "thymosin beta-4" are usually sold as the same thing. That is wrong, and the difference is decisive for the assessment. Thymosin beta-4 is an endogenous protein of 43 amino acids that has been studied in several clinical trials – mainly as eye drops and as an infusion after a heart attack. TB-500, by contrast, is a short, artificially modified fragment of it that has never been tested in a published human study. This chapter keeps the two cleanly separate.

### Profile

- **Thymosin beta-4 (Tβ4):** endogenous peptide of 43 amino acids, present in almost all cells; main function: binds the structural protein actin and thereby regulates cell shape and cell movement. Produced synthetically or by genetic engineering (recombinant) for studies; development names RGN-259 (eye drops), RGN-352 (injection), NL005 (recombinant, China).
- **TB-500:** the fragment of amino acids 17 to 23 of Tβ4, chemically modified (acetylated) at the beginning, sequence Ac-LKKTETQ (Esposito 2012, PMID 22962027). Originally appeared as a veterinary drug in horse racing.
- **Mechanism of action (presumed):** promotion of cell migration, new blood vessel formation and wound healing, dampening of inflammation.
- **Approval EU/USA:** neither Tβ4 nor TB-500 is approved.
- **WADA:** prohibited (S2, growth factors; "thymosin-β4 and its derivatives, e.g., TB-500" are explicitly named).
- **Evidence level:** **B** for thymosin beta-4 (randomized trials, mostly small; large Phase 3 trials in dry eye without clear benefit) · **D** for TB-500 (no published human study).

### How it is supposed to work

In cells, Tβ4 is the most important protein that binds and "parks" individual actin building blocks. Actin forms the internal scaffold of the cell; when a cell needs to move – for example to close a wound – this scaffold has to be rebuilt quickly. Tβ4 provides the building blocks for this.

In addition, Tβ4 apparently has its own signaling effects outside the cell. A review describes several active segments in the molecule: the short piece at the beginning (Ac-SDKP) has anti-inflammatory and anti-scarring effects in laboratory experiments, while segment 17 to 23 (LKKTETQ) promotes new blood vessel formation, wound healing and cell migration (Sosne 2010, PMID 20179146). In cell experiments, the seven-amino-acid actin-binding motif showed the same effect on the migration of vascular cells as the whole molecule (Philp 2003, PMID 14500546).

The idea of TB-500 is based on this laboratory finding: if the short piece carries the effect, it is enough to produce only that – cheaper and simpler than the whole protein. However, it was not exactly the natural sequence that was used, but a form acetylated at the beginning. A study on its breakdown found that TB-500 is rapidly degraded and that in cell experiments it was not the original peptide but a breakdown product (Ac-LKKTE) that promoted wound healing (Rahaman 2024, PMID 38382158). It has therefore not been shown that TB-500 does the same in the body as Tβ4. A 2026 orthopedic review emphasizes that TB-500 is frequently equated with Tβ4, although no biological or clinical equivalence has been demonstrated (Tham 2026, PMID 42937313).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence (search October 2026):** The automated search found **9** publications classified as human studies and **18** entries on ClinicalTrials.gov. Of the nine publications, only six actually study Tβ4 as an active substance; the others measure Tβ4 in the blood or concern other questions. **Not a single** published study has investigated TB-500 in humans.

#### Eye drops (RGN-259)

Tβ4 is furthest along as eye drops. The company RegeneRx and its partner ReGenTree have tested it in dry eye and in neurotrophic keratopathy – a rare condition in which the cornea heals poorly because of damaged nerves.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Severe dry eye (Sosne 2015, PMID 25826322; NCT01393132) | 9 people, randomized vs. vehicle | 28 days plus 28 days follow-up | on day 56, symptoms 35.1% and corneal staining 59.1% lower than with vehicle |
| Dry eye, challenge model (Sosne 2015, PMID 26056426) | 72 people, RCT | 28 days | both primary endpoints not significant; individual secondary endpoints better |
| ARISE-2 (NCT02974907) | 601 people, Phase 3, RCT | 28 days | primary endpoints practically the same in both groups according to registry results |
| ARISE-3 (NCT03937882) | 700 people, Phase 3, RCT | 14 days | corneal staining −0.41 vs. −0.46 points, symptoms −0.4 vs. −0.4 points (Tβ4 vs. placebo) |
| SEER-1, neurotrophic keratopathy (Sosne 2022, PMID 36613994) | 18 people, Phase 3, RCT | 4 weeks plus 2 weeks | complete healing in 6 of 10 vs. 1 of 8 (p = 0.0656, not significant) |

The pattern is typical of a medicine with uncertain benefit: small studies show favorable signals, large studies do not confirm them. The small study with nine people found clear improvements (Sosne 2015, PMID 25826322). But the study with 72 people already missed both prespecified primary endpoints; the authors instead emphasized improvements in secondary endpoints (Sosne 2015, PMID 26056426). In the two large Phase 3 trials ARISE-2 and ARISE-3, with a combined total of around 1,300 participants, the values of the primary endpoints were almost identical in the Tβ4 and placebo groups according to the results published in the registry (NCT02974907; NCT03937882). No journal publication of these results was found in PubMed.

In neurotrophic keratopathy, more eyes healed on Tβ4 than on placebo in the small Phase 3 trial SEER-1; however, the difference narrowly missed statistical significance, and the trial included only 18 people (Sosne 2022, PMID 36613994). A 2025 Cochrane review rated the trial as "low certainty" and found no established difference (Kruoch 2025, PMID 41347649). A larger follow-up trial (SEER-2) is ongoing.

#### Heart

Tβ4 was developed as a cardiac drug because of spectacular mouse experiments (see Animal and laboratory studies).

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safety, infusion (Ruff 2010, PMID 20536472) | 40 healthy people in 4 dose groups, RCT | single dose, then daily for 14 days | no dose-limiting toxicity, no serious side effects |
| Recombinant Tβ4 NL005, Phase 1 (Wang 2021, PMID 34346165) | 54 healthy people (single dose) and 30 healthy people (multiple dose), RCT | up to 10 days plus 28 days | side effects mild to moderate, none serious |
| NL005 in heart attack (Zhang 2025, PMID 41229390) | 96 people with STEMI after catheter treatment, RCT vs. placebo | 90 days | overall no significant difference in infarct size; benefit only in the subgroup dosed within 8 hours (n = 43) |
| Tβ4-pretreated stem cells (Zhu 2016, PMID 27288307) | 10 people with heart attack, randomized pilot study | 6 months | walking distance improved more; Tβ4 was only added to the cells in the laboratory, not given to the patients |

The most important cardiac trial is the Chinese trial with 96 heart attack patients. Across all participants, recombinant Tβ4 did not significantly reduce infarct size after 90 days; a benefit was seen only in the subgroup that received the first dose within eight hours (Zhang 2025, PMID 41229390). Subgroup results are considered an indication, not proof. The authors themselves call for further randomized trials.

#### Skin and wounds

In the 2000s, RegeneRx tested a Tβ4 gel in venous leg ulcers, pressure ulcers and the hereditary blistering disease epidermolysis bullosa. The trial in venous ulcers randomized 73 people; safety was comparable with placebo, and an effect could only be described as a trend for the 0.03% concentration (Guarnera 2010, PMID 20536470). The trial in epidermolysis bullosa was terminated early (NCT00311766). None of these trials led to approval.

#### TB-500 in humans

There is no published human study for the fragment TB-500. A registry entry on TB-500 in cardiovascular disease entered in 2026 (NCT07487363) describes itself in its own description as a "fictitious" example of a registry entry; it should therefore not be regarded as a real trial. The only human publication mentioning Tβ4 for joint complaints is a retrospective telephone survey in which four people had BPC-157 injected into the knee together with Tβ4 (Lee 2021, PMID 34324435) – too little for any conclusion.

### Animal and laboratory studies

Tβ4's reputation as a "repair peptide" stems mainly from two papers in the journal Nature. In mice with artificially induced heart attacks, Tβ4 improved the survival of heart muscle cells and cardiac function (Bock-Marquette 2004, PMID 15565145). A follow-up study in mice showed that Tβ4 activates progenitor cells of the outer layer of the heart (epicardium) and stimulates the formation of new vessels (Smart 2007, PMID 17108969). An independent group, however, could not confirm that Tβ4 causes new heart muscle cells to arise from these cells after a heart attack (Kispert 2012, PMID 22019445) – an example of how important independent replications are.

Further animal and laboratory findings concern skin, cornea, brain, kidney and liver. In rats, Tβ4 improved the survival of skin flaps (Tao 2024, PMID 38757041), and in mice, fracture healing (Brady 2014, PMID 25042765). For tendons, ligaments and muscles – exactly the areas of use for which TB-500 is advertised online – there are only cell and rodent data (Tham 2026, PMID 42937313). A 2026 rat study found, after transection of the Achilles tendon in 32 animals, higher tensile strength and better tissue structure in the TB-500 group than in the control group (Biçer 2026, PMID 42542926). This is one of the few animal studies with the fragment actually sold.

### Risks and side effects

#### Documented (thymosin beta-4)

In the randomized trials with eye drops, gel and infusion, Tβ4 was well tolerated. In 40 healthy people, no dose-limiting side effects occurred with infusions of up to 1,260 mg daily over 14 days (Ruff 2010, PMID 20536472). In the Chinese Phase 1 trial, side effects were mild to moderate (Wang 2021, PMID 34346165). However, these trials are short, and they concern the whole protein in pharmaceutical quality – not gray-market products.

#### Theoretical: cancer

Tβ4 promotes cell migration and new blood vessel formation – two properties that tumors also use for growth and spread. In tissue samples from 138 bladder cancer patients, strong Tβ4 production was associated with advanced stage, lymph node involvement and shorter survival (Wang 2016, PMID 27596300). In cultures of breast cancer cells, added Tβ4 increased the cell count (Larsson 2007, PMID 17495241). Such associations do not prove that Tβ4 administered from outside causes cancer. They are, however, one reason why experts advise caution in people with cancer or an increased cancer risk. Long-term data are lacking. Part IV deals with the topic in detail.

#### Gray market

What is sold as "TB-500" does not reliably contain what is on the label. A horse doping laboratory analyzed products offered online and found that the content of "TB500" and "TB1000" did not consistently match the declared composition (Delcourt 2023, PMID 36482504). Some products contain the fragment, others possibly the whole Tβ4, still others contaminants. In addition, there are the known risks of non-sterile injections. That horse doping laboratories developed detection methods for TB-500 as early as 2012 (Ho 2012, PMID 23084823) shows where the product comes from: the veterinary field, not human medicine.

### Legal status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **EU, Germany, Austria:** neither Tβ4 nor TB-500 is approved as a medicine. Supplying them for use in humans is not permitted; products labeled "for research purposes only" do not change this.
- **USA:** no FDA approval. To the author's knowledge, in 2023 the FDA assigned the Tβ4 fragment (TB-500), like BPC-157, to Category 2 of the bulk substances for compounding, i.e., the substances with significant safety concerns; the evaluation of peptides for compounding is currently being debated controversially (Willis 2026, PMID 42752426).
- **WADA:** Tβ4 and its derivatives such as TB-500 are explicitly prohibited in class S2 (peptide hormones, growth factors), in and out of competition. It is also prohibited in equestrian sport (Delcourt 2025, PMID 39314109).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed (according to the registry):**

- **SEER-2 (NCT05555589):** Phase 3 trial with RGN-259 eye drops in neurotrophic keratopathy, 70 people planned, recruiting, estimated study completion May 2026 according to the registry. Results had not been published as of October 2026.
- **NL005 in heart attack (NCT07586865):** Chinese Phase 2c trial with recombinant Tβ4, 189 people planned, primary endpoint infarct size on cardiac MRI after 90 days; not yet recruiting, estimated completion 2028. Two previous Phase 2 trials by the same manufacturer with 62 and 90 people have been completed according to the registry (NCT05485818; NCT05984134).
- **TB-500:** no credible registered human trial.

**Author's assessment:** Tβ4 has the most realistic chance of approval as eye drops in the rare neurotrophic keratopathy, where even small trials can suffice for approval as an orphan drug – if SEER-2 is positive. In dry eye, the large Phase 3 data argue against a clinically relevant benefit. In heart attack, everything depends on the ongoing Chinese trial. For TB-500 as an agent for tendon and muscle healing, no serious human research is in sight for the foreseeable future.

### Conclusion

Thymosin beta-4 is an endogenous peptide that was well tolerated as eye drops and infusion in randomized trials, but whose benefit was not confirmed in the large dry eye trials and has so far only been hinted at in a subgroup in heart attack (Zhang 2025, PMID 41229390). TB-500 is something else: a modified fragment that has never been tested in a published human study and whose gray-market products demonstrably do not always contain what is declared. Advertising for TB-500 relies on studies with the whole protein – and even these hardly concern tendons, ligaments or muscles. For athletes, both substances are prohibited.
