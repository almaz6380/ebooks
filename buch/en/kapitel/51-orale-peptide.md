## Tablets Instead of Injections

Almost all peptide drugs in this book are injected. There is a simple reason for this: the gastrointestinal tract is designed to break down proteins and peptides into their building blocks. What makes sense for food renders a peptide useless as a drug before it reaches the blood. Part I described these obstacles in detail (chapter "How Peptides Get into the Body").

There are several reasons why tablets are nevertheless being intensively researched. Many people do not like injecting themselves. Injections usually have to be refrigerated, and pens are complex to manufacture. And because, according to current knowledge, anti-obesity drugs have to be taken permanently to work, every simplification of long-term treatment counts. A review describes how oral forms of insulin, calcitonin, parathyroid hormone, and vasopressin had already been tested in clinical studies, and that only semaglutide brought the breakthrough for a widely used agent (Drucker 2020, PMID 31848464).

Today there are two fundamentally different routes to a tablet: either you protect a **peptide** so that at least a small part is absorbed – or you replace the peptide with a **small molecule** that passes from the gut into the blood like an ordinary drug and nevertheless acts on the same receptor.

### Route 1: Peptide plus absorption enhancer

#### The principle

An **absorption enhancer** (technical term: permeation enhancer) is an excipient that briefly makes the mucosa more permeable or protects the peptide from degradation. In the semaglutide tablet, this is the excipient **SNAC**. It raises the pH in the immediate vicinity of the tablet, dampens the activation of the digestive enzyme pepsin, and ensures that semaglutide is present as a single molecule that is more easily absorbed. Nevertheless, according to a review, only about 0.4 to 1% of the active ingredient reaches the blood – and only when fasting and with little water. After a meal, practically no semaglutide is detectable in the blood (Vahora 2026, PMID 42687069). This is the reason for the strict administration rules set out in the prescribing information.

#### The semaglutide tablet in obesity

For type 2 diabetes, the semaglutide tablet (Rybelsus) has been approved in the EU since 2020. For obesity, higher doses were tested:

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| OASIS 1 (Knop 2023), PMID 37385278 | 667 adults without diabetes, tablet vs. placebo | 68 weeks | −15.1% weight vs. −2.4% |
| OASIS 4 (Wharton 2025), PMID 40934115 | 307 adults without diabetes, lower tablet dose vs. placebo | 64 weeks | −13.6% vs. −2.2% |
| For comparison: STEP 1 (Wilding 2021), PMID 33567185 | 1,961 adults without diabetes, weekly injection vs. placebo | 68 weeks | −14.9% vs. −2.4% |

In both tablet studies, gastrointestinal complaints were common: 80% versus 46% on placebo in OASIS 1, 74.0% versus 42.2% in OASIS 4. According to the chapter Semaglutide, the OASIS 4 study forms the basis of the Wegovy tablet approved in the US (please check current approval status). Another phase 3 trial of the tablet is ongoing (OASIS 5, NCT07770841, completion April 2028).

The figures are close to those of the injection. A large direct comparison between tablet and injection in obesity is lacking, however, and cross-trial comparisons are only a rough guide.

**A disadvantage that is rarely mentioned:** because only a fraction is absorbed, the tablet needs many times more active ingredient. A cost estimate for generic versions after patent expiry arrived at 28 to 140 US dollars per person per year for injected semaglutide, and 186 to 380 US dollars for the tablet (Levi 2026, PMID 42437874). The tablet is therefore not automatically the cheaper solution (more on this in the chapter "Patents, Generics, and Prices").

#### Hard endpoints: SOUL

For type 2 diabetes, the benefit of the semaglutide tablet beyond blood sugar has been demonstrated. In SOUL with 9,650 people with type 2 diabetes and vascular or kidney disease, major cardiovascular events over a mean observation period of about four years occurred in 12.0% on the tablet and in 13.8% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.86). The confirmatory kidney endpoints did not differ significantly (McGuire 2025, PMID 40162642). For people with obesity without diabetes, there is as yet no such study with the tablet.

#### Amylin and GLP-1 as a tablet

Novo Nordisk is also developing amycretin (since 2026 zenagamtide) as a tablet. According to the study publication, the peptide acts on GLP-1, amylin, and calcitonin receptors. In a phase 2 trial with 186 adults with type 2 diabetes, HbA1c fell after 36 weeks by 0.5 to 1.1 percentage points more in the three dose groups than with placebo. Gastrointestinal complaints occurred in 26 to 47% depending on dose (placebo 23%); serious adverse events affected 4% on zenagamtide and nobody on placebo (Mora 2026, PMID 42532079). The phase 3 trial AMAZE 9 is testing the tablet in overweight with 950 participants (NCT07720271, completion August 2028). A phase 1 study explicitly examines how the amount of water and the timing of meals affect absorption (NCT07815067) – an indication that this tablet, too, is likely to be subject to administration rules.

#### Oral peptides beyond metabolism

The breakthrough is not limited to weight-loss drugs. Two examples show that oral peptides are becoming a drug class of their own:

- **Enlicitide** is a ring-shaped (macrocyclic) peptide that inhibits the protein PCSK9 and thus lowers LDL cholesterol. Until now, such inhibitors existed only as injections (antibodies). The agent was discovered using a method that searches very large peptide libraries for suitable binding partners, and was then specifically optimized chemically (Johns 2023, PMID 37125593). In the phase 3 trial CORALreef Lipids with 2,909 participants, LDL cholesterol fell by 57.1% after 24 weeks, while it rose by 3.0% on placebo. Adverse events occurred with similar frequency in both groups (Navar 2026, PMID 41879224). Whether this also prevents heart attacks and strokes is to be shown by CORALreef Outcomes with 14,550 participants (NCT06008756, completion November 2031).
- **Icotrokinra** is an oral peptide that blocks the receptor for the inflammatory messenger interleukin-23. In the phase 3 trial ICONIC-LEAD with 684 adults and adolescents with moderate to severe psoriasis, 65% achieved (almost) clear skin after 16 weeks compared with 8% on placebo; adverse events were equally frequent in both groups (49%) (Bissonnette 2025, PMID 41191940). In March 2026, icotrokinra was approved in the US, and the review in the EU was ongoing (Keam 2026, PMID 42243571; please check current status). The path there is remarkable: according to a pharmacological study, icotrokinra works **without** an absorption enhancer. In animal experiments, only 0.1 to 0.3% reached the blood, and yet a systemic effect was measurable, because the peptide is very stable and very potent (Knight 2025, PMID 40629250). Studies in ulcerative colitis are ongoing (ICONIC-UC, NCT07196748).

Neither agent is a gray-market peptide; both are molecules deliberately designed from scratch and tested over years in large trials. They have nothing in common with the "oral" peptides offered on the internet.

### Route 2: Small molecules instead of peptides

#### Orforglipron

Orforglipron is not a peptide but a small, chemically manufactured molecule that activates the GLP-1 receptor (described in detail in the chapter Orforglipron in Part II · A). It is taken once daily like an ordinary tablet, according to the studies without regard to meals or the amount of water (Rosenstock 2026, PMID 41765029).

- **Weight:** In ATTAIN-1 with 3,127 adults with obesity, weight fell by 11.2% after 72 weeks at the highest dose, compared with 2.1% on placebo. Between 5.3 and 10.3% discontinued due to side effects (placebo 2.7%) (Wharton 2025, PMID 40960239).
- **Blood sugar:** In an open-label comparison with the semaglutide tablet (ACHIEVE-3, 1,698 participants), orforglipron lowered HbA1c more after 52 weeks, at the higher dose of each by 1.91 versus 1.47 percentage points (Rosenstock 2026, PMID 41765029).
- **Maintaining weight:** In ATTAIN-MAINTAIN, participants switched to orforglipron or placebo after weight loss with injected tirzepatide or semaglutide. After 52 weeks, they maintained 74.7% and 79.3% of their weight loss, respectively, with orforglipron, and 49.2% and 37.6% with placebo. There was no group that simply continued to receive the injection; the authors themselves cite this as a limitation (Aronne 2026, PMID 42120723).
- **Liver:** With small GLP-1 molecules, liver values were a particular concern. A pooled analysis of seven phase 3 trials with 11,220 participants found no case with orforglipron that met the criteria for drug-induced liver injury (Wharton 2026, PMID 42338042).

#### Not every small molecule makes it

That the route is difficult is shown by **danuglipron** from Pfizer. In a phase 2b trial with 628 adults with obesity, in which the tablets were taken twice daily, weight did fall considerably more than with placebo, but only 39.3% completed treatment; about 38% discontinued due to side effects (Buckeridge 2025, PMID 40539310). According to statements by the manufacturer, development was discontinued (as of 2026, please check). Other companies are developing their own tablets; AstraZeneca, for example, is testing the agent elecoglipron in a phase 3 trial against the semaglutide tablet (Eluminate-2, NCT07662213).

### Peptide tablet or small molecule: a comparison

A direct comparison of the semaglutide tablet with orforglipron in obesity is lacking. One analysis compared the two indirectly by matching the individual participant data of OASIS 4 with the published aggregate data of ATTAIN-1. According to this, weight loss with the semaglutide tablet was around 3 percentage points greater, and discontinuations due to side effects were more frequent with orforglipron (Michalak 2026, PMID 42225305). Such indirect comparisons should be read with caution: individual data were available for only one of the two studies, namely those of the semaglutide program.

| Feature | Peptide injection | Peptide tablet | Small molecule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example | Semaglutide, tirzepatide | Semaglutide tablet, zenagamtide tablet | Orforglipron |
| Administration | weekly to monthly | daily, fasting, with administration rules | daily, no food rules |
| Amount of active ingredient needed | low | very high (absorption about 1%) | normal for tablets |
| Weight loss in studies | highest | similar to semaglutide injection | somewhat lower |
| Metabolism, interactions | like protein, hardly any interactions | like protein | via liver enzymes, interactions possible |
| Hard endpoints | demonstrated for several agents | demonstrated in type 2 diabetes (SOUL) | outcome trial running until 2031 |

The point "interactions" deserves an explanation: peptides are broken down in the body like other proteins. Small molecules such as orforglipron, by contrast, are metabolized by liver enzymes through which many other drugs also pass. For this reason, numerous interaction studies were carried out for orforglipron (see the chapter Orforglipron).

### Open questions

**Do people take a daily tablet more reliably than a weekly injection?** That is by no means certain. Anyone who swaps one injection a week for seven tablets has seven opportunities a week to forget a dose. With the semaglutide tablet, there is the additional factor that even a meal taken too early can practically prevent absorption (Vahora 2026, PMID 42687069). How this affects efficacy in everyday life is not shown by approval trials with their closely supervised participants.

**Are there hard endpoints for tablets in obesity?** Not yet. SOUL concerned people with type 2 diabetes. The cardiovascular trial of orforglipron runs until 2031 (NCT07241390), and orforglipron is even being tested in people with overweight to prevent progression to obesity or to complications (ATTAIN-NOW, NCT07794579, 2,001 participants, completion September 2030).

**Is the tablet the drug for maintenance?** ATTAIN-MAINTAIN suggests that switching from the injection to a tablet can maintain a large part of the weight loss. Whether this is as good as continuing the injection was not tested.

**Will tablets become cheaper?** For small molecules this is plausible, because they are manufactured using standard chemical processes. For peptide tablets, the cost estimate tends to argue against it, because they need much more active ingredient (Levi 2026, PMID 42437874). Specific prices in Europe are open.

**What about drops, orally disintegrating tablets, and nasal sprays from the internet?** Caution is warranted here. A study of compounded pharmacy preparations made in the US found semaglutide or tirzepatide preparations for use under the tongue and as orally disintegrating tablets for which there is no evidence of efficacy and safety (Belcourt 2026, PMID 41689811). In a laboratory analysis, copies of oral semaglutide in some cases contained considerably less active ingredient than stated and released the active ingredient and SNAC differently from the original (Hach 2024, PMID 39379664). A peptide sold "as a tablet" is hardly absorbed without a tested formulation – this applies to semaglutide as much as to BPC-157 or other gray-market peptides.

### Author's assessment

The injection will not disappear, but its monopoly is over. For the phase of substantial weight loss, injected agents are likely to remain ahead in the coming years, because they achieve the most in studies and the cardiovascular data have so far been available almost exclusively for them. For years-long maintenance, for people with a fear of needles, and for countries without a reliable cold chain, on the other hand, tablets are a real advance. Whether peptide tablets or small molecules will prevail will be decided less by efficacy than by price, convenience of administration, and long-term safety. This is an assessment; what is established is only that both routes are being tested in large phase 3 trials.

### Key points in brief

- Peptides as tablets work only with great technical effort. With the semaglutide tablet, about 1% of the active ingredient reaches the blood, yet in studies it achieves weight loss similar to that of the injection.
- Small molecules such as orforglipron circumvent the problem, but achieved somewhat less weight loss in studies and are metabolized by liver enzymes.
- Oral peptides are also becoming a drug class of their own outside metabolism, for example against high cholesterol (enlicitide) and psoriasis (icotrokinra).
- Long-term data, hard endpoints in obesity, adherence in everyday life, and prices remain open.
- Drugs should only be used under medical supervision and in accordance with the prescribing information; "oral" gray-market peptides have nothing to do with these developments.
