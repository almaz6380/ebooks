## Exenatide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** Peptide of 39 amino acids, a synthetically produced form of exendin-4. About half of the sequence matches the human gut hormone GLP-1.
- **Origin:** Exendin-4 was isolated in 1992 from the venom of the Gila monster (*Heloderma suspectum*) (Eng 1992, PMID 1313797).
- **Mechanism of action:** GLP-1 receptor agonist. Exenatide mimics the satiety and incretin hormone GLP-1 but is broken down much more slowly.
- **Dosage forms:** twice daily (Byetta) and once weekly as a depot with microspheres (Bydureon, later as a prefilled pen or autoinjector). Additionally in studies: pump implant (ITCA 650) and pegylated variants (PB-119, NLY01).
- **EU approval:** yes, for the treatment of type 2 diabetes (Byetta, Bydureon). No approval for weight reduction.
- **US approval:** yes, type 2 diabetes (Byetta in 2005 as the very first GLP-1 receptor agonist, Bydureon/Bydureon BCise), the weekly form also for adolescents aged 10 and older. Copycat versions of the twice-daily form are now also available there. Availability of individual products varies – please check for current information.
- **WADA:** not listed (status as of 2026; please check for current information).
- **Evidence level:** **A** – numerous large randomized studies, one large cardiovascular outcomes trial, approved since 2005.

### How it is supposed to work

After a meal, the gut releases the hormone GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1). It signals the pancreas to release more insulin – but only as long as blood sugar is elevated. At the same time, it suppresses the hormone glucagon, which raises blood sugar, slows gastric emptying, and acts on centers in the brain that control hunger and satiety. Because insulin release is glucose-dependent, GLP-1 receptor agonists on their own rarely cause hypoglycemia.

The body's own GLP-1, however, has a decisive disadvantage: the enzyme DPP-4 breaks it down within about two minutes. It is therefore useless as a medication. Exendin-4 from lizard venom also binds to the GLP-1 receptor but is largely insensitive to DPP-4. This chance discovery made exenatide the first representative of a drug class that two decades later, with semaglutide and tirzepatide, would transform medicine.

The original form acts for only a few hours and must therefore be injected twice daily before the main meals. Above all, it lowers the rise in blood sugar after eating, partly because it markedly slows gastric emptying. The weekly form packages the same drug in biodegradable polymer microspheres (PLGA) from which it is slowly released. As a result, levels remain more constant; the effect on fasting glucose becomes stronger, while the effect on gastric emptying weakens somewhat over time.

An important difference from the later drugs: exenatide is only about half similar to human GLP-1. The immune system therefore more often recognizes it as foreign and forms antibodies. In most people treated, this has no consequences; in a small proportion with high antibody levels, the effect may diminish.

### Human studies

The body of research on exenatide is extensive. The PubMed query evaluated for this book (search term "exenatide" in title or abstract, query date October 2026) yielded 437 human studies; ClinicalTrials.gov lists 379 registry entries involving exenatide. Many of these are small mechanistic or combination studies. Three groups carry most weight for the assessment: the AMIGO (twice-daily form) and DURATION (weekly form) approval programs, the cardiovascular outcomes trial EXSCEL, and a series of studies beyond diabetes, particularly on Parkinson's disease.

#### The AMIGO Trials: The Start of the Class

Three placebo-controlled trials, each lasting 30 weeks, formed the basis of the first approval. They tested exenatide twice daily (5 or 10 micrograms per dose) as an add-on to existing tablets.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result (10 µg arm vs. placebo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buse 2004, PMID 15504997 | Type 2 diabetes on sulfonylurea, RCT vs. placebo | 30 weeks | HbA1c −0.86 vs. +0.12 percentage points; weight −1.6 kg |
| DeFronzo 2005, PMID 15855572 | Type 2 diabetes on metformin, RCT vs. placebo | 30 weeks | HbA1c −0.78 vs. +0.08; weight −2.8 kg; 46% vs. 13% reached HbA1c ≤ 7% |
| Kendall 2005, PMID 15855571 | Type 2 diabetes on metformin plus sulfonylurea, RCT | 30 weeks | HbA1c −0.8 vs. +0.2; mild hypoglycemia 28% vs. 13% |
| Heine 2005, PMID 16230722 | 551 people, open-label comparison with insulin glargine | 26 weeks | HbA1c −1.11 in each group; weight −2.3 kg vs. +1.8 kg |

The HbA1c value (long-term blood sugar) describes the proportion of "glycated" hemoglobin and reflects blood sugar over the past two to three months. A reduction of 0.8 to 1 percentage point was considered clinically meaningful at the time. What was new was that weight did not increase, as is usual with insulin or sulfonylureas, but decreased. However, the Kendall study also shows that in combination with a sulfonylurea, which releases insulin independently of blood sugar, mild hypoglycemia increased.

In a direct comparison with insulin glargine (Heine 2005, PMID 16230722), both treatments lowered HbA1c equally. The weight difference was 4.1 kg in favor of exenatide, and nocturnal hypoglycemia was less frequent (0.9 vs. 2.4 events per patient-year). The price: nausea occurred in 57.1% versus 8.6%, vomiting in 17.4% versus 3.7%. 19.4% of the exenatide group discontinued the study, compared with 9.7% on insulin.

#### The DURATION Program: Once a Week

The weekly form was tested in a series of trials named DURATION – against the twice-daily form, against tablets, against insulin, and against liraglutide.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| DURATION-1 (Drucker 2008), PMID 18782641 | 295 people, open-label: exenatide weekly vs. twice daily | 30 weeks | HbA1c −1.9 vs. −1.5; target ≤ 7% in 77% vs. 61% |
| DURATION-1, 5-year data (Wysham 2015), PMID 25744115 | 258 people in the extension, 153 remained for 5 years | 5 years | HbA1c still −1.6; weight −3.0 kg |
| DURATION-2 (Bergenstal 2010), PMID 20580422 | 514 people on metformin, vs. sitagliptin or pioglitazone | 26 weeks | HbA1c −1.5 vs. −0.9 and −1.2, respectively; weight −2.3 kg |
| DURATION-3 (Diamant 2010), PMID 20609969 | 456 people, open-label vs. insulin glargine | 26 weeks | HbA1c −1.5 vs. −1.3; discontinuation due to side effects 5% vs. 1% |
| DURATION-6 (Buse 2013), PMID 23141817 | 911 people, open-label vs. liraglutide daily | 26 weeks | HbA1c −1.28 vs. −1.48; non-inferiority not met |

In DURATION-1, the weekly form lowered long-term blood sugar more than the daily form, with comparable weight loss and without more hypoglycemia. The five-year analysis is noteworthy (Wysham 2015, PMID 25744115): of the 258 people in the extension phase, 153 (59.3%) remained on treatment for five years. In them, HbA1c remained 1.6 percentage points below baseline, and 43.9% achieved a value below 7%. Such extensions, however, have a built-in bias: people who responded poorly or had side effects are often no longer included.

In DURATION-2 (Bergenstal 2010, PMID 20580422), the weekly form was superior to two tablets commonly used at the time: the difference compared with sitagliptin was 0.6 percentage points, and compared with pioglitazone 0.3 percentage points. In terms of weight, the difference compared with pioglitazone, which tends to cause weight gain, was 5.1 kg. In DURATION-3 (Diamant 2010, PMID 20609969), weekly exenatide was slightly superior to the basal insulin glargine (difference −0.16 percentage points), although more people discontinued treatment because of side effects.

#### Head-to-Head Comparisons Within the GLP-1 Class

To place exenatide in context, the head-to-head trials with later drugs are particularly revealing. They show that exenatide is effective but is surpassed by its successors.

In **DURATION-6** (Buse 2013, PMID 23141817), the weekly form was pitted against once-daily liraglutide. Liraglutide lowered HbA1c by 1.48 percentage points, exenatide by 1.28. The difference of 0.21 percentage points missed the prespecified margin for "not worse." On the other hand, exenatide was better tolerated: nausea occurred in 9% versus 21%, diarrhea in 6% versus 13%, vomiting in 4% versus 11%.

In **SUSTAIN 3** (Ahmann 2018, PMID 29246950), weekly exenatide lost clearly to weekly semaglutide over 56 weeks. HbA1c fell by 0.9 versus 1.5 percentage points, weight by 1.9 versus 5.6 kg. An HbA1c below 7% was reached by 40% versus 67%. Gastrointestinal complaints were somewhat less frequent with exenatide (33.3% vs. 41.8%), but injection-site reactions were much more frequent (22.0% vs. 1.2%).

| Comparison | Design | Duration | Result (exenatide weekly vs. comparator) |
|---|---|---|---|
| DURATION-6 (Buse 2013), PMID 23141817 | 911 people, open-label vs. liraglutide | 26 weeks | HbA1c −1.28 vs. −1.48; nausea 9% vs. 21% |
| SUSTAIN 3 (Ahmann 2018), PMID 29246950 | open-label vs. semaglutide weekly | 56 weeks | HbA1c −0.9 vs. −1.5; weight −1.9 vs. −5.6 kg |

The comparison with dulaglutide and with liraglutide versus twice-daily exenatide (the AWARD-1 and LEAD-6 trials) is discussed in the respective chapters.

In summary, exenatide ranks in the lower middle of its class: stronger than the DPP-4 inhibitors (gliptins), weaker than liraglutide, dulaglutide, and above all semaglutide.

#### EXSCEL: The Cardiovascular Trial

Since 2008, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has required new diabetes medications to demonstrate that they do not increase cardiovascular risk. For weekly exenatide, EXSCEL was conducted for this purpose – one of the largest trials of its kind.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| EXSCEL (Holman 2017), PMID 28910237 | 14,752 people with type 2 diabetes, 73.1% with prior cardiovascular disease, RCT vs. placebo | median 3.2 years | Major cardiovascular events 11.4% vs. 12.2%; HR 0.91 (0.83–1.00), superiority narrowly missed (p = 0.06) |
| FREEDOM-CVO (Ruff 2022), PMID 34873344 | 4,156 people, exenatide pump implant ITCA 650 vs. placebo | median 16 months | Events 4.6% vs. 3.8%; HR 1.21 (0.90–1.63), safety criterion met |

The composite primary endpoint of EXSCEL (Holman 2017, PMID 28910237) – death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or stroke – occurred in 839 of 7,356 people (11.4%) on exenatide and in 905 of 7,396 (12.2%) on placebo. This corresponds to a hazard ratio (relative risk over time) of 0.91. Exenatide was thus safe, but not demonstrably protective. Acute pancreatitis, pancreatic cancer, and medullary thyroid carcinoma did not occur more frequently than on placebo.

Why exenatide performed worse in EXSCEL than liraglutide in LEADER or dulaglutide in REWIND has not been conclusively explained. Possible reasons under discussion include a weaker effect of the molecule itself, the high discontinuation rate in the trial, and a pragmatic design with little control. In a joint meta-analysis of the seven large GLP-1 outcomes trials with a total of 56,004 participants (Kristensen 2019, PMID 31422062), the class reduced major cardiovascular events by 12% (HR 0.88) – without meaningful differences between subgroups. EXSCEL therefore fits the overall picture but lies at the weaker end.

The pump implant ITCA 650, a matchstick-sized device intended to deliver exenatide evenly over months, met only the generous 2008 safety margin in FREEDOM-CVO (Ruff 2022, PMID 34873344). The point estimate lay unfavorably above 1, and side effects were more frequent than on placebo (72% vs. 63.9%). The system did not receive approval.

#### Weight Without Diabetes and in Adolescents

Exenatide is not approved for weight reduction but has been studied for this purpose in smaller trials. Among 152 adults with obesity without diabetes, participants on exenatide twice daily plus a lifestyle program lost 5.1 kg over 24 weeks, compared with 1.6 kg on placebo (Rosenstock 2010, PMID 20332357). A meta-analysis of six small studies with 362 participants found a mean additional loss of 4.47 kg compared with controls (Su 2016, PMID 27343423). This is a fraction of what was later achieved with semaglutide or tirzepatide.

In adolescents with type 2 diabetes, the weekly form lowered HbA1c after 24 weeks by 0.85 percentage points more than placebo in a study with 83 participants (Tamborlane 2022, PMID 35679098). A weight difference of −1.22 kg was not statistically significant. This study formed the basis for extending the approval to adolescents.

#### Parkinson's Disease: Hope, Disillusionment, and an Expression of Concern

No other area of use has brought exenatide as much attention beyond diabetes as Parkinson's disease. The starting point was animal and cell experiments in which GLP-1 receptor agonists protected nerve cells.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exenatide-PD (Athauda 2017), PMID 28781108 | 62 people with moderate Parkinson's disease, RCT vs. placebo | 48 weeks + 12-week washout | Motor score 3.5 points better than placebo (p = 0.0318) |
| Exenatide-PD3 (Vijiaratnam 2025), PMID 39919773 | 194 people, RCT vs. placebo, six centers | 96 weeks | No difference: +5.7 vs. +4.5 points of worsening (p = 0.47) |
| NLY01 (McGarry 2024), PMID 38101901 | 255 people with early untreated Parkinson's disease, pegylated exenatide vs. placebo | 36 weeks | No difference in either dose group |

The small study by Athauda 2017 (PMID 28781108) found a difference of 3.5 points on the motor rating scale MDS-UPDRS Part III after 60 weeks, which persisted even after discontinuation. This was widely interpreted as a possible first indication of a disease-slowing effect. The authors remained cautious: whether exenatide influences the course of the disease or merely has long-lasting symptomatic effects was unclear, they said.

The larger phase 3 trial Exenatide-PD3 (Vijiaratnam 2025, PMID 39919773) could not confirm this. After 96 weeks, the motor score had worsened similarly in both groups. The researchers concluded that there was no evidence for exenatide as a disease-modifying treatment. In 2026, the journal published a so-called Expression of Concern regarding this publication (Expression of Concern, The Lancet 2026, PMID 42330995) – an editorial notice that questions about a publication are being examined. The PubMed entry contains no summary of the reasons; the assessment of the study should therefore be made with reservations until the matter is clarified. A pegylated, longer-acting exenatide variant (NLY01) also showed no benefit in early Parkinson's disease (McGarry 2024, PMID 38101901).

In multiple system atrophy, a rare related disease, an open-label study with 50 participants found an advantage in the primary endpoint but no difference in objective measures such as biomarkers or imaging (Vijiaratnam 2025, PMID 40727970). The authors themselves consider a placebo or observer effect possible.

#### Other Areas of Use in Studies

- **Alzheimer's disease and mild cognitive impairment:** A pilot study that was ended early found no differences in memory or imaging measures (Mullins 2019, PMID 31518224). A 32-week study in mild cognitive impairment likewise showed no benefit (Dei Cas 2024, PMID 38565814).
- **Alcohol dependence:** In a study with 127 participants, weekly exenatide did not significantly reduce the number of heavy drinking days. In a post hoc subgroup analysis of people with obesity, an effect was seen; on brain MRI, the response to alcohol cues was attenuated (Klausen 2022, PMID 36066977). Such subgroup findings are hypotheses, not evidence.
- **Idiopathic intracranial hypertension** (raised intracranial pressure without an identifiable cause, mostly in young women with overweight): in 16 women, exenatide lowered measured intracranial pressure after 24 hours by an average of 6.4 cm of water compared with placebo (Mitchell 2023, PMID 36907221). The study is very small; a larger study is pending.

### Animal and laboratory studies

Exendin-4 was originally not sought as a diabetes drug at all. In the first description in 1992, the researchers studied its effect on cells from the pancreas of guinea pigs (Eng 1992, PMID 1313797). Only later did it become clear that it acts on the GLP-1 receptor.

A large number of animal studies followed. In rats from which part of the pancreas had been removed, exendin-4 promoted the formation and division of insulin-producing beta cells and attenuated the development of diabetes (Xu 1999, PMID 10580413). Whether such beta cell regeneration can also be achieved to a meaningful extent in humans has not been demonstrated to this day.

In addition, there were experiments in rodents on neuroprotection (Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease, stroke), alcohol and addictive behavior, and intracranial pressure. These animal data were the reason for the human studies described above – whose results have so far been considerably more modest. This is an instructive example of how difficult it is to transfer results from rats and mice to humans.

On the thyroid: in rats and mice, GLP-1 receptor agonists trigger proliferation of the C cells of the thyroid, whereas in primates and humans these cells have hardly any receptors (Bjerre Knudsen 2010, PMID 20203154). More on this in the chapter Liraglutide and in Part IV.

### Risks and side effects

**Established from randomized trials:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints** are the most common side effect. With the twice-daily form, nausea occurred in 57.1% of those treated in the comparison with insulin glargine, and vomiting in 17.4% (Heine 2005, PMID 16230722). The weekly form causes less nausea; in DURATION-6 it was 9% (Buse 2013, PMID 23141817). The complaints usually decrease over time.
- **Hypoglycemia** is rare on exenatide alone but becomes more frequent in combination with sulfonylureas or insulin (Kendall 2005, PMID 15855571).
- **Injection-site reactions:** With the weekly form, palpable nodules frequently form under the skin because the microspheres are slowly broken down there. In the five-year analysis of DURATION-1, such reactions became less frequent over time (Wysham 2015, PMID 25744115).
- **Antibody formation:** Because of its origin in lizard venom, a proportion of those treated form antibodies. This is described in more detail in Part IV.

**Investigated but not confirmed or uncertain:**

- **Acute pancreatitis:** Shortly after market launch, there were spontaneous reports that led to warnings. In a pooled analysis of two insurance databases with 49,956 people on exenatide twice daily, the risk was not increased without adjustment (relative risk 1.0) and was slightly increased after correction for misclassification (1.3; confidence interval 1.0–1.7) (Dore 2013, PMID 23981106). In EXSCEL, pancreatitis did not occur more frequently than on placebo (Holman 2017, PMID 28910237). Pancreatitis remains listed as a rare side effect in the prescribing information.
- **Pancreatic and thyroid cancer:** In a US cohort study with 33,629 people on exenatide, the risk of pancreatic cancer was not increased (HR 0.76); for thyroid cancer, the estimate was 1.46, with a confidence interval of 0.98 to 2.19, i.e., not statistically significant (Liang 2019, PMID 30474347). A meta-analysis of 50 randomized trials on the entire GLP-1 class found no overall increase in cancer risk, but a signal for thyroid cancer (odds ratio 1.55) that needs further investigation (Silverii 2025, PMID 40437949). A European safety study on pancreatic cancer with exenatide is ongoing (NCT05663515).
- **Cardiovascular risk:** not increased in EXSCEL.

**Notes for practice:** Exenatide is excreted via the kidneys; it is not recommended in severely impaired kidney function. Like all GLP-1 receptor agonists, it slows gastric emptying and can thereby affect the absorption of other medications. For use, contraindications, and interactions, the current prescribing information is authoritative; treatment belongs in the hands of a physician.

### Legal status

- **EU:** Exenatide is centrally authorized as a prescription medicine for the treatment of type 2 diabetes (Byetta since 2006, Bydureon since 2011). It is not approved for weight reduction.
- **Germany and Austria:** prescription-only. Prescribing it purely for weight loss would be off-label use and is generally not covered by health insurers. Some dosage forms are not permanently on the market in all countries – please check for current information.
- **USA:** approved for type 2 diabetes; the weekly form also for adolescents aged 10 and older. Generics are approved for the twice-daily form.
- **Sport (WADA):** GLP-1 receptor agonists are not on the Prohibited List (status as of 2026; please check for current information).
- **Gray market:** Alongside semaglutide and tirzepatide, exenatide plays hardly any role on the gray market. General risks of unapproved sources of supply are described in Part I.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established (registered studies):** As a diabetes drug, exenatide has been largely "researched out"; there are hardly any new approval trials in this indication. Current registry entries mainly concern:

- Modifications of the molecule: pegylated variants are being developed in China. For PB-119, a phase 2 trial with 251 participants lowered HbA1c after 12 weeks by up to 1.18 percentage points more than placebo (Ji 2021, PMID 33687487). Another candidate, JY09 – exendin-4 coupled to an antibody component (Fc fusion protein), injected every two weeks in the studies – is being tested in phase 3 trials against placebo and against dulaglutide (NCT06254014, NCT06257966).
- Neurology: the pegylated variant NLY01 is being studied in multiple sclerosis in a phase 2 trial with 120 participants (NCT07497399).
- Safety: in addition to the European study on pancreatic cancer (NCT05663515), a long-term surveillance program for medullary thyroid carcinoma under GLP-1 receptor agonists is ongoing (NCT01511393).
- Diagnostics: radiolabeled exendin-4 is used as a tracer in PET imaging of insulinomas (rare insulin-producing tumors) (NCT07808879).

**Assessment:** Exenatide will continue to lose importance in diabetes treatment because stronger and more convenient successors are available. Its lasting role is that of a pioneer and a research tool. Whether the Parkinson's hypothesis will be revived depends on drugs that penetrate the brain better and on clarification of the questions surrounding Exenatide-PD3.

### Conclusion

In 2005, exenatide was the first GLP-1 receptor agonist on the market and is among the best-studied peptide drugs of all. In large trials, it lowered long-term blood sugar by about 1 to 1.5 percentage points and weight by a few kilograms; in EXSCEL, with 14,752 participants, it showed no increased cardiovascular risk, but also no demonstrated protection. In direct comparisons, it is inferior to liraglutide and semaglutide. The initially promising data on Parkinson's disease were not confirmed in the larger phase 3 trial. For people with type 2 diabetes, exenatide remains a tested option, but no longer the first choice – the decision belongs in the hands of a physician.
