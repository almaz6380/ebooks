## Setmelanotide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** small, ring-shaped (cyclic) synthetic peptide that mimics the body's own messenger alpha-MSH. Once-daily injection under the skin. Former development code RM-493.
- **Origin:** developed by Rhythm Pharmaceuticals; brand name Imcivree.
- **Mechanism of action:** agonist at the melanocortin-4 receptor (MC4R) in the hypothalamus, a central control point for hunger and satiety.
- **Approval EU:** yes (Imcivree) for obesity due to genetically confirmed POMC, PCSK1 or leptin receptor deficiency and in Bardet-Biedl syndrome.
- **Approval USA:** yes (Imcivree), first in 2020, for the same rare genetic forms (Markham 2021, PMID 33638809).
- **WADA:** not listed by name; as an approved medicine, not under S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **A** – approval based on Phase 3 trials; because of the rarity of the conditions, however, the trials are small and partly uncontrolled.

### How it is supposed to work

Setmelanotide differs fundamentally from all the other substances in this part. It is not a general weight-loss drug but a targeted treatment for people in whom a particular signaling chain in the brain is defective.

This chain is called the leptin-melanocortin pathway. Simplified: adipose tissue releases the hormone leptin. Leptin acts in the hypothalamus on nerve cells that produce the messenger alpha-MSH from a precursor molecule called POMC. This production requires an enzyme called PCSK1. Alpha-MSH finally activates the MC4 receptor – and the signal reads: "Enough eaten."

If one of these links fails due to an inherited genetic change, an agonizing, insatiable hunger (hyperphagia) develops that leads to severe obesity as early as infancy. Diet and exercise alone can do little against it. Setmelanotide acts at the end of the chain and activates the MC4 receptor directly – it bypasses, so to speak, the defective links before it.

Setmelanotide also acts at the closely related melanocortin-1 receptor in the skin. This explains the most common side effect: tanning of the skin (see below) – and it links setmelanotide to the tanning peptides such as melanotan II and afamelanotide (Part II, Group E).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 15 human studies. On ClinicalTrials.gov, 27 trials were registered. Because of the rarity of the target conditions, the trials often include only 10 to 40 participants; the largest published randomized trial (on hypothalamic obesity) enrolled 120 people.

|---|---|---|---|

#### POMC, PCSK1 and leptin receptor deficiency

The first reports were individual cases: two patients with POMC deficiency lost 51.0 kg after 42 weeks and 20.5 kg after 12 weeks, respectively, in an investigator-initiated study, and their hunger subsided permanently (Kühnen 2016, PMID 27468060). Similar findings were described for three people with leptin receptor deficiency (Clément 2018, PMID 29736023).

The approval trials (Clément 2020, PMID 33137293) were single-arm – so there was no continuous placebo group – but included a short blinded withdrawal phase. After about one year, 8 of 10 people with POMC deficiency and 5 of 11 with leptin receptor deficiency had lost at least 10% of their weight. Hunger on a scale of 0 to 10 decreased by 27.1% and 43.7%, respectively. For such rare conditions, small, uncontrolled trials are often the only feasible option. For a condition in which practically no weight loss is to be expected without treatment, the results are nevertheless meaningful.

#### Bardet-Biedl syndrome

Bardet-Biedl syndrome (BBS) is a rare hereditary disease in which the fine cell projections (cilia) are impaired; in addition to retinal damage, kidney problems and extra fingers or toes, it includes severe hunger and obesity. In the Phase 3 trial (Haqq 2022, PMID 36356613), 32.3% of people with BBS aged 12 and over achieved a weight loss of at least 10% after 52 weeks. In the related Alström syndrome, the results were inconclusive. A comparison with a matched control group from a BBS registry found that 58.6% of treated patients versus 6.9% of untreated patients reached the respective success criterion (Argente 2026, PMID 41703984) – an indirect but helpful context.

#### Young children

In the open-label VENTURE trial with 12 children between 2 and 5 years of age, BMI fell by an average of 18% after one year: by 26% in POMC or leptin receptor deficiency and by 10% in BBS. 91% of caregivers reported that the children were less hungry (Argente 2025, PMID 39549719).

#### Hypothalamic obesity

A second, more common target group consists of people whose hypothalamus has been damaged by a tumor (often a craniopharyngioma), surgery or radiation. They frequently develop obesity that is difficult to treat. After an open-label Phase 2 trial with 18 people, in which BMI fell by an average of 15% after 16 weeks (Roth 2024, PMID 38697184), the randomized Phase 3 trial TRANSCEND followed (Miller 2026, PMID 42418774):

- 120 people between 4 and 66 years of age, 2:1 ratio of setmelanotide to placebo.
- After 52 weeks: BMI −16.5% on setmelanotide, +3.3% on placebo.
- Hunger score: −2.73 vs. −1.45 points.
- Serious adverse events: 28% vs. 8%.

#### More common genetic variants

The data are less convincing in people with only one altered copy of the MC4R gene – one of the most common genetic causes of severe obesity. In a small 28-day study, such people did lose weight, but considerably less than people with POMC defects (Collet 2017, PMID 29031731). The larger Phase 3 trial EMANATE on various variants in the MC4R pathway has been completed according to the registry (NCT05093634); a peer-reviewed publication was not yet available at the time of research.

### Animal and laboratory studies

In cell experiments, setmelanotide is considerably more potent at the MC4 receptor than the body's own alpha-MSH and can partially reactivate some defective receptor variants. In normal rodents it led to weight loss, but not in mice completely lacking the MC4 receptor (Collet 2017, PMID 29031731). This confirms that the effect actually runs via this receptor.

### Risks and side effects

**Documented in studies:**

- **Skin reactions at the injection site** (redness, swelling): in 96% in a meta-analysis of seven studies with 185 people (Sridharan 2026, PMID 39924461).
- **Darker skin (hyperpigmentation):** in 62% in the same meta-analysis. Through its action at the melanocortin-1 receptor, the skin tans, lips and moles become darker, and red hair can turn brown. In an observation over up to 46 months, the tanning stabilized over time; no malignant skin changes occurred (Kanti 2021, PMID 34058738). Experts recommend regular skin examinations.
- **Nausea and vomiting:** in 36% and 26%, respectively; diarrhea in 21% (Sridharan 2026, PMID 39924461).
- **Serious adverse events:** more frequent on setmelanotide in TRANSCEND (28% vs. 8%) (Miller 2026, PMID 42418774); the abstract does not break them down individually.
- In the BBS trial, serious events reported included suicidal ideation, which was not classified as treatment-related (Haqq 2022, PMID 36356613). The prescribing information contains warnings on depression and suicidal ideation as well as on spontaneous erections in men.

**Theoretical:** Whether the sustained stimulation of pigment cells influences the risk of skin cancer in the long term has not been clarified; observations to date over several years show no signal.

**Potential for misuse:** Because of its tanning effect, setmelanotide is occasionally associated with melanotan II in internet forums. For people without a proven genetic defect, it is not a suitable weight-loss agent and has not been studied for that purpose.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** approved (Imcivree), prescription only, only for genetically confirmed forms (POMC, PCSK1, leptin receptor deficiency, Bardet-Biedl syndrome). The diagnosis requires genetic testing; initiation and monitoring belong in specialized centers. Details on the extension to hypothalamic obesity: as of 2026, please check for updates.
- **USA:** approved since 2020 (POMC, PCSK1, LEPR), later extended to Bardet-Biedl syndrome and to younger children; an application for hypothalamic obesity has been submitted on the basis of TRANSCEND (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Sport:** not named on the WADA list (as of 2026, please check for updates).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed (registered trials, as of October 2026):**

- **TRANSCEND** (NCT05774756) continues in an extension; a substudy is examining congenital hypothalamic obesity (NCT06760546).
- **Prader-Willi syndrome** (NCT06772597): Phase 2 with 18 participants.
- **Long-term observation** in BBS and severe genetic obesity in routine practice (NCT07674290).
- **EMANATE** (NCT05093634) and **DAYBREAK** (NCT04963231) on further gene variants: completed, publication pending.

**Assessment:** Setmelanotide has shown that precision medicine is possible in obesity: if you know the cause, you can treat it in a targeted way. The target group remains small; with hypothalamic obesity, however, it could expand considerably. For the great majority of people with obesity, in whom no such genetic defect is present, GLP-1-based drugs are more effective and better studied.

### Conclusion

Setmelanotide is a small cyclic peptide that activates the MC4 receptor in the brain and thereby bypasses defective links of the leptin-melanocortin pathway. In small approval trials, 80% of people with POMC deficiency and 45% with leptin receptor deficiency lost at least 10% of their weight; in hypothalamic obesity, BMI fell by 16.5% after one year compared with an increase on placebo. Almost all those treated develop skin reactions at the injection site, and the majority darker skin. Setmelanotide is approved in the EU and the USA for rare genetic forms of obesity and is not a drug for general weight loss.
