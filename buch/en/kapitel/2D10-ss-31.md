## SS-31 (Elamipretide)

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic tetrapeptide of four building blocks (D-arginine, dimethyltyrosine, lysine, phenylalanine) that penetrates cell membranes thanks to an unusual sequence of positively charged and ring-shaped amino acids. Research designations SS-31, MTP-131 and Bendavia; international nonproprietary name elamipretide; brand name Forzinity.
- **Origin:** developed by Hazel Szeto and Peter Schiller (hence "SS" peptides), clinically developed further by the US company Stealth BioTherapeutics.
- **Mechanism of action:** accumulates at the inner membrane of the mitochondria and binds there to cardiolipin, a lipid indispensable for energy production. It is said to stabilize the respiratory chain and limit harmful oxidation.
- **EU approval:** no (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **US approval:** yes – in September 2025, **accelerated approval** as Forzinity to improve muscle strength in Barth syndrome for adults and children weighing at least 30 kg. The approval is contingent on a confirmatory trial.
- **WADA:** not listed by name (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **B** – numerous randomized trials, including a phase 3 trial with 218 participants; however, most of the large trials missed their primary endpoint, and the approval is based on a very small study in an extremely rare disease.

### How it is supposed to work

Mitochondria produce energy via a chain of protein complexes in their inner membrane. For these complexes to work together properly, cardiolipin is needed – a special lipid found only in this membrane. If cardiolipin is damaged or missing, the respiratory chain works less well, and the protein cytochrome c can turn from an electron carrier into a harmful enzyme (a peroxidase).

According to its developer, SS-31 binds selectively to cardiolipin via electrostatic and hydrophobic forces, prevents this conversion of cytochrome c and thereby protects the structure of the mitochondria (Szeto 2014, PMID 24117165). The principle has been studied in many animal models of heart, kidney, muscle and eye diseases.

The connection is clearest in **Barth syndrome**: in this very rare, X-linked disease, which affects almost exclusively boys and men, the gene *TAZ*, which is needed for the remodeling of cardiolipin, is defective. Those affected have heart muscle weakness, muscle weakness, severe fatigability and a reduced number of certain white blood cells. An agent that specifically targets cardiolipin is biologically particularly obvious here.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 19 publications classified as human studies; on closer inspection, four of them concern other agents (the abbreviation "SS-31" also appears elsewhere). On ClinicalTrials.gov, 21 studies were registered, almost all by the manufacturer, on Barth syndrome, mitochondrial myopathies, heart failure, heart attack, renal artery stenosis, dry macular degeneration and eye diseases. Elamipretide is thus the best-studied peptide in this part.

#### Barth syndrome: TAZPOWER and the approval

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| TAZPOWER part 1 (Reid Thompson 2021), PMID 33077895 | 12 people, double-blind, crossover vs. placebo | 12 weeks each | both primary endpoints (6-minute walk test, symptom scale) missed |
| TAZPOWER open-label extension, PMID 33077895 | 10 people, all on elamipretide | 36 weeks | walking distance +95.9 m, symptom scale −2.1 points |
| Extension to 168 weeks (Thompson 2024), PMID 38602181 | 10 enrolled, 8 to week 168 | 168 weeks | cumulative walking distance +96.1 m; cardiac volumes improved |
| Comparison with natural history (Hornby 2022), PMID 36056411 | 8 treated vs. 19 untreated people from a disease registry | 64–76 weeks | difference in walk test 79.7 and 91.0 m in favor of elamipretide |

The story of the approval exemplifies how decisions are made in extremely rare diseases:

**The controlled part failed.** In TAZPOWER (NCT03098797), twelve people with Barth syndrome received daily subcutaneous elamipretide or placebo for twelve weeks and then the other in each case. In this blinded part, no significant difference was achieved in either the 6-minute walk test or the symptom scale (Reid Thompson 2021, PMID 33077895).

**The open-label extension was positive.** Ten people continued treatment open-label. After 36 weeks, they walked on average 95.9 m farther than at baseline, and knee extensor strength and individual cardiac parameters also improved (Reid Thompson 2021, PMID 33077895). The improvement persisted to week 168; eight people reached that time point (Thompson 2024, PMID 38602181). Without a control group, however, it is impossible to separate what share is due to the drug and what to familiarization with the test, motivation or chance.

**Comparison with a registry control.** To clarify this, a further study compared eight treated people with 19 untreated people from a natural history study. The advantage in the walk test after 64 weeks was 79.7 m (Hornby 2022, PMID 36056411). Such "historical controls" are often the only option in rare diseases but are considered much weaker than randomization.

**The FDA decision in 2025.** The US drug agency initially rejected the application in May 2025 (Complete Response Letter). In September 2025, it then granted **accelerated approval** for elamipretide (Forzinity) to improve muscle strength in Barth syndrome for patients weighing at least 30 kg – as the first disease-specific treatment for this condition (Shirley 2026, PMID 41335372). The basis was the improvement in knee extensor strength, which the agency regards as a surrogate measure that makes a clinical benefit "reasonably likely." Accelerated approval obligates the manufacturer to conduct a confirmatory trial; if the benefit is not shown there, the approval can be withdrawn. A corresponding trial with 48 planned participants has been recruiting since July 2026 (NCT07531251).

#### Primary mitochondrial myopathy: MMPOWER-3

Mitochondrial myopathies are hereditary diseases in which energy production in the muscles is impaired; those affected suffer from muscle weakness and severe fatigability.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| MMPOWER (Karaa 2018), PMID 29500292 | 36 people, infusion at increasing doses vs. placebo | 5 days | highest dose +64.5 m in walk test vs. +20.4 m (p = 0.053) |
| MMPOWER-2 (Karaa 2020), PMID 32096613 | 30 people, crossover vs. placebo | 4 weeks each | walk test +19.8 m (not significant); less fatigue |
| MMPOWER-3 (Karaa 2023), PMID 37268435 | 218 people, phase 3 vs. placebo | 24 weeks | both primary endpoints missed; walk test difference −3.2 m |

After encouraging small studies, **MMPOWER-3** (NCT03323749) was meant to prove efficacy. 218 adults with genetically confirmed mitochondrial myopathy received elamipretide or placebo for 24 weeks. The trial missed both primary endpoints: in the 6-minute walk test the difference was −3.2 m (95% CI −18.7 to 12.3), and there was likewise no difference on the fatigue scale. Tolerability was good (Karaa 2023, PMID 37268435). The authors classify this as evidence of the highest class that elamipretide does not improve walking distance and fatigue in this group.

A post hoc analysis found indications of a benefit in a subgroup: people with defects in nuclear genes required for replication of mitochondrial DNA (such as *POLG* and *TWNK*) walked 25.2 m farther on elamipretide compared with 2.0 m on placebo (p = 0.06) (Karaa 2024, PMID 39574155). Post hoc subgroup analyses generate hypotheses, not evidence. On this basis, the phase 3 trial NuPOWER (NCT05162768) with 102 participants was conducted; according to the registry, it was completed in December 2024, and results had not been published at the time of our research.

#### Heart, kidney, eye and aging

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMBRACE STEMI (Gibson 2016), PMID 26586786 | people with first anterior myocardial infarction, infusion during catheter treatment, double-blind | 72 hours | infarct size not reduced |
| PROGRESS-HF (Butler 2020), PMID 32068002 | 71 people with heart failure (HFrEF), vs. placebo | 4 weeks | no improvement in cardiac volumes |
| Daubert 2017, PMID 29217757 | 36 people with heart failure, single infusion | 24 hours | briefly smaller cardiac volumes at the highest dose |
| Saad 2017, PMID 28916603 | 14 people with renal artery stenosis undergoing stenting | 3 months | less oxygen deprivation, better renal blood flow (pilot study) |
| ReCLAIM-2 (Ehlers 2025), PMID 39605874 | 176 people with dry macular degeneration, vs. placebo | 48 weeks | primary endpoints missed; slower loss of photoreceptor structures (secondary endpoint) |
| Roshanravan 2021, PMID 34264994 | 39 healthy older people, single infusion vs. placebo | 7 days | briefly higher energy capacity in a hand muscle, but no better fatigue resistance |

The pattern is striking: in **heart attack** and **heart failure**, the larger controlled trials showed no benefit (Gibson 2016, PMID 26586786; Butler 2020, PMID 32068002). In **dry macular degeneration**, a common cause of vision loss in old age, ReCLAIM-2 missed both primary endpoints (low-luminance visual acuity and growth of the damaged retinal area); a prespecified secondary endpoint – loss of the photoreceptor layer – progressed more slowly on elamipretide (Ehlers 2025, PMID 39605874). This measure now serves as the primary endpoint of the ongoing phase 3 trial ReNEW (NCT06373731).

Of interest to the longevity scene is the study in older people: a single infusion increased the energy capacity of a hand muscle immediately afterward; after seven days the effect had disappeared, and muscle endurance did not improve (Roshanravan 2021, PMID 34264994). No "anti-aging" benefit can be derived from this.

### Animal and laboratory studies

In animal models, favorable effects have been described in heart failure, kidney damage, muscle weakness, and eye and nerve diseases (Szeto 2014, PMID 24117165). In dogs with advanced heart failure, long-term treatment improved pumping function and mitochondrial function (Sabbah 2016, PMID 26839394). In a rat model of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, elamipretide improved the energy metabolism of the heart muscle but not cardiac function (Schauer 2026, PMID 41596703). This discrepancy between better energy metabolism and a lack of clinical benefit is mirrored in the human studies.

### Risks and side effects

**Established:**

- The most common side effects are **injection site reactions** (itching, pain, bruising, redness). In MMPOWER-2, they occurred in 80% of those treated, mostly mild (Karaa 2020, PMID 32096613). In ReCLAIM-2, 86% on elamipretide and 71% on placebo reported some adverse event (Ehlers 2025, PMID 39605874).
- Serious side effects were not more frequent in the trials; in MMPOWER-3, events were predominantly mild to moderate (Karaa 2023, PMID 37268435).
- Long-term data exist only for a few people with Barth syndrome over a little more than three years (Thompson 2024, PMID 38602181).

**Theoretical:** An agent that continuously intervenes in the mitochondria of all body cells could have unknown effects with years of use in healthy people; there are no data on this.

**Gray market:** "SS-31" is sold online as powder for injection for "mitochondrial rejuvenation." These products are not the approved medicine; content and purity are unverified. No benefit has been demonstrated for healthy people.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved. Elamipretide is a medicine; use is possible only within studies or compassionate use programs (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **USA:** granted accelerated approval (Forzinity, September 2025) only for Barth syndrome in patients weighing at least 30 kg; refer to the prescribing information and medical supervision. For other diseases, there is an expanded access program (NCT04689360).
- **Sport:** not listed by name on the WADA list (as of 2026, please check for updates).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established (registered studies, as of October 2026):**

- **ReNEW** (NCT06373731): phase 3 in dry macular degeneration, 313 participants, recruitment completed, planned completion 2027.
- **4TAZPower** (NCT07531251): confirmatory trial in Barth syndrome with 48 planned participants, recruiting.
- **NuPOWER** (NCT05162768): phase 3 in mitochondrial disease due to nuclear DNA mutations, 102 participants, completed; results pending.
- A small phase 2 trial in healthy older people is examining physical function (NCT07275424).

**Assessment:** Whether elamipretide finds a place beyond Barth syndrome will be decided by ReNEW and NuPOWER. The experience from MMPOWER-3, EMBRACE and PROGRESS-HF counsels caution: a convincing mechanism does not guarantee clinical benefit.

### Conclusion

Elamipretide (SS-31) is a tetrapeptide that binds to cardiolipin in the mitochondria and, as the first mitochondrial drug, received accelerated approval in the USA in 2025 for the very rare Barth syndrome – based on a study of twelve people whose controlled part missed its primary endpoints, and on open-label long-term data. In larger randomized trials in mitochondrial myopathy, heart attack, heart failure and macular degeneration, the primary endpoints were not met. The drug is well tolerated; injection site reactions in particular are common. For healthy people or as an "anti-aging" agent, there is no demonstrated benefit.
