## Pinealon

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic tripeptide of three amino acids: Glu-Asp-Arg (abbreviated EDR).
- **Origin:** developed at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology (the group around Vladimir Khavinson, which also developed Epitalon). The name refers to the pineal gland (glandula pinealis), although the peptide is described mainly as a protective agent for nerve cells.
- **Mechanism of action:** as a "short peptide bioregulator," it is said to penetrate cells and cell nuclei, bind to DNA or histones and thereby influence the activity of genes that protect nerve cells from oxidative stress and cell death.
- **EU approval:** no.
- **US approval:** no.
- **Russia:** marketed in capsule form as a component of so-called "cytogens," that is, more as a dietary supplement than as a tested medicine.
- **WADA:** not listed by name; an active substance not approved as a medicine can fall under the catch-all category S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **C** – a few small, nonrandomized Russian observational studies with surrogate measures such as "biological age."

### How it is supposed to work

Pinealon belongs to a family of very short peptides (two to four amino acids) that the St. Petersburg group calls "peptide bioregulators." The central hypothesis is that such mini-peptides reach the cell nucleus, bind there to specific DNA segments and thereby regulate genes – similar to transcription factors, that is, proteins that switch genes on and off.

Evidence for this comes from the laboratory:

- In cancer cells (HeLa), fluorescently labeled Pinealon, Epitalon and related peptides penetrated the cytoplasm, nucleus and nucleoli; in the test tube, they altered the fluorescence of labeled DNA fragments depending on their sequence (Fedoreyeva 2011, PMID 22117547).
- In nerve cells from the cerebellum of rats and in other cell cultures, Pinealon limited the accumulation of reactive oxygen species and reduced cell death (Khavinson 2011, PMID 21978084).

A review by the developers summarizes that the EDR peptide influenced genes and proteins in cell and animal experiments that play a role in Alzheimer's disease, such as enzymes of antioxidant defense and cell death signals (Khavinson 2020, PMID 33396470). This is a hypothesis that has so far hardly been tested by other research groups. It is also known from classical pharmacology that such short peptides are rapidly broken down in the digestive tract and in the blood; how much unchanged Pinealon reaches the nerve cells after taking a capsule has not been studied.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded **one** publication classified as a human study; in total, PubMed lists only 15 papers with "Pinealon" in the title or abstract. No study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. A follow-up search found three further small Russian papers involving humans, all in the journal "Uspekhi Gerontologii" (Advances in Gerontology).

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bashkireva 2012, PMID 23734521 | 150 truck drivers, 150 metalworkers as comparison; peptides in subgroups | not stated | according to the authors, better psycho-emotional scores, best effect with a combination of two peptides |
| Nazimko 2012, PMID 22708445 | locomotive driver teams, Pinealon capsules | 2 weeks | according to the authors, better "biological age" and better adaptive responses |
| Meshchaninov 2015, PMID 26390612 | 32 people (41–83 years) with multimorbidity and organic psychosyndrome | not stated | "anabolic" effect; indications of an oxidizing effect and inhibition of blood formation |
| Myakotnykh 2016, PMID 28539017 | 110 people, comparison of various "anti-aging" methods | not stated | strongest effect on "biological age" with a combination of two peptides |

All four papers share weaknesses:

- **No placebo, no blinding, no randomization** discernible. With questionnaires on mood and stress, the expectation effect is considerable.
- **Surrogate measures instead of clinical endpoints:** In Russian gerontology, "biological age" is calculated from a combination of measurements such as blood pressure, lung function, reaction time and self-report. It is not an internationally standardized measure, and an improvement does not automatically mean better health or a longer life.
- **Combinations:** In several studies, Pinealon was given together with the peptide Vesugen (Lys-Glu-Asp), so the effect cannot be attributed to one substance.
- **Unclear figures:** The abstracts give hardly any effect sizes.

One finding that does not appear in the advertising is noteworthy: in the study with 32 older people, the authors observed a **pro-oxidative** activity with Pinealon and Vesugen – that is, the opposite of the claimed antioxidant effect – and a decrease in blood-forming stem cells (CD34+) in the blood, which they describe as a marked inhibition of blood formation. Nevertheless, they recommend the peptides for geriatric practice (Meshchaninov 2015, PMID 26390612).

### Animal and laboratory studies

- In rats whose mothers had elevated homocysteine levels during pregnancy due to methionine-rich feed, Pinealon (given to the mothers) improved the orientation and learning ability of the offspring; their cerebellar nerve cells were more resistant to oxidative stress (Arutjunyan 2012, PMID 22567179).
- In 18-month-old rats, Pinealon and Cortexin (a Russian brain extract preparation) influenced behavior and brain messengers under oxygen deprivation and hypothermia; Cortexin had a stronger effect on radical processes and cell death enzymes (Mendzheritsky 2015, PMID 28509493).
- In cell cultures, Pinealon protected various cell types from oxidative stress (Khavinson 2011, PMID 21978084).

Almost all papers come from the developer group or cooperating Russian institutes.

### Risks and side effects

**Established:** There are no systematic safety data. The only study that reports laboratory values in more detail describes a pro-oxidative activity and a decrease in blood-forming stem cells in the blood (Meshchaninov 2015, PMID 26390612). What this means is unclear – but it is a warning signal that has not been investigated further.

**Theoretical:** If the developers' hypothesis is correct and Pinealon actually binds to DNA and regulates genes, unwanted genetic effects would also be conceivable. Studies on genetic damage, cancer risk or long-term effects are not available.

**Gray market:** Pinealon is offered online as capsules, nasal spray or powder for injection, usually with promises about memory, sleep and "brain rejuvenation." Content and purity are unverified.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved as a medicine. Sale as a dietary supplement would only be permissible if the substance is permitted under food law; as a novel, synthetic substance with an intended pharmacological effect, this is not the case in our assessment (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **USA:** not approved.
- **Russia:** marketed as a component of peptide preparations that have not gone through the approval process for medicines.
- **Sport:** not listed by name; possibly covered by WADA category S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established:** No registered study (search October 2026).

**Assessment:** As long as research remains limited to a small circle of Russian institutes and no randomized, placebo-controlled trials with clinical endpoints are conducted, Pinealon will remain a laboratory concept with a marketing superstructure.

### Conclusion

Pinealon is a synthetic tripeptide from St. Petersburg that protected nerve cells from oxidative stress in cell cultures and in rats; the developers suspect a direct effect on genes. The few human studies are small, uncontrolled and mainly measure a nonstandardized "biological age." One of them even found indications of a pro-oxidative effect and an inhibition of blood formation. The data are not sufficient to assess a benefit or safety in humans.
