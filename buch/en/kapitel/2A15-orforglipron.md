## Orforglipron (for comparison)

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** **not a peptide**, but a small, chemically manufactured molecule ("small molecule") that acts like a peptide at the GLP-1 receptor. Development code LY3502970. Taken once daily as a tablet or capsule.
- **Origin:** originally discovered by Chugai Pharmaceutical, licensed and developed by Eli Lilly.
- **Mechanism of action:** agonist at the GLP-1 receptor – the same target as semaglutide or liraglutide, but with a completely different chemical structure.
- **Approval EU / USA:** according to the research for this book (October 2026), not recorded as approved; applications for approval have been submitted or announced on the basis of the Phase 3 trials – please check for updates.
- **WADA:** not listed by name; as long as it is not approved anywhere, prohibited under S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **A** based on the data (several large Phase 3 trials including a cardiovascular safety trial); formal approval is still pending or should be checked.

### Why this chapter is in a peptide book

All GLP-1 drugs up to 2025 were peptides. Peptides are largely digested in the gastrointestinal tract and hardly absorbed – which is why they have to be injected. The only exception, semaglutide as a tablet, requires an excipient that facilitates absorption through the gastric mucosa, and strict rules for taking it: on an empty stomach, with little water and at an interval before breakfast (see chapter Semaglutide and Part I on the question of how peptides get into the body).

Orforglipron fundamentally circumvents this problem: as a small molecule, it is absorbed from the gut like a conventional tablet. According to the trials, it can be taken without regard to meals or amount of water (Rosenstock 2026, PMID 41765029). Orforglipron is thus the most important comparison case for the question of whether the future of weight-loss drugs lies with peptides at all (see Part V).

### How it is supposed to work

Orforglipron binds to the GLP-1 receptor and triggers effects similar to those of the peptide agonists: less appetite, delayed gastric emptying, more insulin in a glucose-dependent manner. It activates only the GLP-1 receptor, not GIP or glucagon receptors. Studies in people with type 2 diabetes showed improvements in markers of beta-cell function and insulin sensitivity (Rosenstock 2025, PMID 40808573).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 20 human studies. On ClinicalTrials.gov, 53 trials were registered. The approval programs are called **ATTAIN** (obesity) and **ACHIEVE** (type 2 diabetes).

#### Obesity: ATTAIN

**ATTAIN-1** (Wharton 2025, PMID 40960239) is the main trial. After 72 weeks, the 3,127 participants on the highest dose lost an average of 11.2% of their weight, and 2.1% on placebo. At the highest dose, 54.6% achieved at least 10% and 18.4% at least 20% weight loss. 5.3 to 10.3% discontinued due to side effects (placebo 2.7%).

For context: this is considerably less than with injected semaglutide or tirzepatide in their approval trials and less than the Phase 2 trial, with up to 14.7% after 36 weeks, had led people to expect (Wharton 2023, PMID 37351564). Cross-trial comparisons are, however, only a rough guide.

**ATTAIN-2** (Horn 2026, PMID 41275875) showed a weight loss of up to 9.6% compared with 2.5% on placebo in people with type 2 diabetes.

**ATTAIN-MAINTAIN** (Aronne 2026, PMID 42120723) is particularly interesting for practice. Participants who had previously lost weight with injected tirzepatide or semaglutide in the SURMOUNT-5 trial switched to orforglipron or placebo. After 52 weeks, those previously treated with tirzepatide maintained 74.7% of their weight loss with orforglipron (placebo 49.2%), and those previously treated with semaglutide 79.3% (placebo 37.6%). There was no comparison group that simply continued receiving the injection – the authors themselves cite this as a limitation. The concept "lose weight with the injection, maintain with the tablet" is thus supported for the first time, but not yet tested against the alternative "continue the injection."

#### Type 2 diabetes: ACHIEVE

- **ACHIEVE-1** (Rosenstock 2025, PMID 40544435): In early type 2 diabetes without other medications, HbA1c fell by up to 1.48 percentage points after 40 weeks (placebo −0.41), and weight by up to 7.6%. No severe hypoglycemia occurred.
- **ACHIEVE-2** (Welch 2026, PMID 42259339): Against the SGLT2 inhibitor dapagliflozin, orforglipron was non-inferior for HbA1c and stronger at all doses (up to −1.56 vs. −0.81 percentage points).
- **ACHIEVE-3** (Rosenstock 2026, PMID 41765029): In direct comparison with the semaglutide tablet, orforglipron lowered HbA1c more (−1.91 vs. −1.47 percentage points at the respective higher doses). The trial was open-label, i.e., not blinded. Gastrointestinal complaints were more frequent on orforglipron (58–59% vs. 37–45%), as were discontinuations due to side effects (9–10% vs. 4–5%).
- **ACHIEVE-4** (Klein 2026, PMID 42815506): the cardiovascular safety trial (see below).

### Animal and laboratory studies

For this comparison chapter, the preclinical data are not presented in detail. One pharmacological difference is important: as a small molecule, orforglipron is broken down by liver enzymes. Numerous studies on interactions with other drugs were therefore conducted, for example with clarithromycin, ciclosporin, carbamazepine or quinidine (NCT05469126, NCT05573230, NCT06370728, NCT06704763). Peptide agonists, by contrast, are broken down like proteins and have hardly any such interactions.

### Risks and side effects

**Documented in studies:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints** as with all GLP-1 agonists, mostly mild to moderate and during dose escalation. In ACHIEVE-4, 62.1% on orforglipron compared with 14.2% on insulin glargine reported gastrointestinal complaints (Klein 2026, PMID 42815506).
- **Discontinuations due to side effects:** up to 10.3% in ATTAIN-1 (Wharton 2025, PMID 40960239), about twice as frequent as with the semaglutide tablet (Rosenstock 2026, PMID 41765029).
- **Pulse:** In ACHIEVE-3, heart rate rose by 3.7 to 4.7 beats per minute on orforglipron, and by 1.0 to 1.5 on the semaglutide tablet (Rosenstock 2026, PMID 41765029).
- **Cardiovascular safety:** In ACHIEVE-4, major cardiovascular events occurred in 4.2% on orforglipron and 5.0% on insulin glargine (hazard ratio 0.84; 95% CI 0.59–1.20) – orforglipron was therefore non-inferior. Clinically significant or severe hypoglycemia was less frequent (6.8% vs. 19.2%) (Klein 2026, PMID 42815506). Whether orforglipron, like some peptide agonists, *reduces* cardiovascular events is to be shown by a dedicated outcome trial (NCT07241390).
- **Liver:** With small GLP-1 molecules from other manufacturers, elevated liver values were a problem. A pooled analysis of seven Phase 3 trials with 11,220 participants found no case of drug-induced liver injury for orforglipron; liver values even decreased on average (Wharton 2026, PMID 42338042).

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** recorded as not approved as of October 2026; an approval procedure is to be expected or may already be under way – please check for updates.
- **USA:** applications for approval for obesity and type 2 diabetes have been submitted or announced on the basis of the ATTAIN and ACHIEVE data; please check for updates on the status of the decision.
- **Sport:** as long as it is not approved anywhere, WADA class S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- Non-approved "orforglipron" offerings online are not tested medicines.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed (registered trials, as of October 2026):**

- **Cardiovascular outcome trial** in atherosclerosis (NCT07241390, 7,140 participants, until 2031).
- **Peripheral artery disease** (NCT07223593), **hypertension** (NCT06948422, NCT06948435, NCT06952530), **knee osteoarthritis** (NCT07153471), **stress urinary incontinence** in women with obesity (NCT07202884).
- **Adolescents** (NCT06672939) and **children with type 2 diabetes** compared with dulaglutide (NCT07668336).
- Trial on **sleep apnea** (NCT06649045) completed, publication pending.

**Assessment:** Orforglipron is expected to become the first widely available non-peptide GLP-1 drug. Its strength is not maximum weight loss – here the injected peptides are ahead – but the simple administration, presumably cheaper manufacturing and suitability for long-term treatment after a phase with injections. For the peptide market, this means serious competition, especially in long-term and maintenance therapy (see Part V).

### Conclusion

Orforglipron is not a peptide but a small molecule that activates the GLP-1 receptor as a tablet and can be swallowed without rules for taking it. In ATTAIN-1, weight fell by up to 11.2% after 72 weeks – less than with injected semaglutide or tirzepatide – and after switching from an injection, participants maintained around three quarters of their weight loss. In type 2 diabetes, it lowered HbA1c more than the semaglutide tablet and proved cardiovascularly safe compared with insulin. Gastrointestinal complaints and treatment discontinuations are somewhat more frequent than with the semaglutide tablet.
