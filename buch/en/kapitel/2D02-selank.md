## Selank

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic heptapeptide (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro). The first four amino acids correspond to tuftsin, an endogenous peptide that is cleaved from an antibody fragment and stimulates immune cells; attached to it – as with Semax – is the stabilizing sequence Pro-Gly-Pro.
- **Origin:** developed in Moscow at the Institute of Molecular Genetics and the Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology.
- **Mechanism of action:** said to relieve anxiety without causing drowsiness or dependence as benzodiazepines do. Proposed mechanisms include inhibition of the breakdown of endogenous enkephalins (messengers that dampen pain and stress), effects on the GABA system and on immune messengers. It is administered as nasal drops.
- **EU approval:** no.
- **US approval:** no. In 2023, Selank was on the FDA "Category 2" list for pharmacy compounding; its current status is inconsistently documented (as of October 2026, please check for updates).
- **Russia:** registered as a medicine (anxiolytic).
- **WADA:** not listed by name (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **C** – three small comparative studies from Russia, without placebo control and without registration.

### How it is supposed to work

Tuftsin is a short peptide that activates phagocytic cells of the immune system. Animal experiments showed that modifications of it also influence behavior. Selank is one such modification, intended to remain in the body longer thanks to the attached Pro-Gly-Pro.

There are several hypotheses for the mechanism, based on laboratory and animal data:

- **Enkephalins:** In blood serum, Selank inhibited enzymes that break down enkephalins in the test tube. The researchers also found a shortened half-life of enkephalins in the blood of people with generalized anxiety disorder and concluded that Selank could act via this pathway (Zozulya 2001, PMID 11550013).
- **GABA system:** In rats, one hour after administration Selank altered the activity of 45 of 84 examined genes involved in nerve transmission in the cerebral cortex, similarly to the messenger GABA itself. The authors suspect an influence on the GABA system, which is also the target of benzodiazepines (Volkova 2016, PMID 26924987).
- **Immune system:** In rats and mice, Selank influenced inflammatory genes; in cell cultures and in mice, it showed effects against influenza viruses (Ershov 2009, PMID 19882898).

How much Selank actually reaches the brain in humans after intranasal administration has not been published.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 3 publications classified as human studies; in total, PubMed lists around 70 papers with "Selank" in the title or abstract, almost all animal experiments. The search on ClinicalTrials.gov yielded 2 hits – on closer inspection, both concern other procedures (brain stimulation) and are not Selank studies. There is therefore no registered clinical trial with Selank.

#### Anxiety disorders

All three clinical studies compared Selank with benzodiazepines – medazepam or phenazepam, which is widely used in Russia. In the first study, with 62 people, the anxiolytic effect of Selank and medazepam on questionnaire scales (Hamilton, Zung, CGI) was similar; Selank is said to have additionally acted against fatigue. The researchers also measured the half-life of the messenger Leu-enkephalin in the blood, which increased on Selank (Zozulia 2008, PMID 18454096). A second study, with 60 people, describes a pronounced anxiolytic and a mild memory-enhancing effect that persisted for a week beyond the end of treatment (Medvedev 2014, PMID 25176261).

The third study tested Selank as an add-on: 40 people received phenazepam plus Selank, 30 phenazepam alone. According to the authors, the effect began earlier in the combination group, and typical benzodiazepine side effects such as attention and memory problems, fatigue and sexual dysfunction were less frequent – even after the tranquilizer was stopped (Medvedev 2015, PMID 26356395).

These results sound encouraging but have clear limitations:

- **No placebo:** In anxiety disorders, symptoms often improve considerably even on dummy medications. Without a placebo group, it is impossible to say what share is attributable to Selank.
- **"Similarly effective" is not proof:** Demonstrating equivalence requires predefined margins and a sufficient number of participants. Neither is discernible in the abstracts.
- **Short observation, small groups, no registration:** All studies come from Russian centers, were not preregistered in a public registry and were published in Russian-language journals.

#### Immune system and healthy people

One paper describes changes in immune messengers (Th1/Th2 balance) in the blood of people with anxiety and fatigue states after 14 days of Selank; in the test tube, Selank suppressed the production of the inflammatory messenger IL-6 in blood cells from people with depression (Uchakina 2008, PMID 18577961). Whether this is clinically meaningful has not been studied. In healthy people, a small imaging study after a single dose showed altered connections between the amygdala, a brain region important for anxiety, and parts of the temporal lobe (Panikratova 2020, PMID 32342318). Studies on concentration or performance in healthy people – the main promise on the internet – do not exist.

### Animal and laboratory studies

- In rats with 30 weeks of alcohol consumption, Selank prevented memory and attention problems during withdrawal and influenced BDNF content in the hippocampus and frontal cortex (Kolik 2019, PMID 31625062).
- In rats, Selank altered the activity of numerous genes of the GABA system (Volkova 2016, PMID 26924987).
- In cell cultures and in mice, Selank inhibited influenza viruses, most strongly when given before infection (Ershov 2009, PMID 19882898).
- Other papers from the same research groups describe effects in rodents in stress, morphine and alcohol withdrawal, and in Parkinson's models.

These findings are animal and laboratory findings and come largely from a small circle of research groups.

### Risks and side effects

**Established:** In the small Russian studies, Selank is described as well tolerated; the abstracts do not give systematically recorded rates of side effects. Long-term data and data on dependence or withdrawal symptoms are lacking.

**Theoretical:** Selank is said to act on the immune, enkephalin and GABA systems. Interactions with tranquilizers, alcohol or antidepressants have not been studied; the Russian combination study does at least show that Selank is used together with benzodiazepines. No data are available for pregnancy, breastfeeding and children.

**Gray market:** Outside Russia, Selank is sold as a "research peptide" as powder for injection or as nasal spray. A Belgian control laboratory found Selank in seized unknown preparations (Vanhee 2020, PMID 31667971). Injection is not a route of administration tested in studies; the purity and content of such products are unverified.

**Important:** Anxiety disorders are very treatable – with psychotherapy and with approved medications whose effect has been demonstrated in large placebo-controlled trials. A gray-market peptide is no substitute for these.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved; as a substance with a pharmacological effect, Selank is considered a medicine, and placing it on the market without approval is not permitted (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **USA:** not approved. In 2023, Selank was placed in FDA "Category 2" for compounding substances with safety concerns; according to reports, the nomination was later withdrawn. Selank was not among the peptides evaluated by the advisory committee PCAC in July 2026. To current knowledge, there is no permission for pharmacy compounding (as of October 2026, please check for updates).
- **Russia:** registered as a medicine.
- **Sport:** not listed by name on the WADA list (as of 2026, please check for updates).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established:** No study with Selank is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (search October 2026).

**Assessment:** Selank is an example of a peptide that entered clinical use in Russia without meeting the study requirements that apply in the EU or the USA. Approval outside Russia would require at least one larger placebo-controlled trial in anxiety disorders; there are no indications of such a trial.

### Conclusion

Selank is a tuftsin-derived peptide registered in Russia as an anxiolytic. Three small Russian studies describe an anxiolytic effect similar to that of benzodiazepines, but none of them had a placebo group or was preregistered. Mechanistic hypotheses about enkephalins, GABA and the immune system are based on laboratory and animal data. The data are not sufficient to assess efficacy and safety by today's standards.
