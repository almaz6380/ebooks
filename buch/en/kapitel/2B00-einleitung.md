# Part II · B – Healing, Regeneration and the Immune System

The peptides in this group promise what many people wish for: faster healing of tendons and muscles, a tighter gut wall, a stronger immune system, less inflammation. In online shops, fitness forums and longevity clinics, BPC-157, TB-500 and thymosin alpha-1 are among the best-selling "research peptides" of all.

### What these peptides have in common

None of the eight substances is meant to change a single, clearly measurable target – such as blood glucose or body weight – but rather to "trigger" or "normalize" the body's own repair and defense processes. This has two consequences that run through all the chapters:

1. **The effect is hard to measure.** Healing, pain and immune function fluctuate considerably, depend on many factors and are susceptible to placebo effects. Small studies without a control group are therefore particularly uninformative.
2. **Plausibility often replaces evidence.** Almost all peptides in this group are derived from substances the body produces itself: from gastric juice (BPC-157), the thymus (thymosin alpha-1, thymalin), skin defenses (LL-37), the hormone alpha-MSH (KPV) or erythropoietin (ARA-290). "Endogenous" sounds harmless, but says nothing about the benefit and safety of administering it from outside at a high dose.

A recurring pattern is also striking: small early studies look promising, while larger and more carefully blinded studies do not confirm them. This can be seen with thymosin alpha-1 in sepsis, with thymosin beta-4 as eye drops, with LL-37 for leg ulcers and with larazotide in celiac disease.

### The group at a glance

| Peptide | Evidence | Approval | In brief |
|---|---|---|---|
| BPC-157 | C | no | Hundreds of animal studies, almost all from one group; three mini pilot studies in humans |
| TB-500 / thymosin beta-4 | B (Tβ4) · D (TB-500) | no | Tβ4 tested in RCTs, large eye studies without clear benefit; TB-500 never in humans |
| Thymosin alpha-1 | B | China and others, not EU/USA | many RCTs; large sepsis trial with no difference from placebo |
| KPV | D | no | effective in mice with colitis; no human study |
| LL-37 | B | no | endogenous defense peptide; wound study with 148 people without benefit |
| ARA-290 | B | no | small RCTs in nerve damage due to sarcoidosis; no new studies since 2015 |
| Larazotide | B | no | celiac disease candidate; Phase 3 stopped |
| Thymalin / Thymogen | C | Russia | almost only Russian studies from one school, hardly randomized |

Evidence levels: **A** several large RCTs or approval · **B** individual RCTs · **C** only small or uncontrolled human studies · **D** only animal and laboratory studies. Approval refers to approval as a medicine, as of 2026.

Not a single peptide in this group is approved as a medicine in the EU or the USA. The two substances with approvals outside the West – thymosin alpha-1 and thymalin – were approved on the basis of studies that only partly meet today's standards.

### Reading guide

The chapters follow the uniform structure of this book. In this group, pay particular attention to three distinctions:

- **Human or animal?** For BPC-157, KPV and TB-500, almost everything claimed online is based on experiments in rats, mice or cell cultures. The chapters explicitly label this in each case.
- **Active substance or measured value?** For LL-37, there are many studies that *measure* the substance in the blood, but only a few that *administer* it as a medicine. Only the latter say anything about a treatment.
- **Original or derivative?** TB-500 is not thymosin beta-4, Thymogen is not thymalin, and the product sold online is not the pharmaceutical form used in studies.

Two chapters contain notes on registry entries whose authenticity is questionable: one sponsor has entered both a Phase 2 trial on BPC-157 and a trial on TB-500 on ClinicalTrials.gov, one of which describes itself as a "fictitious example." So even a registry entry is no proof that a trial is actually taking place.

Questions about gray-market products, contaminants and the theoretical cancer risk from growth and vascular signals are dealt with in more detail and across peptides in Chapter I.5 and in Part IV. For athletes: BPC-157, TB-500 and ARA-290 are explicitly prohibited by WADA; for the others, you should ask your national anti-doping agency before any use.
