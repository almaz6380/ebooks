## Dulaglutide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** Fusion protein. Two slightly modified GLP-1 chains are coupled via a short linker to the constant region (Fc portion) of a human IgG4 antibody. Dulaglutide is thus many times larger than liraglutide – strictly speaking more a protein than a small peptide.
- **Origin:** developed by Eli Lilly, produced by genetic engineering in mammalian cells (Glaesner 2010, PMID 20503261).
- **Mechanism of action:** GLP-1 receptor agonist. The antibody portion protects against breakdown and excretion via the kidneys; one injection per week is sufficient.
- **Product:** Trulicity (prefilled pen, weekly; approved strengths up to 4.5 mg).
- **EU approval:** yes, type 2 diabetes in adults and adolescents aged 10 and older. No approval for weight reduction.
- **US approval:** yes, type 2 diabetes from age 10 and for reducing cardiovascular risk in adults with type 2 diabetes.
- **WADA:** not listed (status as of 2026; please check for current information).
- **Evidence level:** **A** – AWARD study program with more than ten randomized phase 3 trials, outcomes trial REWIND, study in adolescents.

### How It Is Supposed to Work

Dulaglutide acts on the GLP-1 receptor like exenatide, liraglutide, and semaglutide: it enhances glucose-dependent insulin release, suppresses the hormone glucagon, slows gastric emptying, and dampens appetite. The basic mechanism is explained in the chapter Exenatide.

The difference lies in the packaging. Liraglutide and semaglutide are bound to blood proteins via an attached fatty acid. Lilly took a different path and coupled the hormone to the Fc portion of an antibody. This building block is known from biotechnology: it makes molecules so large that the kidneys no longer filter them out, and it uses an endogenous recycling pathway that protects antibodies from breakdown. In rats and cynomolgus monkeys, the half-life of the drug was 1.5 to 2 days; in humans it is longer and allows weekly dosing (Glaesner 2010, PMID 20503261).

In the GLP-1 chains themselves, individual amino acids were exchanged – partly to prevent breakdown by the enzyme DPP-4, partly to reduce the formation of antibodies against the drug. Indeed, in nine studies only 1.6% of those treated with dulaglutide formed antibodies against the medication (Milicevic 2016, PMID 26847401) – considerably fewer than with exenatide.

A disadvantage of its size: the molecule presumably has more difficulty reaching the brain regions that control appetite. This could explain why dulaglutide produces less weight loss than semaglutide despite comparable blood sugar reduction. This explanation is plausible but not proven.

### Human Studies

The PubMed query evaluated for this book (search term "dulaglutide" in title or abstract, query date October 2026) yielded 158 human studies; ClinicalTrials.gov lists 143 registry entries. The number is smaller than for liraglutide or semaglutide, but the studies are particularly systematic: the AWARD program (Assessment of Weekly AdministRation of LY2189265 in Diabetes) tested dulaglutide against almost every common diabetes therapy. It is also striking how often dulaglutide itself serves as the comparator for newer drugs – from semaglutide and tirzepatide to new Chinese developments.

#### The AWARD Program

In most AWARD trials, the doses of 0.75 and 1.5 mg per week were tested. The table shows the result of the higher dose in each case.

Some results deserve explanation:

**Against insulin** (AWARD-2 and AWARD-4), dulaglutide lowered long-term blood sugar somewhat more, with less hypoglycemia and without weight gain (Giorgino 2015, PMID 26089386; Blonde 2015, PMID 26009229). In AWARD-2, however, the insulin dose was not consistently titrated upward; the mean final dose was 29 units. More aggressively titrated insulin might have performed better. Nausea occurred in 15.4% on dulaglutide 1.5 mg and in 1.5% on insulin.

**In kidney disease** (AWARD-7), the result is particularly interesting: blood sugar fell as much as on insulin, but estimated kidney function (eGFR) was higher on dulaglutide after 52 weeks – 34.0 versus 31.3 ml/min per 1.73 m² (Tuttle 2018, PMID 29910024). Protein excretion in the urine did not differ significantly. This is an indication, but not proof, of kidney protection.

**With a sulfonylurea** (AWARD-8), the downside becomes apparent: dulaglutide lowered HbA1c by 1.3 percentage points more than placebo, and 55.3% versus 18.9% achieved a value below 7%. However, hypoglycemia was considerably more frequent (2.37 versus 0.07 events per person-year) because the sulfonylurea releases insulin independently of blood sugar (Dungan 2016, PMID 26799540). Severe hypoglycemia did not occur.

**Higher doses** (AWARD-11): to keep up with semaglutide, Lilly tested 3.0 and 4.5 mg. The highest dose lowered HbA1c by a further 0.24 percentage points and weight by a further 1.6 kg compared with 1.5 mg (Frias 2021, PMID 33397768). Nausea increased slightly (16.4% vs. 13.4%), as did vomiting (9.3% vs. 5.6%). The gain is measurable but limited.

#### Head-to-Head Comparisons Within the GLP-1 Class

| Study | Design | Duration | Result (dulaglutide vs. comparator) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AWARD-1 (Wysham 2014), PMID 24879836 | vs. exenatide twice daily | 26 weeks | HbA1c −1.51 vs. −0.99; less hypoglycemia |
| AWARD-6 (Dungan 2014), PMID 25018121 | 599 people, vs. liraglutide 1.8 mg daily | 26 weeks | HbA1c −1.42 vs. −1.36; nausea 20% vs. 18% |
| SUSTAIN 7 (Pratley 2018), PMID 29397376 | 1,201 people, dulaglutide 0.75/1.5 mg vs. semaglutide 0.5/1.0 mg | 40 weeks | HbA1c −1.4 vs. −1.8 (high doses); weight −3.0 vs. −6.5 kg |

The ranking is clear. Dulaglutide was clearly superior to exenatide twice daily (Wysham 2014, PMID 24879836) and on a par with liraglutide (Dungan 2014, PMID 25018121). In AWARD-6, it thus became the first weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist to keep pace in a direct comparison with the then standard, liraglutide – with considerably fewer injections.

Against semaglutide, dulaglutide lost at both dose levels in SUSTAIN 7 (Pratley 2018, PMID 29397376). At the high doses, HbA1c fell by 1.4 versus 1.8 percentage points, weight by 3.0 versus 6.5 kg. Gastrointestinal complaints were similarly frequent (48% on dulaglutide 1.5 mg, 44% on semaglutide 1.0 mg). It was later pointed out critically that dulaglutide was dosed only up to 1.5 mg in SUSTAIN 7; the higher doses from AWARD-11 were not yet available at the time. Even with 4.5 mg, however, the weight difference would likely persist.

#### REWIND: The Cardiovascular Trial

REWIND differed from other GLP-1 outcomes trials: it ran longer, included older people with comparatively well-controlled blood sugar (median HbA1c 7.2%), and also enrolled many people without previous cardiovascular disease who merely had risk factors. The study population thus resembles everyday primary care more closely than in other outcomes trials.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| REWIND (Gerstein 2019), PMID 31189511 | 9,901 people with type 2 diabetes, mean age 66.2 years, 46.3% women; RCT vs. placebo | median 5.4 years | Major cardiovascular events 12.0% vs. 13.4%; HR 0.88 (0.79–0.99) |
| REWIND, kidneys (Gerstein 2019), PMID 31189509 | the same study, exploratory analysis | median 5.4 years | Kidney endpoint 17.1% vs. 19.6%; HR 0.85, mainly less new macroalbuminuria |
| REWIND, cognition (Cukierman-Yaffe 2020), PMID 32562683 | 8,828 people with memory tests | median 5.4 years | HR 0.93 (not significant); after post hoc adjustment HR 0.86 |

The primary endpoint – death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or stroke – occurred in 594 of 4,949 people (12.0%) on dulaglutide and in 663 of 4,952 (13.4%) on placebo (Gerstein 2019, PMID 31189511). This corresponds to a relative reduction of 12%. All-cause mortality did not differ significantly (10.8% vs. 12.0%; HR 0.90). Gastrointestinal side effects were reported by 47.4% versus 34.1%.

In the kidney analysis (Gerstein 2019, PMID 31189509), the effect was mainly attributable to newly occurring macroalbuminuria (HR 0.77). For a sustained decline in kidney function of at least 30%, the estimate was 0.89, and for dialysis or kidney transplantation 0.75 – neither statistically significant.

The analysis of cognitive performance (Cukierman-Yaffe 2020, PMID 32562683) is occasionally cited as evidence of protection against dementia. That goes too far: the prespecified comparison was not significant. Only after a post hoc statistical adjustment was there a 14% lower relative risk of a marked decline in cognitive performance (hazard ratio 0.86). The authors themselves describe it as an exploratory analysis that justifies further studies.

In a meta-analysis of all large GLP-1 outcomes trials, REWIND fitted well into the overall picture, according to which the class reduces major cardiovascular events by about 12% (Kristensen 2019, PMID 31422062).

#### What Explains the Benefit – and What Does Not?

Several post hoc analyses of REWIND have tried to understand the cardiovascular effect in more detail. In a subanalysis of 9,543 people without atrial fibrillation at baseline, 31% already had cardiovascular disease, while the rest had "only" risk factors (Raubenheimer 2022, PMID 34984808). This underlines that REWIND studied people in a prevention setting far more than other outcomes trials.

| Analysis | Question | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Konig 2021, PMID 34563178 | Which changes explain the benefit? | Partly the reduction in HbA1c and urinary protein excretion, not weight, blood pressure, or LDL cholesterol |
| Branch 2022, PMID 36073143 | Heart failure events | 4.3% vs. 4.6%; HR 0.93, no significant difference |
| Raubenheimer 2022, PMID 34984808 | New atrial fibrillation or flutter | 5.6% vs. 5.3%; no difference |

According to the analysis by Konig 2021 (PMID 34563178), part of the effect could be explained statistically by better blood sugar control and lower urinary protein excretion; another part remained unexplained. Weight loss played no measurable role in this analysis. This is consistent with the widespread assumption that GLP-1 receptor agonists also have direct effects on blood vessels and inflammatory processes – but this has not been demonstrated in humans.

Dulaglutide showed no protection against heart failure events (Branch 2022, PMID 36073143) or against new-onset atrial fibrillation (Raubenheimer 2022, PMID 34984808). Among the 853 participants who already had heart failure at baseline, the picture was no different either. For heart failure with preserved ejection fraction in obesity, data are now available for semaglutide and tirzepatide; for dulaglutide, they are lacking.

#### Dulaglutide as the Comparator Standard for New Drugs

Because dulaglutide is widely used around the world, well tolerated, and has demonstrated cardiovascular benefit, manufacturers frequently choose it as an active comparator. The results of these trials belong in substance to the chapters on the respective new drugs; here is just the overview:

| Study | Design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| SURPASS-CVOT (Nicholls 2025), PMID 41406444 | 13,165 people with type 2 diabetes and vascular disease; tirzepatide vs. dulaglutide | several years | Major cardiovascular events 12.2% vs. 13.1%; HR 0.92, noninferior, superiority not shown |
| SURPASS J-mono (Inagaki 2022), PMID 35914543 | 636 people in Japan; tirzepatide vs. dulaglutide 0.75 mg | 52 weeks | HbA1c −2.4 to −2.8 vs. −1.3 |
| Guo 2026, PMID 41407860 | 731 people in China; mazdutide vs. dulaglutide 1.5 mg | 28 weeks | HbA1c difference −0.24 and −0.30 percentage points, respectively, in favor of mazdutide |
| EECOH-2 (He 2025), PMID 40854315 | 621 people in China; ecnoglutide vs. dulaglutide 1.5 mg | 52 weeks (primary endpoint 32) | HbA1c −1.89 to −1.91 vs. −1.65 |

SURPASS-CVOT (Nicholls 2025, PMID 41406444) is particularly revealing: it was the first large outcomes trial in which two incretin medications competed directly against each other. Tirzepatide was not worse, but also not demonstrably better, than dulaglutide. Since dulaglutide itself had reduced risk in REWIND, a "draw" against dulaglutide is a strong result for both drugs. A post hoc analysis with a broader cardiorenal endpoint found an advantage for tirzepatide (23.7% vs. 27.4%; HR 0.84) (Nissen 2026, PMID 41903177). Post hoc analyses, however, are less robust than the prespecified primary endpoint.

#### Handling: The Prefilled Pen

A practical point rarely appreciated in studies is administration. Dulaglutide is supplied in a single-use prefilled pen that does not require attaching a needle or setting a dose. In a study with 211 people with type 2 diabetes who had never injected themselves, the injection was ultimately successful in 99.1% of cases; fear of injecting decreased (Matfin 2015, PMID 25901022). In a manufacturer-funded comparative study with 310 participants, 84.2% preferred the dulaglutide pen over the semaglutide pen available at the time (Matza 2020, PMID 31646727). Such preference studies say nothing about efficacy, but they may explain why dulaglutide is widely used, particularly among older people.

#### Children and Adolescents

In AWARD-PEDS with 154 adolescents with type 2 diabetes, HbA1c rose by 0.6 percentage points on placebo after 26 weeks and fell by 0.6 (0.75 mg) and 0.9 percentage points (1.5 mg), respectively, on dulaglutide (Arslanian 2022, PMID 35658022). An HbA1c below 7% was achieved by 51% versus 14%. Dulaglutide had no effect on body mass index in this age group. This study was the basis for approval from age 10.

#### Other Areas of Use in Studies

- **Smoking cessation:** In a study with 255 people, dulaglutide did not improve the abstinence rate; after 52 weeks, 32% were smoke-free in both groups (Lüthi 2024, PMID 38371479). The usual weight gain after quitting smoking was prevented in the first 12 weeks, but after one year the difference had disappeared.
- **Alcohol:** In a prespecified secondary analysis of the same study, participants on dulaglutide drank 29% less alcohol than those on placebo after 12 weeks (Probst 2023, PMID 37991022). The study was not designed for this question.
- **Primary polydipsia** (pathologically increased drinking): in a crossover study with 34 people, dulaglutide reduced fluid intake during an eight-hour test by 490 ml, corresponding to 17% (Winzeler 2021, PMID 34473645). An indication that GLP-1 also influences the sensation of thirst.

### Animal and Laboratory Studies

The preclinical characterization of dulaglutide (then LY2189265) was carried out in cells, rats, mice, and cynomolgus monkeys (Glaesner 2010, PMID 20503261). In laboratory experiments, the fusion protein retained full activity at the GLP-1 receptor. In diabetic mice, a single dose improved glucose tolerance; twice-weekly doses over four weeks lowered weight and delayed the rise in blood sugar. In monkeys, the effect on insulin release lasted up to one week after a single dose.

As with all GLP-1 receptor agonists, changes in the C cells of the thyroid occurred in long-term studies in rodents; the mechanism and its limited transferability to humans are described in the chapter Liraglutide (Bjerre Knudsen 2010, PMID 20203154).

### Risks and Side Effects

**Established from randomized trials:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints:** Nausea, diarrhea, and vomiting are the most common side effects. In REWIND, 47.4% of those treated reported gastrointestinal complaints, compared with 34.1% on placebo (Gerstein 2019, PMID 31189511). They increase with higher doses (Frias 2021, PMID 33397768).
- **Hypoglycemia:** rare on its own; more frequent in combination with insulin or sulfonylureas, but less frequent than with insulin alone (Giorgino 2015, PMID 26089386).
- **Pancreatic enzymes:** In AWARD-2, pancreatic enzymes rose on dulaglutide (Giorgino 2015, PMID 26089386). The clinical significance of such increases without symptoms is unclear.
- **Antibodies:** rare (1.6% of those treated), without an accumulation of hypersensitivity reactions (Milicevic 2016, PMID 26847401).
- **Gallbladder:** An increased risk of gallstones and cholecystitis has been demonstrated for the entire class (He 2022, PMID 35344001); details in the chapter Liraglutide.

**Investigated but uncertain:**

- **Pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer:** In the meta-analysis of the outcomes trials, no increased risk for the class (Kristensen 2019, PMID 31422062). Pancreatitis is nevertheless listed as a rare side effect in the prescribing information.
- **Thyroid cancer:** signal in observational studies and meta-analyses for the entire class (Silverii 2025, PMID 40437949), not specific to dulaglutide; see the chapter Liraglutide and Part IV.

**Notes for practice:** Dulaglutide slows gastric emptying, which plays a role in anesthesia and gastroscopy; a large study is under way on whether GLP-1 receptor agonists should be paused before a gastroscopy (NCT07276763). The prescribing information and medical supervision are authoritative.

### Legal Status

- **EU:** Trulicity has been centrally authorized since 2014 for the treatment of type 2 diabetes, now also for adolescents aged 10 and older. Prescription-only. No approval for weight reduction.
- **Germany and Austria:** prescription-only; reimbursable for type 2 diabetes. From 2022 onward, as with other GLP-1 products, there were temporary supply shortages (please check for current information).
- **USA:** approved for type 2 diabetes from age 10 and for reducing cardiovascular risk.
- **Generics:** Since dulaglutide is a recombinantly produced fusion protein, copycat products would be biosimilars with a more complex approval process than for liraglutide or semaglutide. At the time of going to press, to the best of our knowledge no biosimilar is on the market in the EU (please check for current information).
- **Sport (WADA):** not listed (status as of 2026; please check for current information).
- **Gray market:** Dulaglutide plays practically no role on the gray market because a large fusion protein cannot be produced synthetically like small peptides.

### Ongoing Studies and Outlook

**Established (registered studies):**

- **Children and adolescents:** Lilly is testing the higher doses of 3.0 and 4.5 mg in adolescents with type 2 diabetes (NCT06739122) and comparing the tablet orforglipron with dulaglutide in this age group (NCT07668336).
- **Blood vessels and surgery:** One study is investigating dulaglutide in severe narrowing of the cerebral arteries (NCT07282041), another whether it reduces myocardial injury after noncardiac surgery (NCT06324461).
- **Cystic fibrosis:** A small phase 2 trial is investigating dulaglutide in impaired glucose metabolism resulting from the disease (NCT04731272).
- **As a comparator:** Numerous new drugs are being tested against dulaglutide in phase 3 trials, including the exendin-4 fusion protein JY09 (NCT06257966).

**Assessment:** Dulaglutide is a solid, well-studied drug with demonstrated cardiovascular benefit, but it is surpassed by semaglutide and tirzepatide in terms of blood sugar and weight. Its role is likely to shift increasingly to that of a comparator standard in trials. Since Lilly offers its own successors in tirzepatide and orforglipron, further development of dulaglutide for new indications is unlikely.

### Conclusion

Dulaglutide is a weekly injected GLP-1 fusion protein that was tested in the AWARD program against almost all common diabetes drugs. It lowered long-term blood sugar about as much as liraglutide and more than exenatide, sitagliptin, or metformin, but was inferior to semaglutide in SUSTAIN 7, especially in terms of weight. In REWIND, with 9,901 participants, many of them without known cardiovascular disease, it reduced major cardiovascular events by 12%. The side effects correspond to those of the class; antibodies are rare. It is not approved for weight reduction, and treatment belongs in the hands of a physician.
