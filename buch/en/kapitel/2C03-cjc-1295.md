## CJC-1295

Two different substances are sold on the internet under the name CJC-1295 – and that is the most important point of this chapter. The original CJC-1295 from the company ConjuChem is a growth hormone–releasing hormone with a chemical "anchor" that attaches to the protein albumin in the blood and extends its action to around one week. Gray-market dealers also offer "CJC-1295 without DAC," which in the scientific literature is usually called "modified GRF (1-29)." For the first there are two small studies in healthy people; for the second, not a single one.

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic analog of growth hormone–releasing hormone, based on its first 29 amino acids with four substituted building blocks that make it harder to break down.
  - *CJC-1295 with DAC* (Drug Affinity Complex): additionally a reactive anchor at the end of the chain that binds firmly to albumin after injection (Jetté 2005, PMID 15817669).
  - *CJC-1295 without DAC* ("Mod GRF 1-29"): the same chain without the anchor; short duration of action.
- **Origin:** developed by ConjuChem (Canada) in the 2000s; clinical development was not continued.
- **Mechanism of action:** binding to the GHRH receptor of the pituitary gland; rise in growth hormone and IGF-1.
- **EU approval:** no. **US approval:** no.
- **WADA:** prohibited, class S2 – CJC-1295 is explicitly named as a GHRH analog.
- **Evidence level: C** – two small phase 1 studies in healthy people (with DAC); no study of a clinical benefit; no human study for the variant without DAC.

### How it is supposed to work

Natural GHRH is eliminated from the blood within minutes by the enzyme dipeptidyl peptidase-4 and via the kidneys. The DAC anchor was meant to solve this problem: after injection, it reacts with a free sulfur group on albumin. The peptide then "rides" on albumin, which has a lifespan of weeks, and is released slowly. In rats, CJC-1295 thus remained detectable in the blood for over 72 hours (Jetté 2005, PMID 15817669).

This makes CJC-1295 with DAC fundamentally different from all other GH releasers: instead of short pulses, it produces continuous stimulation of the pituitary gland. Whether this is physiologically favorable was one of the open questions of its development. The variant without DAC, by contrast, acts about as briefly as sermorelin and is often offered on the gray market in combination with GH releasers from the ghrelin group (such as ipamorelin) – a practice for which there are no studies.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence (search October 2026):** This book's automated search found **2** human studies in PubMed (`humanstudien_alle`) and **1** entry on ClinicalTrials.gov. A targeted follow-up search additionally yielded a secondary analysis of blood samples from one of the two studies. All papers concern CJC-1295 *with* DAC.

**Dose finding in healthy people:** In two randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind ascending-dose studies, healthy adults between 21 and 61 years of age received a single injection or two to three weekly or biweekly injections. After a single injection, growth hormone rose on average 2- to 10-fold for six days or longer, and IGF-1 1.5- to 3-fold for 9 to 11 days. The half-life was 5.8 to 8.1 days. After multiple doses, IGF-1 remained above baseline for up to 28 days. No serious side effects were reported (Teichman 2006, PMID 16352683).

**Pulsatility:** A second study examined in healthy men between 20 and 40 years of age whether the natural pulsatile pattern of GH secretion is preserved. One week after the injection, the frequency and height of the GH pulses were unchanged, but the baseline level between pulses was increased 7.5-fold; mean GH rose by 46%, IGF-1 by 45% (Ionescu 2006, PMID 17018654).

**Blood proteins:** An analysis of serum samples from 11 young men before and one week after the injection looked for new markers of growth hormone action and found changes in individual blood proteins (Sackmann-Sala 2009, PMID 19386527). This is basic research without clinical implications.

#### The terminated phase 2 trial and the reported death

At the end of 2005, ConjuChem started a randomized, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial with two doses of CJC-1295 in people with HIV and increased abdominal fat – that is, in the same indication for which tesamorelin was later approved (NCT00267527). The registry entry gives the status "terminated" (last updated October 2006), but no reason for termination and no results.

Online forums and some reviews claim that the trial was stopped after the death of a participant. **This death is not documented in the sources reviewed for this book:** neither the registry entry nor any PubMed publication documents it. It is therefore neither possible to confirm that it occurred nor to judge whether it was related to the drug. All that is documented is that the trial ended early and that CJC-1295 was not developed further afterward.

### Animal and laboratory studies

- **Rats:** Of three albumin-anchor variants tested, CJC-1295 released the most growth hormone – about four times as much over two hours as unmodified GRF(1-29) – and remained in the blood for over 72 hours (Jetté 2005, PMID 15817669).
- **Mice:** In mice lacking their own GHRH, daily administration normalized weight and length; administration every 48 or 72 hours was not fully sufficient for this. In the pituitary gland, the number of GH-producing cells increased (Alba 2006, PMID 16822960). An increase in pituitary cells is desirable when a deficiency is to be corrected – in healthy people, its long-term significance is unknown.

### Risks and side effects

**Established from the phase 1 studies:** no serious side effects in a small number of healthy people treated for a short time; tolerability was described as relatively good, especially at the lower doses (Teichman 2006, PMID 16352683). This database is too small to detect rare or long-term risks.

**Plausible but not studied:** The DAC variant keeps growth hormone and IGF-1 continuously elevated for days. Typical consequences of elevated GH levels are fluid retention, joint pain, carpal tunnel symptoms and reduced insulin sensitivity. Whether a persistently elevated IGF-1 increases cancer risk in the long term has not been studied for CJC-1295; for the general evidence, see the introduction to this group and Part IV.

**Gray market:** As early as 2009, a Norwegian laboratory working on behalf of the police and customs analyzed an unknown preparation and found a peptide of 29 amino acids that matched the sequence sold as CJC-1295 (Henninge 2010, PMID 21204297). Danish customs samples contained "modified GRF 1-29" and ghrelin mimetics with an additional, undeclared amino acid, glycine (Gajda 2019, PMID 30136411). Buyers therefore cannot know whether they are receiving the variant with or without DAC, a modified molecule, or something else entirely. An analysis of bodybuilding forums shows that women also use CJC-1295 for weight loss, for the skin or for sleep, relying on anecdotal reports rather than studies (Van Hout 2016, PMID 26771670).

### Legal status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **EU, Germany, Austria:** no approval. CJC-1295 is an unapproved drug substance; supplying and trading it for use in humans is prohibited under medicines laws, even when products are labeled as "research peptides."
- **USA:** no approval. The FDA has classified CJC-1295 – like several other peptides in this book – among the bulk drug substances for compounding pharmacies as a substance with safety concerns (Category 2). These classifications have since been debated controversially in the relevant advisory committee and in professional circles (Willis 2026, PMID 42752426); changes are possible.
- **WADA:** prohibited (S2). Detection methods for CJC-1295 with and without DAC in urine have been described (Memdouh 2021, PMID 34665524); it is also screened for in equestrian sport (Timms 2019, PMID 30938069).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

As of October 2026, ClinicalTrials.gov lists no entry for CJC-1295 other than the phase 2 trial terminated in 2006. *Author's assessment:* No further clinical development is apparent. The principle of "peptide with an albumin anchor for longer action," however, lives on in other drugs, for example in long-acting growth hormone preparations and GLP-1 medications.

### Conclusion

CJC-1295 with DAC raises growth hormone and IGF-1 in healthy people for one to two weeks after a single injection – nothing more has been studied in humans. The only patient trial was terminated in 2006 without a published reason; a frequently claimed death is not documented in the registry or in the scientific literature. For the variant without DAC, which is the one most often sold on the internet, there is not a single human study, and laboratory analyses show that the contents of gray-market products are uncertain.
