## ARA-290 (Cibinetide)

ARA-290 is an artificial peptide derived from the blood-forming hormone erythropoietin (EPO) – the hormone that became famous as a doping agent in cycling. ARA-290, however, is intended precisely not to stimulate blood formation, but only to make use of EPO's tissue-protective properties. In small studies of nerve pain in sarcoidosis, it showed encouraging signals. In the biohacking scene, it is now offered as a "nerve regeneration" and "anti-aging" peptide.

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic peptide of 11 amino acids (Winicki 2022, PMID 36741836); active substance name cibinetide.
- **Origin:** modeled on a segment of the three-dimensional structure of erythropoietin that is thought to be responsible for its tissue protection; developed by the US company Araim Pharmaceuticals.
- **Mechanism of action:** binds to a so-called "innate repair receptor" (a combination of the EPO receptor and the receptor component CD131), not to the classic EPO receptor of blood formation; intended to dampen inflammation and trigger tissue repair.
- **Approval EU/USA:** no.
- **WADA:** prohibited – non-erythropoietic EPO receptor agonists such as ARA-290 are explicitly named in class S2; anti-doping laboratories have developed detection methods (Thomas 2017, PMID 28941172). As of 2026, please check for updates.
- **Evidence level: B** – several small randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trials; no Phase 3 trial.

### How it is supposed to work

Erythropoietin is produced in the kidney and stimulates the formation of red blood cells in the bone marrow. In the 2000s, animal experiments showed that EPO can also protect nerves, heart and kidneys from damage. As a medicine, however, EPO is unsuitable for this: it increases the number of red blood cells, thickens the blood and raises the risk of thrombosis.

The developers of ARA-290 suspected that the protective effect runs via a different receptor than blood formation. This "repair receptor" is only produced in greater amounts when there is tissue damage or inflammation. ARA-290 is intended to activate only this receptor in a targeted way. In rats with nerve injury, it dose-dependently reduced hypersensitivity to touch and cold for up to 20 weeks and dampened the activation of immune cells in the spinal cord (Swartjes 2014, PMID 24529189).

ARA-290 is rapidly broken down in the blood; an anti-doping laboratory described pronounced enzymatic degradation with one main breakdown product (Thomas 2017, PMID 28941172).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence (search October 2026):** The automated search found **5** human studies in PubMed and **5** entries on ClinicalTrials.gov. One further study (diabetic macular edema) was found through a targeted search. All studies are small and lasted four to twelve weeks.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sarcoidosis, pilot study (Heij 2012, PMID 23168581) | 22 people with nerve pain in sarcoidosis, double-blind RCT, infusion | 4 weeks | neuropathy questionnaire −11.5 vs. −2.9 points; main pain scale improved equally in both groups |
| Sarcoidosis, injection under the skin (Dahan 2013, PMID 24136731) | People with nerve fiber loss in sarcoidosis, blinded, placebo-controlled study | 28 days | improvement in symptoms, more nerve fibers in the cornea, longer walking distance |
| Sarcoidosis, Phase 2b (Culver 2017, PMID 28475703; NCT02039687) | 64 people, RCT with three doses and placebo | 28 days | corneal nerve fiber area significantly increased only at the middle dose; pain improved in all groups including placebo |
| Type 2 diabetes with neuropathy (Brines 2015, PMID 25387363) | People with type 2 diabetes and nerve pain, Phase 2, vs. placebo | 28 days plus 28 days | HbA1c and blood lipids improved, neuropathy questionnaire better |
| Diabetic macular edema (Lois 2020, PMID 32674280) | 9 people, Phase 2 without control group | 12 weeks | no improvement in visual acuity or retinal thickness; no antibodies against the active substance |
| Healthy people, mood (Cerit 2015, PMID 26431906) | 36 healthy people, double-blind RCT, single dose | 1 week | no effect on mood; findings do not clearly support an antidepressant effect |

#### Sarcoidosis – the main area of application

Sarcoidosis is an inflammatory disease in which small nodules form in the lungs, skin, eyes and other organs. Many of those affected suffer from burning pain and abnormal sensations due to damaged small nerve fibers (small fiber neuropathy). There are hardly any effective treatments.

In the first pilot study with 22 people, a questionnaire on neuropathy symptoms improved considerably more on ARA-290 than on placebo. However, the scores of the main pain questionnaire and of fatigue improved equally in both groups – an indication of a substantial placebo effect (Heij 2012, PMID 23168581).

The largest study, a Phase 2b trial with 64 people, chose as its primary endpoint the area of the small nerve fibers in the cornea of the eye, which can be measured with a special microscope. Only at the middle of three doses did this area increase significantly compared with placebo; the low and the high dose showed no significant effect. Pain improved in all groups, including placebo (Culver 2017, PMID 28475703). The corneal nerve fibers are a surrogate marker (surrogate endpoint): whether more nerve fibers in the cornea also mean less pain in the long term has not been demonstrated.

A review by members of the Leiden research group rated the prospects as promising (van Velzen 2014, PMID 24555851). It should be noted that a large proportion of the studies come from the developer company and researchers closely associated with it.

#### Diabetes

In people with type 2 diabetes and painful neuropathy, long-term blood glucose (HbA1c), blood lipids and neuropathy symptoms improved on ARA-290 over 28 days (Brines 2015, PMID 25387363). The number of participants is not stated in the abstract; the study was short. A separately registered Swedish study on the glucose-lowering effect in prediabetes with 24 people planned has an unknown status (NCT01933529). In nine people with diabetic macular edema – a swelling of the center of the retina – visual acuity and retinal thickness did not improve (Lois 2020, PMID 32674280).

### Animal and laboratory studies

Animal research on ARA-290 is broad. In rats, mice and pigs, protective effects have been described in nerve pain (Swartjes 2014, PMID 24529189; Pulman 2013, PMID 23262243), kidney damage after impaired blood flow in pigs (van Rijt 2013, PMID 23302512), diabetes in rats (Muller 2016, PMID 26736179) and Alzheimer-like changes (Al-Onaizi 2022, PMID 34343617). In a long-term study in aging rats over 15 months, ARA-290 reduced signs of inflammation in the heart and attenuated age-related deterioration in cardiac function (Winicki 2022, PMID 36741836) – the marketing as an "anti-aging peptide" relies on this study. All of these results come from animal experiments.

### Risks and side effects

#### Documented

No safety problems were reported in any of the published studies (Heij 2012, PMID 23168581; Brines 2015, PMID 25387363). In the twelve-week macular edema study, no serious side effects and no antibodies against ARA-290 occurred (Lois 2020, PMID 32674280). However, the database is small, with considerably fewer than 200 people treated in total and treatment durations of twelve weeks at most.

#### Theoretical

- **EPO-like effects:** ARA-290 was designed so that it does not stimulate blood formation. Whether EPO-like effects nevertheless occur with longer use or higher doses, such as those circulating on the gray market, has not been studied in humans.
- **Tumor growth:** EPO itself is suspected of promoting tumor growth in some cancers. Whether this plays a role for ARA-290 is unknown; long-term data are lacking.
- **Gray market:** The studies were conducted with pharmaceutical-quality product. For freely sold products, purity and content are unknown.

### Legal status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **EU, Germany, Austria:** not approved. Supplying it for use in humans is not permitted.
- **USA:** no FDA approval.
- **WADA:** prohibited in and out of competition (S2, peptide hormones and related substances; ARA-290 is named as an example of non-erythropoietic EPO receptor agonists).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed:** As of October 2026, no ongoing trial with ARA-290 or cibinetide is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. The largest trial to date (NCT02039687) was completed in 2015; no Phase 3 trial has been started since. The macular edema trial was ended with nine people (NCT06626971).

**Author's assessment:** The results in sarcoidosis neuropathy were interesting enough for a Phase 3 trial, which, however, has not discernibly been started more than ten years after the Phase 2b trial. Without a new developer or funder, approval is unlikely. Gray-market marketing as an anti-aging agent relies on rat studies and has little to do with the applications studied in humans.

### Conclusion

ARA-290 is a peptide derived from erythropoietin that is intended to protect tissue without stimulating blood formation. In small randomized trials in sarcoidosis-related nerve damage, nerve fibers in the cornea increased, but only at one of three doses, and pain also improved markedly on placebo (Culver 2017, PMID 28475703). Tolerability was good in the short trials; long-term data are lacking. There have been no new registered trials since 2015; ARA-290 is prohibited in sport.
