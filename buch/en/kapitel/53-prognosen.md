## Forecasts 2030 to 2035

Predictions in medicine have a poor track record. This book, too, contains examples: animal experiments and observational data raised hopes that GLP-1 drugs would slow cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease. In the two phase 3 trials evoke and evoke+ with a combined 3,808 participants, however, the disease progressed just as quickly with semaglutide tablets as with placebo (Cummings 2026, PMID 41865758). In Parkinson's disease, the phase 3 trial Exenatide-PD3 with 194 participants found no indication that exenatide slows progression (Vijiaratnam 2025, PMID 39919773).

This chapter therefore strictly separates, in every section:

- **Established:** what is entered in trial registries (with a planned completion date), what has been published in peer-reviewed studies, and what authorities have decided. Even what is established can change – completion dates shift, trials are terminated.
- **Author's assessment:** a reasoned expectation that may be wrong. It is labeled as such and justified in each case.

The registry data reflect the status as of October 2026.

### Group A – Metabolism and weight

**Established:**

- By 2032, at least nine large outcome and liver trials will end: SURMOUNT-MMO on tirzepatide (NCT05556512) and REDEFINE 3 on CagriSema (NCT05669755) in 2027, TRIUMPH-Outcomes on retatrutide (NCT06383390) and LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis on survodutide (NCT06632457) in 2029, MARITIME-CV and MARITIME-HF on maridebart cafraglutide (NCT07037433, NCT07037459) as well as AMBIENCE on zenagamtide (NCT07861100) in 2030, ATTAIN-Outcomes on orforglipron (NCT07241390) in 2031, and SYNERGY-Outcomes on tirzepatide and retatrutide (NCT07165028) in 2032.
- Pure amylin agents are in phase 3: eloralintide (ENLIGHTEN-1, NCT07321886, completion 2030) and petrelintide (ZUPREME-3, NCT07843498, completion 2029).
- For CagriSema and orforglipron, according to the respective chapters, marketing applications have been submitted or announced; for retatrutide, they are expected after completion of TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 (as of October 2026, please check).
- In twelve countries, patent protection for semaglutide ends in 2026, including large markets outside the EU and the US (Levi 2026, PMID 42437874). In the EU and the US, generics are expected around 2031/2032 (as of 2026, please check).

**Author's assessment:** by 2030, several new classes of agents are likely to be approved in the EU – at least one amylin combination, one triple agonist, and one small molecule as a tablet. Reasoning: the phase 3 programs are well advanced, the results published so far are clearly positive, and the manufacturers have submitted or announced marketing applications. A mean weight loss of 20 to 25% in studies is then likely to be no longer the exception. More important than a new record, however, will be the question of which agent suits which person: who mainly needs heart protection, who has fatty liver disease, who wants to avoid injections, who tolerates side effects poorly. Between 2030 and 2035, I expect substantially falling prices for semaglutide in Europe due to generics, while the newest agents remain expensive. I consider the risk of two-tier care – the most potent drugs for those who pay out of pocket, older drugs for everyone else – to be real.

### Group B – Healing, regeneration, and immune system

**Established:**

- The phase 3 trial of thymosin alpha-1 after colorectal cancer surgery with 2,500 participants is scheduled to end in March 2027 (NCT05086614).
- The phase 3 trial SEER-2 with thymosin beta-4 eye drops for neurotrophic keratopathy was completed in May 2026 according to the registry; results were not available in October 2026 (NCT05555589).
- A Chinese phase 2 trial with recombinant thymosin beta-4 after heart attack is planned (NCT07586865, 189 participants, completion May 2028).
- For BPC-157, a small randomized study with 30 participants after shoulder surgery is registered but has not yet started (NCT07803250, planned completion August 2027).

**Author's assessment:** I consider approval of BPC-157, TB-500, or KPV in the EU by 2035 to be very unlikely. Reasoning: there are no phase 2 or phase 3 programs and no manufacturer with a discernible interest in approval, and even a positive result from the small shoulder study would only be a first indication. More realistic is a niche approval for thymosin beta-4 eye drops for a rare corneal disease, provided SEER-2 turns out positive. Thymosin alpha-1 could play a greater role in cancer follow-up care in China if the colorectal cancer study is positive – but with reservations because of the lack of double-blinding.

### Group C – Growth hormone axis

**Established:**

- Ibutamoren (not a peptide) is being tested as LUM-201 in a phase 3 trial with 150 children with growth hormone deficiency (NCT06948214, completion January 2028). A phase 2 trial with 202 older people with obesity is to study LUM-201 in addition to semaglutide (NCT07754045, completion February 2028).
- The long-term registry for mecasermin runs until the end of 2027 (NCT00903110).
- For sermorelin, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, hexarelin, and IGF-1 LR3, no ongoing study is registered (see the respective chapters).

**Author's assessment:** I consider a new approval of a growth hormone–releasing peptide for adults by 2035 to be unlikely. Reasoning: the substances are old and hardly patentable, and the safety questions surrounding persistently elevated IGF-1 levels (see Part IV) make large studies expensive and risky. Another trend is interesting: because substantial weight loss also costs muscle, the growth hormone axis could receive new attention as a possible means of protecting muscle – the study of LUM-201 and semaglutide is a first example. Whether any benefit will result is completely open.

### Group D – Brain, sleep, and longevity

**Established:**

- Elamipretide (SS-31) has had accelerated approval in the US for Barth syndrome since September 2025, tied to a confirmatory study (4TAZPower, NCT07531251, completion November 2029). The phase 3 trial ReNEW in dry macular degeneration is scheduled to end in September 2027 (NCT06373731).
- For Cerebrolysin, only smaller studies are ongoing, for example in CADASIL (NCT05755997).
- For Semax, Selank, Dihexa, DSIP, Pinealon, humanin, FOXO4-DRI, and MOTS-c, no reliably registered study administering the agent is listed. For Epitalon, there is only an uncontrolled study in which it is one of five components of a skin serum (NCT07888595).
- In the US, the classification of several of these peptides for pharmacy compounding is being reassessed (see the chapters Semax and Dihexa; Willis 2026, PMID 42752426).

**Author's assessment:** the longevity peptides are likely to remain by 2035 what they are today: substances with interesting animal data and great online demand, but without proof of benefit in humans. Reasoning: studies on "healthy aging" require many years and clear endpoints, which hardly exist yet; no manufacturer has a discernible program. Should US authorities approve individual peptides for compounding, this would increase their spread without improving the data. Elamipretide is the only agent in this group with a realistic path to broader approval, and even that only if ReNEW turns out positive.

### Group E – Hormones, sexuality, and pigmentation

**Established:**

- The phase 3 trial of afamelanotide in vitiligo with 200 participants was completed in June 2026 according to the registry; peer-reviewed results were not available in October 2026 (NCT06109649).
- Kisspeptin is being studied in small phase 2 trials in congenital hormone deficiency (NCT05896293, completion May 2028) and in hypothalamic amenorrhea (NCT07224438, completion May 2030).
- Oxytocin nasal spray is being tested as an adjunct to couples therapy for post-traumatic stress disorder with 240 participants (NCT06194851, completion September 2028).

**Author's assessment:** the most conceivable development by 2030 is an extension of the afamelanotide approval to vitiligo – if the phase 3 trial is positive. Kisspeptin is likely to remain a research and diagnostic tool; approval by 2035 would require larger comparative studies, which are lacking so far. For oxytocin nasal spray, I do not expect approval for autism or social anxiety, at most evidence for narrowly defined uses in combination with psychotherapy. Melanotan II will remain a gray-market product.

### Group F – Collagen, nutrition, and cosmetics

**Established:**

- A placebo-controlled study with 204 participants is testing collagen hydrolysate in knee osteoarthritis, with pain as the primary endpoint (NCT04998188, completion December 2027).
- A phase 1/2 study with 144 participants is examining carnosine in peripheral artery disease, with six-minute walking distance as the primary endpoint (NCT06480760, completion October 2028).
- For lactotripeptides, Matrixyl, and Argireline, no ongoing study is registered.

**Author's assessment:** this group will remain food and cosmetics; drug approvals are not to be expected, because the products are already freely available and nobody would want to bear the costs of an approval program. What could change is the quality of the evidence: independently funded, preregistered studies such as the one on knee osteoarthritis will show whether the small effects from manufacturer-funded studies hold up. I expect the effects in such studies to turn out smaller, if anything.

### Cross-cutting topics

#### Long-term data

**Established:** the WHO guideline on GLP-1 therapies for obesity issues only a conditional recommendation and cites limited long-term data as one of the reasons (Celletti 2026, PMID 41324410). The ongoing outcome trials follow their participants for several years, but hardly any for more than ten years.

**Author's assessment:** by 2035, ten-year data will come mainly from national registries and health insurance data, not from randomized trials. They will capture rare side effects better, but will be less able to separate cause and effect. For children and adolescents, for whom several studies run into the early 2030s, the question of long-term consequences is particularly urgent.

#### Maintenance therapy

**Established:** after stopping, a large part of the lost weight returns. In the STEP 1 extension, it was on average two-thirds one year after the end of semaglutide treatment (Wilding 2022, PMID 35441470). In SURMOUNT-4, participants regained 14.0% in the following year after switching from tirzepatide to placebo (Aronne 2024, PMID 38078870). Switching from the injection to orforglipron maintained a large part of the weight loss in ATTAIN-MAINTAIN (Aronne 2026, PMID 42120723). Further studies on weight maintenance are ongoing (TRIUMPH-6, NCT06859268; switching to monthly agents, NCT07575399, NCT07845500). Modeling studies suggest that less frequent dosing could maintain part of the effect; this has not yet been tested in studies and is no basis for making changes to treatment on your own (Cengiz 2025, PMID 39950222).

**Author's assessment:** by 2030, a stepped model will become established: first a phase with a highly effective agent, then maintenance with a tablet, a monthly product, or a lower intensity. Reasoning: several manufacturers are testing exactly this sequence, and it is attractive for payers. Whether maintenance is needed for life, or whether some people can stop without substantial weight regain, will remain one of the most important open questions.

#### Personalization

**Established:** precision medicine in obesity already exists for rare genetic forms: setmelanotide is approved only for certain gene defects (see the chapter Setmelanotide). Orforglipron is being tested in a study with 2,001 participants with overweight to prevent progression to obesity or complications (ATTAIN-NOW, NCT07794579, completion September 2030). The WHO has announced that it will next develop criteria for who should be treated first when resources are limited (Celletti 2026, PMID 41324410).

**Author's assessment:** a reliable test that predicts in advance who will respond well to which agent is not in sight by 2030. Personalization will initially be pragmatic: according to coexisting conditions, tolerability, willingness to inject, and costs. The question of whether drugs should be used even before obesity develops will remain ethically and economically controversial.

#### Regulation of the gray market

**Established:** the laboratory analyses and case reports from Part I and Part IV show counterfeits, dosing errors, and impurities. Meanwhile, unapproved agents such as retatrutide and zenagamtide are also being sold online (Rubin 2026, PMID 42853576). In the US, pharmacy copies of semaglutide and tirzepatide were largely ended after the shortages ended; modified preparations continue to exist (Belcourt 2026, PMID 41689811). The classification of peptides for compounding is being renegotiated in the US (Willis 2026, PMID 42752426).

**Author's assessment:** the gray market will not disappear by 2035, but it will change. Falling prices for approved drugs are likely to reduce the incentive for counterfeiting semaglutide. At the same time, every new agent that causes a stir in studies will be offered as a "research peptide" even before its approval. I expect stricter controls on online sellers and payment channels in the EU, but no effective solution as long as orders from countries with weak oversight remain possible.

#### Artificial intelligence in peptide design

**Established:** so far, all published evidence comes from laboratory and animal experiments. A research team used machine learning methods to design 10,000 new GLP-1 receptor agonists, selected 60 on the computer, and tested them in the laboratory; two candidates acted about three times as long as semaglutide in mice (Wei 2025, PMID 40787887). Another method designed ring-shaped peptides that bind specifically to predefined proteins; the structures determined in the laboratory closely matched the computer models (Rettie 2025, PMID 40542165). Using a learning model, new antimicrobial peptides were derived from bacterial peptides, one of which reduced the bacterial load by more than 95% in mice with a thigh infection (Wang 2025, PMID 39825096). The oral PCSK9 peptide enlicitide was found using another high-throughput method and is already in phase 3 (Johns 2023, PMID 37125593; NCT06008756).

**Author's assessment:** by 2030, the first peptides designed using AI methods will be in early human studies; approval by 2035 is possible but not likely, because even a quickly found molecule has to pass through all clinical phases. AI shortens the search for candidates, not testing in humans. For readers, this means: offers that promise "AI-optimized peptides" are advertising, not proof of efficacy.

### The book's conclusion

Of the peptides in this book, only a few have proven in large human studies what they can do – above all the GLP-1-based drugs, whose benefit extends to fewer cardiovascular events. For most of the peptides advertised on the internet as miracle cures for healing, muscle, or a long life, by contrast, there are only animal experiments, small studies, or no data at all – and no ongoing large study that would change this soon. The future of peptide medicine will therefore be decided not in online shops but in the outcome trials ending between 2027 and 2032. If you are interested in a peptide, you are best served by three questions: Are there randomized studies in humans? Is the product a tested drug? And is a physician supervising its use?
