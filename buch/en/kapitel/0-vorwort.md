# Preface

For more than five years, peptides haven't let go of me. It started the way it does for many people: with sport, with the wish to recover faster, to perform better and to get more out of my body. Through biohacking I came across substances most people have never heard of – BPC-157, MOTS-c, Semax, GHK-Cu, later retatrutide. I dug deep into them, read countless personal reports and talked with many people who use them.

What kept bothering me along the way: the information you find is either advertising or scaremongering. Vendor sites promise miracles, forums swing between enthusiasm and horror stories, and on the other side there is often blanket rejection. What is almost always missing is a sober look at the studies. What was actually investigated – in humans, not just in rats? How large were the studies? What is known about the risks?

This book is the book I wish I'd had myself five years ago.

I am convinced that peptides are among the most exciting developments in medicine. Within just a few years, the GLP-1 drugs have changed how we think about obesity, diabetes and heart health. Many other peptides show a potential in the lab and in early studies that thrills me. I believe that one day they can solve many of our health problems and help us get the best out of our bodies.

But – and this is exactly why this book turned out the way it did – enthusiasm is no substitute for evidence. For many peptides celebrated on the internet, there is still not a single meaningful study in humans to this day. No matter how many personal reports there are, they remain individual cases, not proof. Anyone who blindly follows every hype takes risks that often nobody knows: contaminated products from the grey market, unknown long-term effects, legal problems.

That's why, for each of the 58 peptides, this book gives you both: the potential and the open questions. Every statement is backed by a source you can look up yourself. The evidence levels from A to D show you at a glance how solid the knowledge is. And where the data are thin, it says so explicitly.

You won't find dosages or places to buy here. That's intentional. This book is meant to help you form your own judgement – and to ask the right questions when you talk to your doctor.

Research is moving faster than ever. Much of what is still open today will be answered within a few years. I'm looking forward to it – and I hope this book accompanies you along the way.

*Josef Gallab*
