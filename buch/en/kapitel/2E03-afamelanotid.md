## Afamelanotide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** linear peptide of 13 amino acids. It corresponds to the body's own hormone alpha-MSH with two building blocks exchanged (norleucine at position 4, the mirror-image D-form of phenylalanine at position 7). These changes make it more stable and more potent. Former names: melanotan I, MT-I, NDP-MSH, CUV1647.
- **Origin:** developed at the University of Arizona together with melanotan II (Hadley 2006, PMID 16412534); later developed into a medicine by the Australian company Clinuvel.
- **Mechanism of action:** agonist at the melanocortin 1 receptor (MC1R) of the pigment cells. It stimulates production of the dark, light-protective pigment eumelanin – without prior UV radiation.
- **Dosage form:** small, self-dissolving implant (16 mg) inserted under the skin that releases the active substance over weeks. It is inserted in specialized centers.
- **Approval EU:** yes, since 2014 (Scenesse) for the prevention of phototoxicity in adults with erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP). The approval was subject to conditions, including a post-authorization safety study and a patient registry (Homey 2025, PMID 40082741).
- **Approval USA:** yes, since 2019 for adults with EPP.
- **WADA:** not listed (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **A** – approval in the EU and USA based on two randomized, placebo-controlled trials, supplemented by several long-term cohorts. Because the disease is rare, the studies are smaller than for common diseases.

### How It Is Supposed to Work

Erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP) is a rare hereditary disease. In Europe, at least one in 140,000 people is affected (Homey 2025, PMID 40082741). Because of an enzyme defect in the production of the red blood pigment, a precursor, protoporphyrin IX, accumulates in the blood and skin. This substance is activated by visible light and then damages tissue and small blood vessels of the skin.

The result is burning pain, often lasting for days, after just a few minutes in sunlight – frequently without visible skin changes. Those affected avoid daylight from childhood on. Conventional sunscreen hardly helps, because it is mainly visible light and not UV radiation that triggers the reaction. Some of those affected also develop liver damage because the excess protoporphyrin is excreted via the bile (Minder 2023, PMID 37109595).

Afamelanotide does not address the cause but the skin: it tans the skin, and the additional eumelanin absorbs part of the light before it reaches the protoporphyrin. Laboratory experiments also suggest anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. How much of this plays a role in humans is unclear.

Unlike melanotan II (chapter 2E02), afamelanotide acts mainly on the pigment receptor MC1R and only slightly on the receptors in the brain. That is why nausea and sexual side effects are much less common.

### Human Studies

**Size of the evidence base (search October 2026):** The automated search for this book found **10** human studies in PubMed (`humanstudien_alle`) and **23** entries on ClinicalTrials.gov. However, the search only covers the name "afamelanotide"; older studies under the names melanotan I or NDP-MSH as well as several cohort studies are not included and are discussed here in addition.

#### Background: Melanotan I in Healthy People

In the 1990s and early 2000s, healthy volunteers in Arizona received daily injections. Their skin darkened on the face, neck and forearms; the eumelanin content of the forearm skin rose by an average of 98% (Dorr 2000, PMID 11045725). Combined with sunlight, the skin tanned more intensely and for longer, and participants without the active substance needed 50% more sun exposure for the same tan (Dorr 2004, PMID 15262693). In a study of 77 fair-skinned people, the effect was even stronger in carriers of certain MC1R gene variants – that is, in people with particularly sun-sensitive skin (Fitzgerald 2006, PMID 16293341). The plan to turn this into skin cancer protection for the general population was not pursued.

#### The Approval Trials in EPP

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harms 2009, PMID 19656325 | 5 people with EPP, open-label pilot study | 120 days | Tolerance to light markedly increased |
| EU trial (Langendonk 2015), PMID 26132941 | 74 people, RCT vs. placebo implant | 9 months | Pain-free time in direct sunlight median 6.0 vs. 0.8 hours; 77 vs. 146 phototoxic reactions |
| US trial (Langendonk 2015), PMID 26132941 | 94 people, RCT vs. placebo implant | 6 months | Pain-free time in direct sunlight median 69.4 vs. 40.8 hours |
| Systematic review (Heerfordt 2023), PMID 36525819 | 40 studies on 18 treatments for EPP | – | moderate positive effect for afamelanotide and mineral sunscreen; no effect for beta-carotene, among others |

In the first pilot study, five patients received two implants 60 days apart. Afterward, they tolerated considerably more light in a standardized light test, and the melanin density of the skin increased (Harms 2009, PMID 19656325).

The approval is based on two double-blind trials published together in the New England Journal of Medicine (Langendonk 2015, PMID 26132941; NCT00979745, NCT01605136). Every 60 days, participants received an implant with afamelanotide or a sham implant. The primary endpoint was the number of hours in direct sunlight without pain, which participants recorded in a diary.

Both trials showed an advantage for afamelanotide, and quality of life improved in both. The absolute figures, however, are sobering: in the European trial, participants spent a median of six hours pain-free in direct sunlight over nine months, compared with just under one hour under placebo. That the values are so low is also due to the disease itself: people who have experienced pain for years continue to avoid the sun even if they tolerate it better. Such diary measurements also depend heavily on weather and season, which makes the trials difficult to compare (Heerfordt 2023, PMID 36525819).

#### Long-Term Data from Practice

Because the approval trials were short and small, observations from routine care are especially important.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biolcati 2015, PMID 25494545 | 115 people in Rome and Zurich, 1,023 implants, observational study | up to 8 years | Quality of life from 31% to 74% of the maximum score |
| Wensink 2020, PMID 32186677 | 117 people in Rotterdam, prospective cohort | median 2 years | 6.1 hours more outdoors per week; 98% continued |
| Homey 2025, PMID 40082741 | 200 people in Germany, post-authorization safety study | ongoing | Quality of life significantly better; 91.0% continued |
| Seidl-Philipp 2026, PMID 41793078 | 20 people in Austria | 2023 | Light tolerance median 15 to 250 minutes; phototoxic reactions in 88% vs. 33% |

The Italian-Swiss observational study included treatments since 2006. Only three people stated that the effect did not meet their expectations; 23% interrupted treatment for other reasons, such as pregnancy or cost (Biolcati 2015, PMID 25494545). In the Dutch cohort, phototoxic reactions became less painful, but their number and duration did not change significantly (Wensink 2020, PMID 32186677). The Austrian analysis of all 20 people treated in 2023 shows particularly marked improvements in quality of life (Seidl-Philipp 2026, PMID 41793078).

These cohorts have no control group. People who respond well are more likely to stay in treatment, and expectations of an expensive medicine influence self-reports. Nevertheless, the consistently high continuation rates for a treatment that requires a doctor's visit every two months are a remarkable signal.

#### Further Observations in EPP

- **Liver:** In a Swiss analysis of 70 treated patients with almost 3,000 liver values, the liver enzyme ALT and bilirubin fell the more implants had been inserted in the previous year (Minder 2023, PMID 37109595). This is an association from observational data, not proof of liver protection.
- **Vitamin D:** Afamelanotide did not change vitamin D levels. In a study of 230 people, deficiency persisted in 71.8%; only supplementation with vitamin D3 raised levels (Kluijver 2024, PMID 38634774).
- **Antibodies:** In 23 of 26 people, no antibodies against the active substance developed over up to six years; three already had measurable antibodies before treatment, which did not change (Lengweiler 2015, PMID 25402764).

#### Vitiligo

In vitiligo, the immune system destroys pigment cells, resulting in white patches. Standard treatment is light therapy with narrowband UVB. Afamelanotide is intended to provide additional stimulation to the remaining pigment cells.

A case series with four people (Grimes 2013, PMID 23407924) was followed by a randomized trial with 55 participants (Lim 2015, PMID 25230094; NCT01430195). The combination of light therapy and monthly implants led to new pigmentation faster than light therapy alone. After 168 days, 48.64% of the area was repigmented in the combination arm, compared with 33.26% under light therapy alone. The advantage was seen mainly in darker skin types (IV to VI). One drawback that experts point out: healthy skin also darkens, so the contrast with the white patches may initially increase (Passeron 2015, PMID 25607635). A study in Asian patients has also been published (Toh 2020, PMID 31987791).

#### Other Uses in Pilot Studies

The following results come from very small, mostly open-label studies and should be understood as hypotheses:

- **Solar urticaria** (hives caused by light): less wheal formation in 5 people after one implant (Haylett 2011, PMID 20969564).
- **Acne:** fewer inflammatory lesions in 3 people (Böhm 2014, PMID 22845050).
- **Hailey-Hailey disease** (hereditary blistering disease): healing of skin lesions in 2 people (Biolcati 2014, PMID 24256215).
- **Stroke:** In an open-label feasibility study with 6 people, administration was feasible; one person died of a recurrent stroke, which the researchers did not attribute to the drug (Stanislaus 2023, PMID 37496004).
- **Healthy skin:** In 9 healthy volunteers, skin redness after UV irradiation was lower six days after the implant (Griffin 2026, PMID 42678656).

### Animal and Laboratory Studies

In cell cultures, afamelanotide has antioxidant effects. In skin cells from people with Hailey-Hailey disease, it activated the protective factor Nrf2 and partially restored disturbed cell division (Biolcati 2014, PMID 24256215). Studies on the stability and degradation products of the active substance concern pharmaceutical quality (Chawathe 2026, PMID 41547183). Today, animal data play a minor role in the assessment in humans because the clinical data are more meaningful.

### Risks and Side Effects

#### Established

- **Common, mostly mild side effects:** nausea, headache, fatigue and reactions at the implant site (Langendonk 2015, PMID 26132941; Wensink 2020, PMID 32186677; Biolcati 2015, PMID 25494545). Serious adverse events in the approval trials were not attributed to the active substance.
- **Darker skin and moles:** The tanning is the intended effect. Moles and freckles also darken. In a study of 15 people with 103 moles, dermoscopy showed temporary changes that had regressed after one year; no new moles or signs of malignancy occurred (Arisi 2021, PMID 33721252). The prescribing information calls for regular skin examinations.
- **Pigment changes in the oral mucosa** have been described (de Baat 2020, PMID 32459219; however, the review mixes findings on the approved implant with those on gray-market melanotan), as has a case of apparent depigmentation with concurrent atopic dermatitis (von Bartenwerffer 2011, PMID 21597671).
- **No effect on vitamin D:** see above (Kluijver 2024, PMID 38634774).

#### Open Questions

Data on pregnancy are largely lacking; in practice, treatment is paused. Afamelanotide is not approved for children and adolescents (Barman-Aksözen 2026, PMID 41885813), even though EPP usually begins in early childhood. Individual off-label treatments of children have been published as case reports (Minder 2026, PMID 41542313). Whether decades of stimulation of the pigment cells affect the risk of skin cancer cannot be conclusively determined with the observation periods available so far.

#### Distinction from the Gray Market

"Melanotan I" is offered on the internet as a tanning agent. Such products share only the name of the active substance with the approved implant: they are sold as powder for self-injection, purity and content are untested, and there is no medical monitoring of the skin. The safety data from EPP treatment cannot be transferred to them (Habbema 2017, PMID 28266027).

### Legal Status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **EU:** centrally approved since 2014 as an orphan medicine (Scenesse) for adults with EPP. Treatment is provided in specialized centers; a safety study and a European registry are part of the conditions (Homey 2025, PMID 40082741).
- **Germany and Austria:** available through specialized porphyria and dermatology clinics; reimbursement must be clarified on a case-by-case basis. Published treatment cohorts exist from both countries (Homey 2025, PMID 40082741; Seidl-Philipp 2026, PMID 41793078).
- **USA:** approved since 2019 for adults with EPP and phototoxic reactions.
- **Cost:** Afamelanotide is one of the expensive medicines for rare diseases. In England, the assessment body NICE declined funding – so far the only therapy in its program for very rare diseases to be rejected. Patient representatives criticize the calculation used (Barman-Aksözen 2024, PMID 38791830).
- **WADA:** not listed.

### Ongoing Studies and Outlook

**Established (registered studies):**

- **Vitiligo, phase 3:** A trial with 200 people compares afamelanotide plus narrowband UVB with light therapy alone (NCT06109649). The planned end was June 2026; peer-reviewed results had not been published in PubMed at the time of going to press. A small study on facial vitiligo (NCT05210582) is registered.
- **Other rare light-related diseases:** small phase 2 trials in variegate porphyria (NCT05854784, completed) and in xeroderma pigmentosum, a hereditary disease with impaired repair of UV damage (NCT05370235, NCT05159752).
- **Pharmacology:** A pharmacokinetic study in EPP with 28 people has been completed (NCT06388642).

**New competition:** Two tablets are being developed for EPP: dersimelagon, a non-peptide MC1R agonist, and bitopertin, which acts on the production of the red blood pigment. Both showed effects versus placebo in studies; comparisons with afamelanotide are still lacking (Barman-Aksözen 2026, PMID 41885813).

**Author's assessment:** Afamelanotide will remain the reference for EPP for the time being. Whether it plays a role in vitiligo depends on the result of the phase 3 trial; the combination with light therapy is laborious, and JAK inhibitor creams now offer other options. Approval for adolescents would be particularly important for EPP, because the disease begins in childhood.

### Conclusion

Afamelanotide is the best-studied peptide that stimulates skin tanning via the melanocortin system, and the only one approved in the EU. In adults with erythropoietic protoporphyria, it extended pain-free time in sunlight and improved quality of life in two randomized trials; long-term cohorts from several countries show high continuation rates with predominantly mild side effects. In vitiligo, it accelerated repigmentation under light therapy in a randomized trial; the phase 3 results are pending. The controlled implant in a specialized clinic has practically nothing in common with gray-market "melanotan I" for cosmetic tanning.
