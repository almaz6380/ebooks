## FOXO4-DRI

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** comparatively long synthetic peptide derived from a segment of the protein FOXO4. "DRI" stands for *D-retro-inverso*: the peptide is built from mirror-image D-amino acids in reverse order. As a result, it is hardly broken down by the body's own enzymes. An attached, positively charged segment (derived from a viral protein) is intended to smuggle it into cells.
- **Origin:** introduced in 2017 by a Dutch research group led by Peter de Keizer (Erasmus MC Rotterdam).
- **Mechanism of action:** **senolytic** – a substance intended to drive aged, so-called senescent cells specifically into programmed cell death by disrupting the binding between FOXO4 and the protective protein p53.
- **EU approval:** no.
- **US approval:** no.
- **WADA:** not listed by name; as a substance not approved anywhere, FOXO4-DRI falls in our assessment under the catch-all category S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **D** – exclusively animal and laboratory studies.

### How it is supposed to work

**Senescent cells** are cells that, after damage – for example from radiation, chemotherapy, oxidative stress or simply many divisions – no longer divide but also do not die. They remain in the tissue and release a mixture of inflammatory messengers and tissue-degrading enzymes known as SASP (*senescence-associated secretory phenotype*). With age, they increase in many organs. In mice in which such cells were specifically removed genetically, various signs of aging were delayed. This gave rise to the idea of **senolytics**: drugs that eliminate senescent cells.

Senescent cells survive in part because they suppress self-destruction programs. The Rotterdam group found that the protein FOXO4 plays a key role in this: it holds the protein p53, which would otherwise trigger cell death, in the cell nucleus. The peptide FOXO4-DRI disrupts this binding; p53 is displaced from the nucleus, and the senescent cell dies. Healthy, normally dividing cells are said to be largely spared (Baar 2017, PMID 28340339). Structural studies from 2025 confirmed that FOXO4-DRI binds to a disordered segment of p53 – with the cell-penetrating segment of the peptide also involved in the binding (Bourgeois 2025, PMID 40593617).

#### Senolytics in humans: the context

FOXO4-DRI itself has never been tested in humans. For context, it helps to look at other senolytics that have already been studied clinically – above all the combination of the cancer drug dasatinib and the plant compound quercetin (D+Q for short):

- In a first open-label pilot study with 14 people with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, walking distance and gait speed improved, but lung function did not; one serious adverse event occurred (Justice 2019, PMID 30616998).
- In a randomized phase 2 trial with 60 postmenopausal women, D+Q did not reduce the bone resorption marker (primary endpoint missed); only in a subgroup with many senescent cells, examined post hoc, were favorable effects seen (Farr 2024, PMID 38956196).

Clinical senolytics research is thus still in its infancy, and the first controlled results are modest. That is the background against which FOXO4-DRI is sold on the internet as a "rejuvenation peptide."

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded **no** human study with FOXO4-DRI; in total, PubMed lists around 20 papers with the term. No study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. No case reports on use in humans or analyses of gray-market products were found either.

The closest to humans is a laboratory study with human cartilage cells: in cells that had been expanded in the laboratory for cartilage transplantation, FOXO4-DRI removed more than half of the heavily expanded (and therefore more often senescent) cells but did not improve the ability to form new cartilage (Huang 2021, PMID 33996787).

### Animal and laboratory studies

| Study | Model | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Baar 2017, PMID 28340339 | rapidly aging and naturally aged mice; chemotherapy damage | better fitness, denser fur, better kidney function; protection against doxorubicin damage |
| Zhang 2020, PMID 31959736 | naturally aged mice | testosterone production in the testes improved |
| Han 2022, PMID 35510614 | pulmonary fibrosis in mice | less scarring |
| Kong 2025, PMID 39994346 | keloid tissue (excessive scars) in the laboratory | more cell death of senescent connective tissue cells |
| Born 2023, PMID 36515093 | pulmonary hypertension models in mice and rats | removal of senescent cells, including with FOXO4-DRI, **worsened** the pulmonary circulation |

The first description attracted great attention: in rapidly aging mice and in naturally aged animals, physical fitness, coat appearance and kidney function improved (Baar 2017, PMID 28340339). Later papers by other groups, mainly from China, described effects on the testes, lungs and skin.

An important contrary finding comes from cardiovascular research: in models of pulmonary hypertension, eliminating senescent cells – both genetically and with the senolytics ABT263 or FOXO4-DRI – worsened the pressure in the pulmonary circulation and led to the loss of endothelial cells in the lung. The authors conclude that senolytics could harm the pulmonary vessels (Born 2023, PMID 36515093). This shows that senescent cells are not just "legacy waste" but can also have stabilizing functions in some tissues.

### Risks and side effects

**Established:** No data in humans.

**Theoretical – and partly supported by animal data:**

- **Vessels and lungs:** In the animal model, FOXO4-DRI worsened existing pulmonary hypertension (Born 2023, PMID 36515093).
- **p53 and cancer:** p53 is one of the body's most important tumor suppressors. FOXO4-DRI specifically changes where p53 resides in the cell. What long-term consequences this has for cancer defense has not been studied. Conversely, the developers also see the principle as an approach for cancer therapy (Bourgeois 2025, PMID 40593617).
- **Wound healing and tissue repair:** Senescent cells also participate in wound healing and tissue remodeling; their elimination can disrupt these processes.
- **Immune reactions:** The peptide contains a segment from a viral protein and is relatively large; immune reactions with repeated administration are conceivable but have not been studied.

**Gray market:** FOXO4-DRI is offered online as a "senolytic" for injection, often at high prices. Manufacturing a long peptide from D-amino acids is demanding; whether products sold actually have the correct sequence and purity is unverified.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved; as a substance with a pharmacological effect, FOXO4-DRI is considered a medicine, and selling it for use in humans is not permitted (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **USA:** not approved.
- **Sport:** in our assessment WADA category S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established:** No registered clinical trial with FOXO4-DRI (search October 2026). The developer group is working on further developments of the principle; clinical trials with senolytics are running mainly with other agents.

**Assessment:** Senolytics research is one of the most exciting fields of aging research, but the first controlled studies in humans were sobering. For FOXO4-DRI, systematic toxicity studies and a first cautious study in humans would first be needed – ideally in a clearly defined disease, not as general "anti-aging."

### Conclusion

FOXO4-DRI is an artificial peptide that specifically eliminated senescent cells in cell cultures and in mice and improved fitness, fur and kidney function in aged animals. It has never been studied in humans. In animal models of pulmonary hypertension, removing senescent cells with FOXO4-DRI worsened the course of the disease, and the intervention in the cancer-protective protein p53 raises open questions. The data are not sufficient to assess benefit or safety in humans.
