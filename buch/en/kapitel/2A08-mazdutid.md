## Mazdutide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** synthetic, fatty-acid-modified peptide derived from the gut hormone oxyntomodulin; once-weekly injection under the skin. Development codes LY3305677 (Eli Lilly) and IBI362 (Innovent Biologics).
- **Origin:** originally developed by Eli Lilly; brought to approval in China by Innovent Biologics, and studied further outside China by Lilly.
- **Mechanism of action:** dual agonist at the GLP-1 and glucagon receptors – like survodutide, but a distinct molecule.
- **Approval EU:** no.
- **Approval USA:** no.
- **Approval China:** yes, since June 2025 for weight management, and since September 2025 also for type 2 diabetes (brand name Xinermei).
- **WADA:** not listed by name. Since mazdutide is approved by a governmental health authority (China), it no longer falls under S0 according to the wording; use in sport without a medical indication is nevertheless problematic (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **A** – several large Phase 3 trials and an approval, though almost exclusively in Chinese participants.

### How it is supposed to work

Oxyntomodulin is a natural hormone from the gut that – similar to GLP-1 – is released after eating. It activates both the GLP-1 and the glucagon receptor, but only weakly and very briefly. Mazdutide is a stabilized, long-acting version of this principle.

The GLP-1 component dampens appetite, slows gastric emptying and promotes glucose-dependent insulin secretion. The glucagon component is intended to increase energy expenditure and promote fat breakdown in the liver (see also chapter Survodutide). In humans, the contribution of the glucagon component to weight loss has not been measured directly; the hypothesis rests mainly on animal data.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 9 human studies, supplemented by a high-dose Phase 1 trial and a case report. On ClinicalTrials.gov, 44 trials were registered, most of them in China. The approval trials are called **GLORY** (obesity) and **DREAMS** (type 2 diabetes).

#### Obesity: GLORY-1 and GLORY-2

**GLORY-1** (Ji 2025, PMID 40421736) was the approval trial for China. The inclusion criteria were based on Chinese thresholds: BMI of 28 or more, or 24 or more with a comorbidity. From a baseline weight of 87.2 kg, weight decreased after 48 weeks by 11.0% (4 mg) and 14.0% (6 mg), respectively; on placebo it remained practically unchanged. 35.7% and 49.5% of participants, respectively, lost at least 15%. Discontinuations due to side effects were very rare at 0.5–1.5%.

**GLORY-2** (Gao 2026, PMID 42251595) tested a higher dose in people with a BMI of 30 or more. With an average age of 33.9 years, the participants were strikingly young. After 60 weeks, weight loss was 16.65% compared with 1.50% on placebo. The price was markedly more gastrointestinal complaints: vomiting in 53.1% (placebo 1.3%), nausea in 46.9% (3.2%) and diarrhea in 39.4% (6.5%). Nevertheless, only 2.9% discontinued treatment due to side effects.

#### Type 2 diabetes: DREAMS-1 and DREAMS-2

In **DREAMS-1** (Zhu 2026, PMID 41407859), people with type 2 diabetes that was not adequately controlled with diet and exercise alone (mean diabetes duration 1.9 years) received mazdutide without any other diabetes medication. At the higher dose, HbA1c fell by 2.15 percentage points after 24 weeks (placebo −0.14), and weight by 7.81%.

**DREAMS-2** (Guo 2026, PMID 41407860) is the most important comparative trial so far: against the established GLP-1 drug dulaglutide, mazdutide was non-inferior and superior for HbA1c, albeit by a small margin (an additional −0.24 and −0.30 percentage points, respectively). The difference in weight was more pronounced (an additional −3.78 and −5.76%, respectively). Gastrointestinal side effects were more frequent on mazdutide than on dulaglutide.

A comparative trial against semaglutide in people with early type 2 diabetes and obesity (DREAMS-3) has been completed according to the registry (NCT06184568); its design and the baseline data of the 349 participants have been published (Luo 2026, PMID 41260459), but not yet the results.

#### Studies outside China and higher doses

Lilly is studying mazdutide outside China at higher doses. In a small Phase 1 trial with 32 adults in the USA, body weight at the highest dose fell after 20 weeks by 20.0% and 21.0% (two dose-escalation schedules) compared with 0.1% on placebo; no serious side effects occurred (Bhattachar 2025, PMID 40832785).

In the US Phase 2 trial with 179 participants (Hsia 2026, PMID 42628555), weight loss after 32 weeks was up to 18.1% at the highest dose. However, 20% at the highest dose discontinued treatment due to side effects, mostly gastrointestinal complaints. The main analysis also used the "efficacy estimand," which no longer counts people after discontinuation and therefore tends to present the effect more favorably.

#### Case reports

One case report describes a 15-year-old patient with obesity, type 2 diabetes and elevated uric acid who received mazdutide in addition to metformin and insulin and lost 16.8 kg after 36 weeks (Cheng 2025, PMID 41030857). A single case allows no conclusion about efficacy or safety in adolescents; a trial on this is ongoing (NCT07255209).

### Animal and laboratory studies

In diabetic mice (db/db model), mazdutide performed better than dulaglutide in memory and behavioral tests; brain tissue showed less damage (Dong 2025, PMID 40479843). In mice with diet-induced fatty liver and in liver cell cultures, mazdutide reduced fat deposition and signs of inflammation (Gan 2026, PMID 41901218). Whether this translates into benefits for humans is open; a trial on type 2 diabetes with incipient cognitive impairment is registered in China (NCT07083154).

### Risks and side effects

**Documented in studies:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints** are the most common side effects; their frequency increases with dose. In the Phase 2 diabetes trial, diarrhea occurred in 36%, decreased appetite in 29%, nausea in 23% and vomiting in 14% (Zhang 2024, PMID 37943529). In GLORY-2, more than half of participants vomited at least once (Gao 2026, PMID 42251595).
- **Hypoglycemia:** In the Phase 2 diabetes trial, hypoglycemia was reported in 10% on mazdutide and 8% on placebo (Zhang 2024, PMID 37943529).
- **Discontinuations at high doses:** up to 20% in the US Phase 2 trial (Hsia 2026, PMID 42628555).

**Still open:** There is no cardiovascular outcome trial. Long-term data beyond 60 weeks are lacking. The trials included almost exclusively people of Chinese origin, who had different baseline values (lower body weight) than European study populations for the same weight loss. The class risks known for GLP-1 drugs (gallbladder, pancreas, thyroid C-cells in animal studies) have not yet been assessed independently for mazdutide owing to a lack of patient-years.

### Legal status

- **China:** approved (June 2025 for weight management, September 2025 for type 2 diabetes) (Shirley 2025, PMID 41028652).
- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved. Importing it for use is not legal without approval; "mazdutide" products offered on the gray market are not tested medicines.
- **USA:** not approved; Lilly is conducting its own trials.
- **Sport:** not listed by name; status under S0 unclear because of the Chinese approval – as of 2026, please check for updates.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed (registered trials, as of October 2026):**

- Comparison with semaglutide in fatty liver (NCT06884293) and in early type 2 diabetes (NCT06184568, completed).
- Sleep apnea (NCT06931028), hypertension with obesity (NCT07469800), adolescents (NCT07255209).
- Phase 2 trials on MASH (NCT06937749), heart failure with obesity (NCT06862908) and alcohol use disorder (NCT06817356, completed, sponsor Lilly).
- Lilly is also testing mazdutide in a Phase 2 master protocol (NCT06143956).

**Assessment:** Mazdutide is the first glucagon/GLP-1 dual drug with an approval – though only in China. Whether Lilly will seek approval in the USA and Europe probably depends on whether the molecule can be differentiated from tirzepatide and retatrutide from the same company, for example in fatty liver or alcohol use disorder. This is an assessment; specific applications for approval outside China are not known.

### Conclusion

Mazdutide is a long-acting derivative of the gut hormone oxyntomodulin that activates the GLP-1 and glucagon receptors simultaneously. In Chinese Phase 3 trials, body weight decreased by 11–14% after 48 weeks and by 16.65% after 60 weeks at a higher dose; in type 2 diabetes it was slightly superior to dulaglutide. The most common side effects are gastrointestinal complaints, which become very frequent at higher doses. Mazdutide is approved only in China; data on cardiovascular endpoints and on non-Asian populations are largely lacking.
