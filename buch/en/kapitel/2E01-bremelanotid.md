## Bremelanotide (PT-141)

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** ring-shaped (cyclic) peptide of seven amino acids, produced synthetically. It is derived from the body's own hormone alpha-MSH (alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone) and is a close relative of melanotan II (chapter 2E02). In the literature it is also called PT-141.
- **Origin:** emerged from the development of melanotan II at the University of Arizona; developed further by the company Palatin Technologies (Hadley 2006, PMID 16412534).
- **Mechanism of action:** agonist at melanocortin receptors, above all MC4R in the brain. It does not act on the blood vessels of the genitals like sildenafil, but on brain regions involved in sexual desire.
- **Dosage form:** prefilled syringe (autoinjector) for use under the skin as needed, about 45 minutes before sexual activity. Early studies used a nasal spray.
- **Approval EU:** no.
- **Approval USA:** yes, since 2019 as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized disorder of sexual desire (hypoactive sexual desire disorder, HSDD) (Dhillon 2019, PMID 31429064). Use only on medical prescription and in accordance with the prescribing information.
- **WADA:** not listed by name (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **A** – two large randomized phase 3 trials with a combined total of more than 1,200 participants, approval in the USA. However, the clinical benefit is small and disputed (see below).

### How It Is Supposed to Work

The melanocortin system is a network of messengers and five receptors (MC1R to MC5R), described in more detail in the introduction to this part. For bremelanotide, the MC4R receptor is the most important. It is located, among other places, in the hypothalamus, a control center in the diencephalon, where it influences appetite, energy expenditure and – as was discovered by chance – sexual arousal.

The discovery was a by-product of tanning research: men who received melanotan II for skin tanning in the 1990s reported spontaneous erections (Hadley 2005, PMID 15996790). Bremelanotide is a slightly modified form of this molecule. It activates several melanocortin receptors, but at the doses used, MC4R is the most important (Pfaus 2022, PMID 33455598). It is not entirely free of effects on the skin, however – which explains the pigment spots observed.

Exactly how activation of MC4R influences sexual desire is only partly understood. Animal experiments suggest that bremelanotide stimulates the release of the messenger dopamine, which promotes sexual desire, in a specific region of the hypothalamus (the medial preoptic area) (Pfaus 2022, PMID 33455598). A British study using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) in 31 women with HSDD found altered activity under an MC4R agonist in brain regions that process erotic stimuli (Thurston 2022, PMID 36189794). The authors interpret this as reduced self-monitoring and increased receptiveness to erotic stimuli – an interpretation, not a proven explanation.

Important for understanding: the approval does not target "more desire" in healthy people, but a defined diagnosis. HSDD means persistently low sexual desire that causes distress and is not explained by another illness, medications or relationship problems.

### Human Studies

**Size of the evidence base (search October 2026):** The automated search for this book found **16** human studies in PubMed (`humanstudien_alle`) and **10** entries on ClinicalTrials.gov. In addition, there are several reviews and meta-analyses. The assessment rests mainly on the two RECONNECT approval trials, their extension and the safety analysis of the entire development program.

#### Early Studies in Men

Bremelanotide was first developed as a treatment for erectile dysfunction. In phase 1 studies with nasal spray (Diamond 2004, PMID 14963471) and injection (Rosen 2004, PMID 14999221), it triggered measurable erections in healthy men and in men with erectile dysfunction, including those who responded inadequately to Viagra. In a small study with 19 patients, a low dose of bremelanotide enhanced the effect of a low dose of sildenafil (Diamond 2005, PMID 15833522).

A study of 342 men in whom sildenafil had not worked reported a positive result in 33.5% with bremelanotide nasal spray compared with 8.5% with placebo (Safarinejad 2008, PMID 18206919). This paper should be read with caution: a second bremelanotide study by the same first author in women was retracted by the journal because its methods and results could not be verified (Safarinejad 2008, PMID 18179455); other papers by the author were also retracted.

Development of the nasal spray was discontinued after increases in blood pressure were noticed in studies. The company switched to the injection and to women as the target group.

#### Dose Finding in Women

A first small laboratory study with 18 women with arousal disorder showed moderate to high desire more often after a single dose than after placebo, but no measurable change in vaginal blood flow while watching erotic films (Diamond 2006, PMID 16839319).

The decisive phase 2b trial (Clayton 2016, PMID 27181790; NCT01382719) tested three doses against placebo over twelve weeks in premenopausal women. A total of 327 participants were analyzed. For the two higher doses combined, the number of "satisfying sexual events" rose by 0.7 per month compared with 0.2 under placebo. On the FSFI questionnaire (Female Sexual Function Index, a score for sexual function), the increase was 3.6 versus 1.9 points. A subsequent analysis defined the change above which an effect is considered clinically meaningful; these thresholds were then adopted for the phase 3 trials (Althof 2019, PMID 31277966).

#### The RECONNECT Approval Trials

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| RECONNECT 301 and 302 (Kingsberg 2019), PMID 31599840 | 1,267 premenopausal women with HSDD, two RCTs vs. placebo, use as needed | 24 weeks | Desire (FSFI desire) +0.35 points more than placebo; distress reduced by 0.33 points more |
| Open-label extension (Simon 2019), PMID 31599847 | 684 women from both trials, all on bremelanotide | 52 weeks | 272 completed the extension; no new safety signals |
| Subgroup analysis (Simon 2022), PMID 35230162 | 1,202 women from both trials | 24 weeks | Effect similar in almost all age, weight and hormone subgroups |
| Safety analysis (Clayton 2022), PMID 35147466 | around 3,500 people in 43 studies | up to 18 months | Nausea 40.0% vs. 1.3%, flushing 20.3% vs. 1.3% |

The two identical trials 301 and 302 (NCT02333071, NCT02338960) ran in 2015 and 2016, mostly in the USA. The participants injected themselves as needed before sexual activity with 1.75 mg bremelanotide or placebo using an autoinjector. Two primary endpoints were measured: sexual desire (desire domain of the FSFI, scale from 1.2 to 6) and distress due to low desire (a single item of the FSDS-DAO questionnaire, scale 0 to 4).

Both trials met their primary endpoints. In the pooled analysis, desire rose by 0.35 points more than under placebo, and distress fell by 0.33 points more (Kingsberg 2019, PMID 31599840). These figures are statistically clear but small: on a scale up to 6, a third of a point is a modest difference. Scores also improved markedly under placebo – a pattern well known in sexual medicine.

In exit interviews with 242 participants who did not yet know what they had received, women on bremelanotide more often described physical arousal and more desire; those on placebo tended to mention better communication in the relationship (Koochaki 2021, PMID 33538638).

#### Criticism of the Approval Trials

An independent reanalysis based on the documents submitted to the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) reached a different overall verdict (Spielmans 2021, PMID 33678061). The author confirms the small effects but criticizes that around 73% of the outcomes specified in the study protocol were not published. Two findings were missing from the main publication:

- Discontinuations due to side effects were considerably more frequent under bremelanotide (odds ratio 11.98). Mathematically, about one in six treated women discontinued because of side effects who would not have done so under placebo.
- Those who completed the trial and then moved on to the open-label extension more often came from the placebo group. The manufacturer's safety analysis also gives these figures: 70% of the bremelanotide group versus 87% of the placebo group transitioned to the extension (Clayton 2022, PMID 35147466).

Spielmans concludes that the drug is not useful for most women. This is a pointed individual opinion, but it is based on data that the manufacturer's side has also published.

#### Meta-Analyses

A meta-analysis of medications for female sexual dysfunction (including bremelanotide, flibanserin and intranasal oxytocin) found that around 67.7% of the improvement in the treatment groups also occurred under placebo (Weinberger 2018, PMID 29995725). A more recent meta-analysis confirms small improvements in desire and arousal and a reduction in distress for bremelanotide, without comparison with psychotherapy (Toledo 2026, PMID 40543759). A review of the studies completed on ClinicalTrials.gov criticizes inconsistent outcome measures and incomplete reporting (Ashour 2026, PMID 42254382).

#### Other Questions

- **Alcohol:** In a phase 1 study with 24 healthy people, no meaningful interactions occurred with simultaneous alcohol consumption (Clayton 2017, PMID 28189361).
- **Weight:** Because MC4R also controls appetite, bremelanotide was tested in women with obesity. In two small phase 1 studies, calorie intake fell by about 400 kcal per day; after 16 days, weight was 1.3 kg below that of the placebo group (Spana 2022, PMID 35170192). These are short-term data that say nothing about long-term treatment.
- **Brain:** The fMRI study already mentioned (Thurston 2022, PMID 36189794; NCT04179734) is one of the few studies not led by the manufacturer.

### Animal and Laboratory Studies

Animal research on bremelanotide comes mostly from rats. A review summarizes it as follows: bremelanotide activates MC4R on nerve cells in the hypothalamus, which then release more dopamine; in female rats, behavior with which females signal sexual interest increased (Pfaus 2022, PMID 33455598). The review comes from researchers with ties to the development of the drug.

An independent study in female hamsters came to a sobering result: bremelanotide neither changed the receptors in the reward system nor increased the "reward value" of sexual encounters in a behavioral test (Borland 2025, PMID 39793696). The authors conclude that the drug does not act via the classic reward circuit. Exactly how it works therefore remains open.

In cell cultures, bremelanotide inhibited the growth of glioblastoma cells, a type of brain tumor (Suzuki 2024, PMID 39197897). This is a purely laboratory finding with no clinical significance.

### Risks and Side Effects

#### Established

- **Nausea:** the most common side effect. In the phase 3 trials it affected 40.0% compared with 1.3% under placebo and was the most common reason for discontinuation (Clayton 2022, PMID 35147466). A dedicated study with 228 healthy women tested whether the anti-nausea drug ondansetron helps preventively (NCT03973047).
- **Flushing** in 20.3%, **headache** in 11.3%, **injection site reactions** in 5.4% (compared with 1.3%, 1.9% and 0.5% under placebo).
- **Blood pressure:** In a study with 24-hour monitoring in 397 women, systolic blood pressure rose temporarily by 2.4 to 3.2 mmHg after the dose; the peaks usually lasted less than 15 minutes. At the same time, heart rate fell by around 4.6 beats per minute (White 2017, PMID 27977473). The prescribing information therefore advises against use in uncontrolled high blood pressure or known cardiovascular disease.
- **Pigment spots:** rare when used in accordance with the prescribing information. However, when bremelanotide was given on up to 16 consecutive days, more than a third of participants developed localized dark spots (Clayton 2022, PMID 35147466) – an indication of residual activity at the pigment receptor.
- **Interactions:** Bremelanotide briefly slows gastric emptying and in studies lowered the blood levels of indomethacin and naltrexone (Clayton 2022, PMID 35147466).

#### Open Questions

There are insufficient data for pregnancy. A small study is currently measuring transfer into breast milk (NCT06867835). Long-term data beyond 18 months are lacking.

#### Gray Market

Under the name "PT-141," bremelanotide is offered on the internet as a powder for self-injection, often alongside melanotan II. Forensic laboratories identified both substances in samples seized by police from the bodybuilding scene (Mestria 2021, PMID 33245851). Such products carry the same risks as other gray-market peptides (Part I, chapter "The Gray Market"): unknown purity, wrong amounts, contamination, non-sterile manufacturing. The bremelanotide nasal spray is known to have had a stronger effect on blood pressure; gray-market sprays have not been tested.

### Legal Status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **EU:** no approval, and no ongoing approval procedure is known. Sale for human use is not permitted in the EU.
- **Germany and Austria:** no approval. Individual import of a medicine approved in the USA on medical prescription is legally possible within narrow limits; gray-market products do not fall under this.
- **USA:** approved since June 2019 (Vyleesi) for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. Not approved for men, for postmenopausal women or for increasing desire in healthy people. The marketing rights have changed hands several times; according to registry entries, the company Cosette Pharmaceuticals is now responsible.
- **Other countries:** In South Korea, a bridging study with 193 women was conducted for approval there (NCT04943068).
- **WADA:** not listed by name. Please check the current Prohibited List.

### Ongoing Studies and Outlook

**Established (registered studies):**

- A phase 2 trial with 108 people is testing bremelanotide in addition to tirzepatide in obesity (NCT06565611). The background is the appetite-suppressing effect via MC4R. Published, peer-reviewed results were not available in PubMed at the time of going to press.
- A small open-label phase 2 trial with 16 people with diabetic kidney disease has been completed (NCT05709444), likewise without a published result in PubMed.
- The breastfeeding study (NCT06867835) with 10 women was completed in 2025.

**Author's assessment:** As things stand, there is a lack of commercial interest in approval in Europe; no application is known. More interesting is the role of MC4R in obesity research, where an MC4R agonist, setmelanotide (chapter in Part II · A), is already approved for rare genetic forms. Whether bremelanotide finds a niche here will be decided by the study results still pending.

### Conclusion

Bremelanotide is the only approved medicine that targets sexual desire via the melanocortin system in the brain – but only in the USA and only for a narrowly defined group of women. In two large phase 3 trials, desire and distress improved in a statistically clear way, but to a small extent and with a high placebo effect. Nausea affects almost half of users and frequently leads to discontinuation; brief increases in blood pressure call for caution in people with cardiovascular risks. Gray-market products under the name "PT-141" are untested, and for men or people without a diagnosis, any evidence sufficient for approval is lacking.
