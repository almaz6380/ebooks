## Amycretin (Zenagamtide)

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** synthetic peptide that combines GLP-1 and amylin activity in a single molecule ("unimolecular"). Since 2026 it has carried the international nonproprietary name **zenagamtide**; development code NNC0487-0111. It is being developed in two forms: as a weekly injection and as a daily tablet.
- **Origin:** developed by Novo Nordisk.
- **Mechanism of action:** agonist at GLP-1, amylin and calcitonin receptors.
- **Approval EU:** no.
- **Approval USA:** no.
- **WADA:** prohibited as a non-approved substance under S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **B** – randomized Phase 1 and Phase 2 trials published; Phase 3 began in 2026.

### How it is supposed to work

CagriSema (see previous chapter) combines two separate active substances – the amylin analog cagrilintide and the GLP-1 agonist semaglutide – in one injection. Amycretin goes one step further: a single peptide activates both receptor systems. This simplifies manufacturing and dosing and in principle allows a tablet form, as already exists with oral semaglutide.

GLP-1 and amylin both promote the feeling of satiety, slow gastric emptying and dampen glucagon release, but act via different receptors and nerve pathways in the brainstem. In cell experiments, amycretin activated human, mouse and rat receptors for GLP-1, amylin and calcitonin. In rats with diet-induced obesity, it reduced energy intake by 47% and body weight by 18% over 21 days, while energy expenditure was preserved; insulin sensitivity improved and fatty liver decreased (Kuhre 2025, PMID 40706446). These are animal data.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 4 human studies. The automated registry search under the name "amycretin" found only one entry; under the new name zenagamtide and the development code NNC0487-0111, however, around 30 trials are registered on ClinicalTrials.gov, including the Phase 3 program **AMAZE** (weight) and **AMBITION** (type 2 diabetes), launched in 2026.

#### Tablet form: first-in-human study

The first-in-human study tested amycretin as a tablet (Gasiorek 2025, PMID 40550229). 144 adults with overweight or obesity received single or multiple doses for up to twelve weeks. 62% reported at least one adverse event; all were mild or moderate and increased with dose. About half of the events affected the gastrointestinal tract. Blood levels rose in proportion to the dose. The weight data were only a secondary, exploratory endpoint of this safety study.

#### Injection form: Phase 1b/2a

In the study with the weekly injection (Dahl 2025, PMID 40550231), 125 people received amycretin or placebo in several study parts with different target doses and treatment durations. The weight loss was remarkable for an early study:

- highest dose, 36 weeks: −24.3% compared with −1.1% on placebo
- second-highest dose, 36 weeks: −22.0% compared with +1.9%
- medium dose, 28 weeks: −16.2% compared with +2.3%
- low dose, 20 weeks: −9.7% compared with +2.0%

These figures should be read with caution: the groups were small, the study took place at a single center, and the authors themselves report that many participants left the study early – largely for reasons unrelated to side effects. A weight loss of more than 20% after 36 weeks is a strong signal, but not yet a robust estimate for broad use.

#### Type 2 diabetes: two Phase 2 trials

In 2026, two parallel dose-finding trials were published in people with type 2 diabetes taking metformin (with or without an SGLT2 inhibitor):

- **Tablet** (Mora 2026, PMID 42532079): After 36 weeks, HbA1c fell by 0.9 to 1.4 percentage points (baseline around 8%). Gastrointestinal complaints occurred in 26–47% (placebo 23%). Serious adverse events affected 4% on zenagamtide and no one on placebo.
- **Injection** (Mora 2026, PMID 42532080): HbA1c fell by 0.9 to 1.7 percentage points depending on dose. Serious adverse events occurred in 8% of participants, distributed across all dose groups and placebo.

The weight results of these diabetes trials are not quantified in the abstracts and are therefore not given here.

### Animal and laboratory studies

The preclinical data come from the manufacturer (Kuhre 2025, PMID 40706446): receptor activation in cell cultures, weight loss and improved insulin sensitivity in obese rats, less fatty liver and accumulation in appetite-regulating brain regions of mice. Independent animal studies are scarcely available.

### Risks and side effects

**Documented in studies:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints** are the most common side effects, affecting the majority of those treated in the early studies; they were mostly mild to moderate and dose-dependent (Gasiorek 2025, PMID 40550229; Dahl 2025, PMID 40550231).
- **Serious adverse events** were rare in the Phase 2 trials; in the tablet trial they occurred only on zenagamtide (4% vs. 0%), in the injection trial they were evenly distributed (Mora 2026, PMID 42532079; PMID 42532080).
- **Kidney function:** In impaired kidney function up to end-stage renal failure, blood levels after a single dose did not change to a clinically relevant extent (Oldenburg 2026, PMID 42443140).

**Still open:** long-term safety, effects on bone (calcitonin receptor), muscle mass and the cardiovascular system. For the class risks of GLP-1 drugs (gallbladder, pancreas), there are still too few treatment-years.

**Gray market:** Even before the start of Phase 3, zenagamtide is being offered online as a "research peptide." A report in the journal JAMA describes a growing black market for the two non-approved substances retatrutide and zenagamtide and the associated risks (Rubin 2026, PMID 42853576). Anyone using such products knows neither the content nor the purity nor a tested dosage (see Part I and Part IV).

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved; use only in clinical trials.
- **USA:** not approved.
- **Sport:** WADA class S0 (non-approved substance), as of 2026, please check for updates.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed (registered trials, as of October 2026):**

- **AMAZE 1** (NCT07339423): Phase 3 with 1,150 adults with overweight/obesity, injection form.
- **AMAZE 2** (NCT07533175): overweight with type 2 diabetes.
- **AMAZE 3 and 4** (NCT07571005, NCT07571109): sleep apnea without and with positive airway pressure therapy, respectively.
- **AMAZE 6** (NCT07509307) and another trial (NCT07481630): knee osteoarthritis.
- **AMAZE 9** (NCT07720271): tablet form in overweight.
- **AMAZE 12** (NCT07503210): weight maintenance after weight loss.
- Comparison with semaglutide (NCT07668414; in type 2 diabetes NCT07400107) and with insulin glargine in type 2 diabetes (NCT07797335, AMBITION 7).
- Outcome trials: heart failure with obesity (NCT07567001, 5,610 participants) and cardiovascular events in atherosclerosis (NCT07861100, 8,500 participants planned).

**Assessment:** With this broad Phase 3 program, Novo Nordisk is clearly betting on making amycretin/zenagamtide the successor to semaglutide. Results of the first Phase 3 trials are expected in 2027 at the earliest, and approval hardly before 2028. Whether the impressive figures of the small Phase 1b/2a study hold up in large trials is open.

### Conclusion

Amycretin – since 2026 zenagamtide – is a single peptide that activates GLP-1 and amylin receptors simultaneously and is being developed as an injection and as a tablet. In a small Phase 1b/2a study, weight fell by up to 24.3% after 36 weeks; in Phase 2 trials in type 2 diabetes, HbA1c fell by up to 1.7 percentage points. The most common side effects affect the gastrointestinal tract. The Phase 3 trials began in 2026; until a possible approval, zenagamtide is purely an investigational product, and gray-market goods are no substitute.
