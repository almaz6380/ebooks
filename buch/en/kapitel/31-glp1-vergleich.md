## GLP-1 & Co. Compared Head to Head

No other group in this book has been studied as thoroughly as the incretin drugs: GLP-1 receptor agonists such as liraglutide and semaglutide, the dual agonist tirzepatide, and a series of successors that additionally act on the glucagon or amylin receptor. For these drugs there are approval programs with tens of thousands of participants, large cardiovascular trials, and increasingly also head-to-head trials. This chapter places the most important results side by side. All figures come from the chapters of Part II, Group A, and the studies cited there; that is also where you will find the limitations of each individual study.

This chapter is not a decision aid for treatment. Which drug may be suitable in an individual case depends on coexisting conditions, tolerability, approval status, insurance coverage, and availability, and belongs in a physician's hands. The respective prescribing information and medical supervision are what count.

### First: Why you cannot simply line up studies side by side

The tables in this chapter invite you to compare figures from different studies – for example, 20.9% weight loss with tirzepatide versus 14.9% with semaglutide. Such cross-trial comparisons are valid only to a limited extent. The reasons:

- **Different participants:** People with type 2 diabetes lose considerably less weight on all incretin drugs than people without diabetes. Studies in China or Japan enroll people with a lower starting weight than studies in North America and Europe.
- **Different duration:** Weight loss after 48 weeks is not the same as after 80 weeks; many curves flatten out only late.
- **Different analysis:** Some studies analyze all participants, even if they discontinued treatment (the so-called treatment-policy estimand). Others calculate as though everyone had taken the drug as planned. The second approach yields more favorable figures.
- **Different placebo groups:** On placebo, participants lost anywhere from nothing to more than 5% of their weight depending on the study, depending on how intensive the accompanying diet and exercise counseling was.
- **Different phases:** Results from small phase 2 trials are often more favorable than those of the later large phase 3 trials.

A comparison is robust only when two agents were randomly assigned in the *same* study. Such head-to-head comparisons are compiled at the end of this chapter. The remaining tables show orders of magnitude, not a ranking.

- **Liraglutide** is the only agent for which the approval trial reports loss in kilograms as the main result; with a starting weight of 106.2 kg, this corresponds roughly to 8%. In the head-to-head trial STEP 8 it was −6.4% (see below).

A tiered pattern is striking: pure GLP-1 agonists achieve on average about 8 to 15%, the higher semaglutide dose just under 19%, combinations with GIP or amylin around 20%, and the triple agonist retatrutide 25%. This order is supported by the head-to-head trials SURMOUNT-5, STEP 8, and REDEFINE 5; for retatrutide, the direct comparison with tirzepatide is still pending (NCT06662383).

#### After stopping

Two studies have specifically examined what happens after stopping. In STEP 4, all participants first received semaglutide for 20 weeks; afterward, the group that switched to placebo regained 6.9% over 48 weeks, while the group continuing semaglutide lost a further 7.9% (Rubino 2021, PMID 33755728). In SURMOUNT-4, after 36 weeks of tirzepatide, the placebo group regained 14.0% over the following 52 weeks (Aronne 2024, PMID 38078870). For both agents: the effect lasts only as long as treatment continues. For the newer agents, no published data on this are yet available.

### Blood sugar: HbA1c

The HbA1c value ("long-term blood sugar") indicates what proportion of the red blood pigment (hemoglobin) is bound to sugar and reflects the average blood sugar over the past two to three months. A reduction of one percentage point is considered clinically meaningful. How much the value falls depends strongly on the starting value: someone starting at 9% can lose more than someone starting at 7.5%.

The older agents exenatide and pramlintide lower HbA1c by less than one percentage point, the weekly GLP-1 agonists by about 1.5 points, and tirzepatide and the newer multi-agonists by around 2 points. For blood sugar, the differences among the newer agents are smaller than for weight. REIMAGINE 2 shows this particularly clearly: there, CagriSema lowered HbA1c by only 0.16 percentage points more than semaglutide 2.4 mg alone (−1.91 vs. −1.75; Buse 2026, PMID 42251859). The addition of amylin therefore acts mainly on weight.

An important difference concerns hypoglycemia (low blood sugar). Because GLP-1 enhances insulin release only when blood sugar is elevated, hypoglycemia is rare with these drugs alone. It becomes more frequent when insulin or sulfonylureas are taken at the same time. With pramlintide, which is used only in addition to mealtime insulin, severe hypoglycemia is the most important risk (Edelman 2006, PMID 17003291).

### Cardiovascular outcome trials

Outcome trials do not measure laboratory values but events that matter to patients: cardiovascular death, heart attack, and stroke, combined as "MACE" (major adverse cardiovascular events). The hazard ratio (HR) indicates how high the risk was compared with the control group; 0.80 means a 20% lower relative risk.

| Study (agent) | Participants | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| LEADER (liraglutide; Marso 2016), PMID 27295427 | 9,340 with type 2 diabetes and high risk | median 3.8 years | 13.0% vs. 14.9%; HR 0.87 |
| SUSTAIN 6 (semaglutide; Marso 2016), PMID 27633186 | 3,297 with type 2 diabetes and high risk | 104 weeks | 6.6% vs. 8.9%; HR 0.74 |
| PIONEER 6 (semaglutide tablet; Husain 2019), PMID 31185157 | 3,183 with type 2 diabetes and high risk | median 15.9 months | 3.8% vs. 4.8%; HR 0.79, only non-inferiority shown |
| SOUL (semaglutide tablet; McGuire 2025), PMID 40162642 | 9,650 with type 2 diabetes and vascular or kidney disease | median 49.5 months | 12.0% vs. 13.8%; HR 0.86 |
| SELECT (semaglutide 2.4 mg; Lincoff 2023), PMID 37952131 | 17,604 with overweight and cardiovascular disease, without diabetes | mean 39.8 months | 6.5% vs. 8.0%; HR 0.80 |
| REWIND (dulaglutide; Gerstein 2019), PMID 31189511 | 9,901 with type 2 diabetes, with or without prior cardiovascular disease | median 5.4 years | 12.0% vs. 13.4%; HR 0.88 |
| EXSCEL (exenatide weekly; Holman 2017), PMID 28910237 | 14,752 with type 2 diabetes | median 3.2 years | 11.4% vs. 12.2%; HR 0.91, superiority narrowly missed |
| SURPASS-CVOT (tirzepatide vs. dulaglutide; Nicholls 2025), PMID 41406444 | 13,165 with type 2 diabetes and vascular disease | several years | 12.2% vs. 13.1%; HR 0.92, non-inferior |
| ACHIEVE-4 (orforglipron vs. insulin glargine; Klein 2026), PMID 42815506 | 2,749 with type 2 diabetes and high risk | median 2 years | 4.2% vs. 5.0% (MACE-4), non-inferior |

What this table shows:

- **The class lowers risk.** A meta-analysis of seven placebo-controlled outcome trials with a combined 56,004 participants found, for GLP-1 receptor agonists in type 2 diabetes, 12% fewer major cardiovascular events (HR 0.88), 12% fewer deaths from any cause, and 9% fewer hospital admissions for heart failure, with no meaningful differences between subgroups (Kristensen 2019, PMID 31422062). This meta-analysis also included two agents that are not covered in this book.
- **Differences between agents are uncertain.** Exenatide was at the weaker end in EXSCEL; the molecule itself, the high discontinuation rate, and the pragmatic study design are discussed as explanations. An exenatide pump implant (FREEDOM-CVO) met only the safety criterion (HR 1.21; Ruff 2022, PMID 34873344).
- **SELECT is a special case.** In this overview it is the only large outcome trial conducted exclusively in people *without* diabetes. In absolute terms, over a little more than three years, about 1.5 in every hundred treated people had one fewer major event.
- **Tirzepatide was tested against an effective drug.** SURPASS-CVOT did not compare tirzepatide with placebo but with dulaglutide, which had itself lowered risk in REWIND. Tirzepatide was non-inferior; formal superiority was narrowly missed. How large the benefit would be compared with no treatment can only be estimated from this through modeling.
- **Heart failure:** In heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity, tirzepatide reduced the combined endpoint of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure in SUMMIT with 731 people (9.9% vs. 15.3%; HR 0.62; Packer 2025, PMID 39555826). The difference was based on fewer worsening events; deaths were rare. In STEP-HFpEF, semaglutide improved symptoms and walking distance (Kosiborod 2023, PMID 37622681), but was not designed for hard endpoints.

**Still pending (registered studies):** Outcome data are lacking for the newer agents. Ongoing studies are SURMOUNT-MMO on tirzepatide in obesity without diabetes (NCT05556512), TRIUMPH-Outcomes on retatrutide (NCT06383390), REDEFINE 3 on CagriSema (NCT05669755), a study on maridebart cafraglutide (NCT07037433), and one on orforglipron (NCT07241390). Until these results are available, it remains open whether greater weight loss also leads to fewer heart attacks and strokes. That is a plausible expectation, but not a result.

### Kidney and liver

**Kidney:** Among the studies discussed in this book, only FLOW has a kidney event as its primary endpoint. In 3,533 people with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, semaglutide 1.0 mg reduced major kidney events and death from kidney or cardiovascular causes by 24% (331 vs. 410 events; HR 0.76). The study was stopped early for efficacy; all-cause mortality was 20% lower (Perkovic 2024, PMID 38785209). In the older outcome trials, by contrast, the kidney benefit was based mainly on less protein in the urine: in LEADER HR 0.78 (Mann 2017, PMID 28854085), in REWIND HR 0.85 (Gerstein 2019, PMID 31189509), across all seven trials HR 0.83 (Kristensen 2019, PMID 31422062). In SOUL, the confirmatory secondary endpoints, including kidney events, did not differ significantly (McGuire 2025, PMID 40162642). In moderate to severe kidney disease, kidney function was better preserved with dulaglutide than with insulin glargine (AWARD-7, Tuttle 2018, PMID 29910024). For tirzepatide and the newer agents, there are as yet no studies with kidney events as the primary endpoint.

**Liver:** For the inflammatory fatty liver disease MASH (formerly NASH), there are tissue biopsy studies for three agents:

| Agent | Study | Duration | Resolution of MASH without worsening of fibrosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide 2.4 mg | ESSENCE, phase 3 (Sanyal 2025), PMID 40305708 | 72 weeks (interim analysis, 800 people) | 62.9% vs. 34.3%; less fibrosis 36.8% vs. 22.4% |
| Tirzepatide | SYNERGY-NASH, phase 2 (Loomba 2024), PMID 38856224 | 52 weeks, 190 people | 44–62% vs. 10%; less fibrosis 51–55% vs. 30% |
| Survodutide | Phase 2 (Sanyal 2024), PMID 38847460 | 48 weeks, 293 people | MASH improvement 43–62% vs. 14% |

Only ESSENCE is a phase 3 trial; whether the treatment also prevents cirrhosis, liver failure, and deaths is to be shown by its second part over 240 weeks. For survodutide, the phase 3 trials LIVERAGE (NCT06632444) and LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis (NCT06632457) are ongoing. The higher percentages of the phase 2 trials are not directly comparable with ESSENCE because of the small groups and shorter duration.

### Side effects and discontinuation rates

The side effect profile is similar across the whole class: nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, and constipation, especially during gradual dose escalation, usually mild to moderate and transient. They are the most common reason for stopping treatment. The rates depend strongly on dose and the pace of escalation and are only roughly comparable between studies.

|---|---|---|---|
| Exenatide twice daily | Heine 2005, PMID 16230722 | nausea 57.1% | not reported in abstract |
| Dulaglutide | REWIND (Gerstein 2019), PMID 31189511 | 47.4% vs. 34.1% | not reported in abstract |
| Semaglutide 2.4 mg | STEP 1 (Wilding 2021), PMID 33567185 | most commonly nausea and diarrhea | due to gastrointestinal complaints 4.5% vs. 0.8% |

| Tirzepatide | SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff 2022), PMID 35658024 | most common, mostly during escalation | 4.3–7.1% vs. 2.6% |
| CagriSema | REDEFINE 1 (Garvey 2025), PMID 40544433 | 79.6% vs. 39.9% | not reported in abstract |

Some observations:

- **Weekly formulations are usually better tolerated than older forms.** In DURATION-6, 9% had nausea on weekly exenatide versus 21% on daily liraglutide (Buse 2013, PMID 23141817). In STEP 8, 27.6% stopped liraglutide early, for whatever reason, compared with 13.5% on semaglutide; gastrointestinal complaints were similarly frequent in both groups (82.7% vs. 84.1%) (Rubino 2022, PMID 35015037).
- **Greater efficacy does not necessarily come with more complaints.** In SURPASS-2, nausea (17–22% vs. 18%) and vomiting (6–10% vs. 8%) were similarly frequent with tirzepatide and semaglutide; serious adverse events occurred in 5–7% with tirzepatide versus 3% (Frías 2021, PMID 34170647). In REDEFINE 5, 53% on CagriSema and 51% on semaglutide had gastrointestinal complaints (Yamauchi 2026, PMID 42009015).
- **With the multi-agonists, discontinuations rise at high doses.** With retatrutide, abnormal skin sensations (dysesthesia) also occurred in 4–7% versus 1% on placebo – a new signal for this class. In a US phase 2 trial of mazdutide, 20% at the highest dose discontinued due to side effects (Hsia 2026, PMID 42628555).
- **Long-term studies show higher discontinuation rates than short studies.** The 16.6% in SELECT over more than three years is a more realistic guide for long-term treatment than the values from 68- or 72-week approval trials.

**Class risks that affect all agents:**

- **Gallbladder:** A meta-analysis of 76 randomized trials with 103,371 participants found a 37% increased risk of gallbladder and biliary disease, and in weight-loss trials a more than twofold risk (relative risk 2.29; He 2022, PMID 35344001).
- **Pancreas:** In the meta-analysis of the outcome trials, no increased risk of pancreatitis or pancreatic cancer (Kristensen 2019, PMID 31422062); in insurance data, a signal with use for weight loss (Sodhi 2023, PMID 37796527).
- **Thyroid:** C-cell tumors in rodents; in humans, a signal in meta-analyses and observational studies (Silverii 2025, PMID 40437949) whose significance is disputed.
- **Eyes:** Worsening of existing diabetic retinopathy in SUSTAIN 6 (HR 1.76; Marso 2016, PMID 27633186); a rare signal for a circulatory disorder of the optic nerve (NAION) in observational studies (Simonsen 2025, PMID 40098249).
- **Muscle mass:** About a quarter of the weight lost is fat-free mass, in the same proportion as with other weight loss (Look 2025, PMID 39996356).

**What the head-to-head trials show:**

- **For weight, the ranking is clear.** Semaglutide 2.4 mg outperformed liraglutide 3.0 mg by 9.4 percentage points (STEP 8), tirzepatide outperformed semaglutide 2.4 mg by 6.5 percentage points (SURMOUNT-5), and CagriSema outperformed semaglutide 2.4 mg in East Asia, likewise by 6.5 percentage points (REDEFINE 5). In SURMOUNT-5, more participants on tirzepatide reached each of the thresholds of 10, 15, 20, and 25%, and waist circumference decreased by 18.4 cm versus 13.0 cm.
- **For blood sugar, the gaps are smaller.** Between the weekly GLP-1 agonists there are usually only tenths of a point; tirzepatide lowered HbA1c in SURPASS-2 by 0.15 to 0.45 percentage points more than semaglutide 1 mg.
- **For hard endpoints, there has so far been only one head-to-head comparison.** SURPASS-CVOT showed that tirzepatide is non-inferior to dulaglutide; superiority was not demonstrated.
- **The limitations are considerable.** SURMOUNT-5 and SURPASS-2 were open-label and funded by the manufacturer of tirzepatide; SURPASS-2 used semaglutide only at the 1 mg dose. REDEFINE 5 was conducted exclusively in Japan and Taiwan. The differences in SURMOUNT-5, however, are so large that they can hardly be explained by these weaknesses alone.

**Still pending (registered studies):** The direct comparison of retatrutide with tirzepatide in 800 adults with obesity (NCT06662383) is scheduled for completion at the end of 2026. The comparison trial of CagriSema versus tirzepatide in obesity (REDEFINE 4, NCT06131437) is completed according to the registry; a peer-reviewed publication was not available at the editorial deadline. Mazdutide is being tested against semaglutide in China (NCT06184568, NCT06884293).

### Conclusion

The incretin drugs form a clear tiered pattern: for weight loss, the mean values in the approval trials range from around 8% (liraglutide) through just under 15% (semaglutide 2.4 mg) and around 20% (tirzepatide, CagriSema) to 25% (retatrutide), with the head-to-head trials confirming this order for the pairs compared so far. For the heart and kidneys, benefit has so far been demonstrated in placebo-controlled trials only for the older GLP-1 agonists and semaglutide; whether the more potent successors achieve more here remains open. Side effects are similar across the whole class and mainly affect the gastrointestinal tract; discontinuation rates rise with dose and treatment duration. Cross-trial comparisons between individual studies remain a rough guide – for choosing a drug in an individual case, the prescribing information and medical advice are what count.
