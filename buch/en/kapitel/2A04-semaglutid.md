## Semaglutide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** Modified human GLP-1 of 31 amino acids. Compared with the body's own hormone, two amino acids have been exchanged; at a third position, a fatty acid chain is attached via a linker, which binds the molecule strongly to the blood protein albumin (Lau 2015, PMID 26308095).
- **Origin:** developed by Novo Nordisk as a further development of liraglutide.
- **Mechanism of action:** GLP-1 receptor agonist with a very long duration of action – one injection per week or one tablet per day.
- **Products:** Ozempic (injection, type 2 diabetes), Wegovy (injection, weight management and cardiovascular risk reduction), Rybelsus (tablet, type 2 diabetes). In the USA, a Wegovy tablet for weight management has also been approved since late 2025 (please check for current information).
- **EU approval:** yes – type 2 diabetes (injection since 2018, tablet since 2020), weight management (since 2022, also for adolescents aged 12 and older).
- **US approval:** yes – type 2 diabetes, weight management, reduction of cardiovascular risk in overweight with cardiovascular disease, kidney protection in type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, and fatty liver inflammation (MASH) with fibrosis (status as of 2026; please check for current information).
- **WADA:** not listed (status as of 2026; please check for current information).
- **Evidence level:** **A** – four large approval programs (SUSTAIN, PIONEER, STEP, OASIS), several outcomes trials with a combined total of more than 40,000 participants.

### How it is supposed to work

Semaglutide acts like the gut hormone GLP-1: it enhances glucose-dependent insulin release, inhibits glucagon, which raises blood sugar, slows gastric emptying, and dampens hunger and food cravings in the brain. The basic mechanism of the class is explained in the chapter Exenatide.

What is new about semaglutide is its durability. The developers started from liraglutide and changed two things (Lau 2015, PMID 26308095): an amino acid exchange at position 8 makes the molecule completely insensitive to the degrading enzyme DPP-4. And a longer fatty acid chain with a different linker binds much more tightly to albumin. As a result, semaglutide is released and excreted only slowly. In minipigs, the half-life after intravenous administration was 46.1 hours; in humans it is even longer and allows one injection per week.

For weight loss, effects in the brain are decisive above all. Semaglutide reduces food intake, feelings of hunger, and the desire for particularly fatty and sugary foods. A meaningfully higher energy expenditure has not been demonstrated.

**The tablet:** Peptides are normally digested in the stomach and hardly absorbed. For Rybelsus, semaglutide is combined with the excipient SNAC. This buffers stomach acid directly at the tablet surface, thereby protecting against breakdown, and briefly facilitates absorption through the gastric mucosa – in studies in humans and dogs exclusively in the stomach, not in the intestine (Buckley 2018, PMID 30429357). Even so, only a very small fraction of the drug reaches the blood. The tablet therefore contains many times the injection dose and must be taken on an empty stomach with a little water; the prescribing information sets out the details.

### Human studies

Semaglutide is currently the most extensively studied drug in its class. The PubMed query evaluated for this book (search term "semaglutide" in title or abstract, query date October 2026) yielded 342 human studies. This number underestimates the actual volume of research, because many analyses of large trials are published under the trial's name. ClinicalTrials.gov lists 793 registry entries – more than for any other peptide in this book.

The studies can be divided into four programs: SUSTAIN (injection in type 2 diabetes), PIONEER (tablet in type 2 diabetes), STEP (injection in obesity), and OASIS (tablet in obesity). In addition, there are large outcomes trials on the heart (SUSTAIN 6, PIONEER 6, SOUL, SELECT), kidneys (FLOW), liver (ESSENCE), heart failure, joints, and the brain (evoke).

#### Type 2 Diabetes with the Injection: SUSTAIN

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| SUSTAIN 1 (Sorli 2017), PMID 28110911 | 388 people, monotherapy vs. placebo | 30 weeks | HbA1c −1.55 (1.0 mg) vs. −0.02 percentage points; weight −4.53 vs. −0.98 kg |
| SUSTAIN FORTE (Frías 2021), PMID 34293304 | 961 people; 2.0 mg vs. 1.0 mg | 40 weeks | HbA1c −2.2 vs. −1.9; weight −6.9 vs. −6.0 kg |
| SUSTAIN 6 (Marso 2016), PMID 27633186 | 3,297 people with high cardiovascular risk, vs. placebo | 104 weeks | Major cardiovascular events 6.6% vs. 8.9%; HR 0.74 |

The monotherapy trial SUSTAIN 1 (Sorli 2017, PMID 28110911) already showed that semaglutide lowers long-term blood sugar (HbA1c) by about 1.5 percentage points. In later trials with participants who had higher baseline values, the reduction was greater; with the 2.0 mg dose it reached 2.2 percentage points in SUSTAIN FORTE (Frías 2021, PMID 34293304).

**SUSTAIN 6** (Marso 2016, PMID 27633186) was designed as a safety trial but showed a clear advantage: death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or stroke occurred in 108 of 1,648 people on semaglutide (6.6%) versus 146 of 1,649 on placebo (8.9%). Strokes were significantly less frequent (1.6% vs. 2.7%). At the same time, a warning signal emerged: complications of diabetic retinal disease (retinopathy) – such as vitreous hemorrhage or findings requiring treatment – were more frequent on semaglutide (HR 1.76). This signal is discussed in the section on risks.

#### Head-to-Head Comparisons Within the GLP-1 Class

Semaglutide has competed in direct comparison trials against all older GLP-1 receptor agonists and has outperformed each of them.

| Study | Design | Duration | Result (semaglutide vs. comparator) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SUSTAIN 3 (Ahmann 2018), PMID 29246950 | vs. exenatide weekly, open-label | 56 weeks | HbA1c −1.5 vs. −0.9; weight −5.6 vs. −1.9 kg |
| SUSTAIN 7 (Pratley 2018), PMID 29397376 | 1,201 people; 0.5/1.0 mg vs. dulaglutide 0.75/1.5 mg | 40 weeks | HbA1c −1.8 vs. −1.4; weight −6.5 vs. −3.0 kg (high doses) |
| SUSTAIN 10 (Capehorn 2020), PMID 31539622 | 1.0 mg vs. liraglutide 1.2 mg | 30 weeks | HbA1c −1.7 vs. −1.0; weight −5.8 vs. −1.9 kg |
| PIONEER 4 (Pratley 2019), PMID 31186120 | 711 people; tablet 14 mg vs. liraglutide injection 1.8 mg vs. placebo | 52 weeks (main analysis after 26 weeks) | HbA1c −1.2 vs. −1.1 (noninferior); weight −4.4 vs. −3.1 kg |
| STEP 8 (Rubino 2022), PMID 35015037 | 338 people with obesity without diabetes; 2.4 mg vs. liraglutide 3.0 mg | 68 weeks | Weight −15.8% vs. −6.4% |

The differences in long-term blood sugar range from 0.1 to 0.7 percentage points in favor of semaglutide, and in weight from 1.2 to about 4 kg in diabetes. The gap was greatest in obesity: in STEP 8 (Rubino 2022, PMID 35015037), 38.5% on semaglutide lost at least 20% of their weight, compared with 6.0% on liraglutide. In some comparisons, the price is a somewhat higher rate of gastrointestinal complaints: in SUSTAIN 10, 11.4% versus 6.6% discontinued treatment because of side effects (Capehorn 2020, PMID 31539622). In SUSTAIN 3, on the other hand, injection-site reactions were far more frequent on exenatide (22.0% vs. 1.2%) (Ahmann 2018, PMID 29246950).

PIONEER 4 (Pratley 2019, PMID 31186120) is noteworthy because a tablet competed against an injection: for HbA1c, the semaglutide tablet was not worse than the liraglutide injection, and for weight it was superior by 1.2 kg.

Outside the GLP-1 class, semaglutide has itself been surpassed: in a direct comparison with the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist tirzepatide in 751 people with obesity, weight loss after 72 weeks was −13.7% versus −20.2% (Aronne 2025, PMID 40353578). Details can be found in the chapter Tirzepatide.

#### Type 2 Diabetes with the Tablet: PIONEER and SOUL

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| PIONEER 1 (Aroda 2019), PMID 31186300 | 703 people, monotherapy; 3/7/14 mg vs. placebo | 26 weeks | HbA1c −1.1 percentage points (14 mg) vs. placebo; weight −2.3 kg vs. placebo |
| PIONEER 6 (Husain 2019), PMID 31185157 | 3,183 people at high risk; vs. placebo | median 15.9 months | Events 3.8% vs. 4.8%; HR 0.79, noninferior |
| PIONEER PLUS (Aroda 2023), PMID 37385279 | 1,606 people; 25 and 50 mg vs. 14 mg | 52 weeks | HbA1c −2.0 (50 mg) vs. −1.5 (14 mg) |
| SOUL (McGuire 2025), PMID 40162642 | 9,650 people with vascular and/or kidney disease; vs. placebo | median 49.5 months | Events 12.0% vs. 13.8%; HR 0.86 (0.77–0.96) |

At the standard dose of 14 mg, the tablet is somewhat weaker than the injection. PIONEER PLUS (Aroda 2023, PMID 37385279) showed that higher tablet doses narrow the gap. PIONEER 6 (Husain 2019, PMID 31185157) was a short safety trial; it proved that the tablet does not increase cardiovascular risk but was too small to demonstrate a benefit.

This was provided by **SOUL** (McGuire 2025, PMID 40162642): in 9,650 people with type 2 diabetes and vascular or kidney disease, the semaglutide tablet reduced major cardiovascular events by 14% over about four years. The confirmatory secondary endpoints, including kidney outcomes, did not differ significantly. Serious adverse events were not more frequent on semaglutide (47.9% vs. 50.3%).

#### Obesity with the Injection: STEP

At a dose of 2.4 mg per week, semaglutide, as Wegovy, became the first medication with which people with obesity lost on average about 15% of their body weight in large trials.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| STEP 1 (Wilding 2021), PMID 33567185 | 1,961 adults with obesity without diabetes, RCT vs. placebo | 68 weeks | −14.9% body weight vs. −2.4% |
| STEP 2 (Davies 2021), PMID 33667417 | 1,210 people with overweight and type 2 diabetes | 68 weeks | −9.6% vs. −3.4% |
| STEP 3 (Wadden 2021), PMID 33625476 | 611 people, additional intensive behavioral therapy | 68 weeks | −16.0% vs. −5.7% |
| STEP 4 (Rubino 2021), PMID 33755728 | 803 people after 20 weeks of semaglutide: continued or switched to placebo | 48 weeks | −7.9% (continued) vs. +6.9% (discontinued) |
| STEP 5 (Garvey 2022), PMID 36216945 | 304 people | 104 weeks | −15.2% vs. −2.6% |
| STEP 6 (Kadowaki 2022), PMID 35131037 | 401 people in East Asia | 68 weeks | −13.2% vs. −2.1% |
| STEP UP (Wharton 2025), PMID 40961952 | 1,407 people; 7.2 mg vs. 2.4 mg vs. placebo | 72 weeks | −18.7% vs. −15.6% vs. −3.9% |

In **STEP 1** (Wilding 2021, PMID 33567185), participants lost on average 15.3 kg, compared with 2.6 kg on placebo. Half (50.5%) lost at least 15% of their weight. Because of gastrointestinal complaints, 4.5% discontinued treatment, compared with 0.8% on placebo.

In people with type 2 diabetes, weight loss was smaller (STEP 2, Davies 2021, PMID 33667417) – a pattern that occurs with all drugs in this class. In **STEP 5** (Garvey 2022, PMID 36216945), the effect remained stable over two years.

**What happens after discontinuation?** This question is central to practice. In STEP 4 (Rubino 2021, PMID 33755728), all participants first received 20 weeks of semaglutide and had lost on average 10.6%. Those who then switched to placebo regained 6.9% over the following 48 weeks; those who continued treatment lost a further 7.9%. In the extension of STEP 1 (Wilding 2022, PMID 35441470), one year after discontinuation participants had regained about two thirds of the weight they had lost; blood pressure, blood lipids, and blood sugar also moved back toward baseline values. The authors conclude that obesity is a chronic disease and that treatment is likely to be needed long term.

**Higher dose:** In STEP UP (Wharton 2025, PMID 40961952), the triple dose of 7.2 mg led to 18.7% weight loss. Gastrointestinal complaints were more frequent (70.8% vs. 61.2% on 2.4 mg), and 22.9% reported abnormal skin sensations (dysesthesia), compared with 6.0% on 2.4 mg.

#### Obesity with the Tablet: OASIS

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| OASIS 1 (Knop 2023), PMID 37385278 | 667 people with obesity without diabetes; 50 mg daily vs. placebo | 68 weeks | −15.1% vs. −2.4% |
| OASIS 4 (Wharton 2025), PMID 40934115 | 307 people; 25 mg daily vs. placebo | 64 weeks | −13.6% vs. −2.2% |

In these trials, the tablets achieved values similar to those of the injection in STEP 1 (Knop 2023, PMID 37385278; Wharton 2025, PMID 40934115). Gastrointestinal complaints occurred in 74% to 80%. The 25 mg tablet from OASIS 4 is the basis of the Wegovy tablet approved in the USA. A direct comparison with the injection in a large trial is still lacking.

#### Children and Adolescents

In **STEP TEENS**, with 201 adolescents with obesity, body mass index (BMI) fell by 16.1% after 68 weeks, while it rose by 0.6% on placebo (Weghuber 2022, PMID 36322838). 73% lost at least 5% of their weight, compared with 18% on placebo. Five adolescents (4%) on semaglutide developed gallstones, none on placebo. A study in children aged 6 and older is ongoing (NCT05726227).

#### SELECT: Cardioprotection Even Without Diabetes

SELECT was a milestone because it showed for the first time that a weight-reduction medication reduces heart attacks, strokes, and cardiovascular deaths in people without diabetes.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| SELECT (Lincoff 2023), PMID 37952131 | 17,604 people with overweight or obesity and cardiovascular disease, without diabetes; 2.4 mg vs. placebo | mean follow-up 39.8 months | Major cardiovascular events 6.5% vs. 8.0%; HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90) |

The relative risk reduction was 20% (Lincoff 2023, PMID 37952131). In absolute numbers, about 1.5 in a hundred people treated had one fewer such event over a good three years. The high discontinuation rate was striking: 16.6% permanently stopped treatment because of side effects, compared with 8.2% on placebo. Whether the benefit is due solely to weight loss or also to direct effects on blood vessels and inflammation has not been conclusively resolved.

#### FLOW: Protecting the Kidneys

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| FLOW (Perkovic 2024), PMID 38785209 | 3,533 people with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease; 1.0 mg vs. placebo | median 3.4 years (stopped early for efficacy) | Kidney endpoint 331 vs. 410 events; HR 0.76; all-cause mortality HR 0.80 |

FLOW (Perkovic 2024, PMID 38785209) was the first large trial with a GLP-1 receptor agonist in which a hard kidney endpoint – kidney failure, marked loss of function, or death from kidney or cardiovascular causes – was the primary objective. It was stopped early after an interim analysis because of clear efficacy. The annual loss of kidney function (eGFR) was 1.16 ml/min per 1.73 m² smaller on semaglutide. Cardiovascular events (HR 0.82) and all-cause mortality (HR 0.80) were also reduced.

#### Other Organs: Liver, Heart Failure, Blood Vessels, Joints

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| ESSENCE (Sanyal 2025), PMID 40305708 | 1,197 people with fatty liver inflammation (MASH) and fibrosis stage 2–3; interim analysis of the first 800 | 72 weeks (first part) | Resolution of inflammation 62.9% vs. 34.3%; less fibrosis 36.8% vs. 22.4% |
| STEP-HFpEF (Kosiborod 2023), PMID 37622681 | 529 people with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity; 2.4 mg vs. placebo | 52 weeks | Symptom score +16.6 vs. +8.7 points; walking distance +21.5 vs. +1.2 m |
| STRIDE (Bonaca 2025), PMID 40169145 | 792 people with peripheral artery disease and type 2 diabetes; 1.0 mg vs. placebo | 52 weeks | maximum walking distance improved 13% more |
| STEP 9 (Bliddal 2024), PMID 39476339 | 407 people with obesity and knee osteoarthritis | 68 weeks | Pain score −41.7 vs. −27.5 points; weight −13.7% vs. −3.2% |

**Liver:** In ESSENCE (Sanyal 2025, PMID 40305708), fatty liver inflammation in the tissue sample resolved in almost two thirds of those treated, without any increase in scarring. A reduction in fibrosis – the clinically more important goal – was achieved by 36.8% versus 22.4%. The second part of the trial, with hard liver endpoints such as cirrhosis, is running until 2029 (NCT04822181).

**Heart failure:** In heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity, symptoms and exercise capacity improved markedly (Kosiborod 2023, PMID 37622681). Serious adverse events were less frequent on semaglutide (13.3% vs. 26.7%). Whether semaglutide also reduces hospitalizations or deaths from heart failure was not the primary objective of this trial.

**Peripheral artery disease:** In peripheral arterial occlusive disease, semaglutide increased the maximum walking distance (Bonaca 2025, PMID 40169145).

**Knee osteoarthritis:** In STEP 9 (Bliddal 2024, PMID 39476339), knee pain decreased more than on placebo. Whether this is due solely to weight loss remains open in the study.

#### Brain and Addiction

**Alzheimer's disease:** The hope that GLP-1 receptor agonists could slow cognitive decline was based on animal experiments and observational data. Novo Nordisk tested this in two large phase 3 trials, evoke and evoke+, with a combined total of 3,808 people with early Alzheimer's disease, who received daily semaglutide tablets or placebo for 104 weeks (Cummings 2026, PMID 41865758). The result was clearly negative: the disease progressed equally fast in both groups (difference on the dementia scale CDR-SB −0.08 and +0.10 points, respectively, not significant). As things stand, semaglutide has thus failed as an Alzheimer's drug; a commentary in the same journal discusses the implications for the research field (Schneider 2026, PMID 41865757).

**Alcohol:** In a small randomized trial with 48 people with alcohol use disorder, low-dose semaglutide reduced the amount of alcohol consumed in a laboratory test, the craving for alcohol, and the number of drinks on drinking days; the number of drinking days did not change (Hendershot 2025, PMID 39937469). In a Swedish registry study, semaglutide was associated with a 36% lower risk of alcohol-related hospital admissions (Lähteenvuo 2025, PMID 39535805). Larger randomized trials are ongoing.

### Animal and laboratory studies

Semaglutide was specifically designed on the drawing board: from numerous variants, the developers selected the one with the best combination of albumin binding, receptor activity, and stability (Lau 2015, PMID 26308095). The half-life was first determined in minipigs. Absorption of the tablet through the gastric mucosa was studied in dogs and in humans (Buckley 2018, PMID 30429357).

As with liraglutide, long-term experiments in rodents led to C-cell tumors of the thyroid; transferability to humans is considered questionable because of the low receptor density in human C cells (Bjerre Knudsen 2010, PMID 20203154). Animal experiments on neuroprotection, addiction, and inflammation formed the basis for the human studies described above – with mixed results, as the failure of evoke shows.

### Risks and side effects

**Established from randomized trials:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints:** Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, and constipation affect the majority of those treated in the obesity trials, mostly mild to moderate and transient, especially during dose escalation (Wilding 2021, PMID 33567185). They are the most common reason for discontinuing therapy.
- **Gallstones and cholecystitis:** increased in the class as a whole, especially at high doses and for weight reduction (He 2022, PMID 35344001); in STEP TEENS in 4% of adolescents (Weghuber 2022, PMID 36322838).
- **Worsening of diabetic retinal disease:** more frequent in SUSTAIN 6 (HR 1.76) (Marso 2016, PMID 27633186). It is suspected that a very rapid reduction in blood sugar in existing retinopathy causes temporary harm – an effect also known from insulin. A dedicated trial with 1,500 participants on this question is ongoing (NCT03811561). People with retinopathy should be monitored by an ophthalmologist.
- **Loss of muscle mass:** With any substantial weight loss, fat-free mass is also lost. How significant this is with semaglutide, particularly in older people, is still being investigated; Part IV discusses this.

**Observational data and signals:**

- **NAION (circulatory disorder of the optic nerve):** In 2024, a US study from an eye clinic reported a markedly increased risk of nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, a sudden, usually painless loss of vision in one eye (Hathaway 2024, PMID 38958939). In this study, the hazard ratio was 4.28 (diabetes) and 7.64 (obesity), respectively, albeit with very small case numbers. A Danish-Norwegian registry study of more than 60,000 people with type 2 diabetes confirmed an increased relative risk (HR 2.81), with a low absolute risk: about 1.4 additional cases per 10,000 person-years (Simonsen 2025, PMID 40098249). A 2026 meta-analysis found the signal only in observational studies in diabetes (HR 1.85), not in randomized trials and not in obesity (Liu 2026, PMID 41475544). In 2025, the European Medicines Agency added NAION to the prescribing information as a very rare side effect (status as of 2026; please check for current information). Anyone whose vision suddenly deteriorates while on semaglutide should seek medical help immediately.
- **Pancreatitis and bowel obstruction:** An analysis of insurance data found an increased risk of pancreatitis, bowel obstruction, and gastroparesis with GLP-1 receptor agonists used for weight loss (Sodhi 2023, PMID 37796527). In the large randomized trials, pancreatitis was rare. The prescribing information lists it as a possible side effect.
- **Thyroid cancer:** signal in observational studies and meta-analyses for the class (Bezin 2023, PMID 36356111; Silverii 2025, PMID 40437949); see the chapter Liraglutide.
- **Psychiatric side effects:** Following reports of suicidal thoughts, the authorities conducted reviews in 2023/2024 and found no evidence of a causal relationship. In a large US cohort of 240,618 people with overweight, semaglutide was even associated with fewer new suicidal thoughts than other weight-reduction drugs (Wang 2024, PMID 38182782). Observational data cannot reliably rule out bias.

**Notes for practice:** Semaglutide is not indicated during pregnancy; the prescribing information recommends stopping in good time before a planned pregnancy. Because of delayed gastric emptying, there may be an increased risk during anesthesia that stomach contents enter the lungs; people being treated should mention this before operations and gastroscopies. For use, dose escalation, and contraindications, the prescribing information and medical supervision are authoritative.

### Gray Market, Compounding, and Counterfeits

No other peptide in this book is obtained outside regular channels as often as semaglutide. From 2022 onward, demand exceeded supply for years. Three problems must be distinguished.

**Compounding (USA):** During the official shortages, US pharmacies were allowed to produce semaglutide themselves ("compounding"), often as powder or solution in vials, sometimes with other salt forms of the drug. Since the end of the shortage, the FDA has severely restricted this practice (status as of 2026; please check for current information). A case series from a poison control center described tenfold overdoses because patients were handling syringes and vials instead of prefilled pens and confused milliliters, units, and milligrams (Lambson 2023, PMID 37392810). In an analysis of the US adverse event database, reports of compounding errors, contamination, and hospital admissions were disproportionately frequent for compounded products (McCall 2026, PMID 40285721).

**Illegal online shops:** In a market investigation, 42.3% of the online pharmacy links found led to illegal vendors selling semaglutide without a prescription (Ashraf 2024, PMID 39509151). In test purchases, ordered Ozempic pens never arrived at all. The vials delivered did contain semaglutide, but purity was only 7.7% to 14.37% instead of the stated 99%, and endotoxins (bacterial components that can cause fever and inflammation) were detected in all samples.

**Counterfeits:** Counterfeit Ozempic pens have appeared in Europe and the USA, some filled with insulin or containing no drug at all; medicines regulators and the World Health Organization have issued repeated warnings. General information on gray-market products can be found in Part I and Part IV. Only the regular route is safe: a physician's prescription and dispensing by a pharmacy.

### Legal status

- **EU:** Ozempic (2018), Rybelsus (2020), and Wegovy (2022) are centrally authorized and prescription-only. Wegovy is approved for weight management in adults and adolescents aged 12 and older; the prescribing information now also includes information on cardiovascular risk reduction from SELECT.
- **Germany:** Ozempic and Rybelsus are reimbursed for type 2 diabetes. Wegovy is considered a weight-reduction drug and is in principle excluded from reimbursement by statutory health insurance under Section 34 of Book V of the Social Code (SGB V); a change is being discussed politically (status as of 2026; please check for current information). Prescribing Ozempic purely for weight loss is off-label and was explicitly criticized by the authorities during the shortages.
- **Austria:** prescription-only; coverage of costs for weight management only to a limited extent (please check for current information).
- **USA:** Ozempic, Rybelsus, and Wegovy (injection and tablet) approved, with expanded indications for the heart, kidneys, and MASH.
- **Patents and generics:** In several large markets outside the EU and the USA – including Canada, China, India, and Brazil – patent protection for semaglutide expires in 2026; the first copycat products have been announced or are on the market there. In the EU and the USA, generics are not expected until around 2031/2032 (status as of 2026; please check for current information). More on this in Part V.
- **Sport (WADA):** not listed (status as of 2026; please check for current information).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established (registered studies):** Of the 793 registry entries, a large proportion concern combinations and successors. Examples:

- **Eyes:** A study with 1,500 participants is specifically investigating the effect on diabetic retinopathy (NCT03811561).
- **Liver:** The second part of ESSENCE, with 1,205 participants, is intended to clarify whether semaglutide prevents liver cirrhosis and liver complications (NCT04822181).
- **Children:** A study with 212 children and adolescents is ongoing (NCT05726227).
- **Tablet:** Further phase 3 trials of the tablet in obesity have started (NCT07770841).
- **CagriSema combination:** Semaglutide in fixed combination with the amylin analog cagrilintide is being tested in a cardiovascular trial with 7,101 participants (NCT05669755); details in the chapter Cagrilintide/CagriSema.

**Assessment:** In the coming years, semaglutide will come under pressure from two sides – from more effective successors such as tirzepatide, CagriSema, or retatrutide, and from generics in large markets. At the same time, it is the drug with the broadest evidence base for hard endpoints (heart, kidneys, liver). The tablet form could considerably broaden access. The failed Alzheimer's trials show that not every hope derived from animal experiments and observational data holds up.

### Conclusion

Semaglutide is the most comprehensively studied GLP-1 receptor agonist and has outperformed exenatide, liraglutide, and dulaglutide in direct comparisons. At a dose of 2.4 mg per week, people with obesity in STEP 1 lost on average 14.9% of their weight; after discontinuation, most of the weight returns. Large outcomes trials showed a reduction in major cardiovascular events – even without diabetes (SELECT, 20%) – as well as kidney protection (FLOW) and improvements in fatty liver inflammation. Common side effects affect the gastrointestinal tract and the gallbladder; rare signals such as NAION continue to be monitored, while the Alzheimer's trials showed no benefit. Gray-market products carry considerable risks – treatment belongs in the hands of a physician and the pharmacy.
