## Semax

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic heptapeptide (seven amino acids: Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro). The first four building blocks correspond to a fragment of the stress hormone ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone, segment 4–7), to which the tripeptide Pro-Gly-Pro is attached, which is intended to slow its breakdown.
- **Origin:** developed from the 1980s onward at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences in Moscow.
- **Mechanism of action:** said to stimulate the production of nerve growth factors (especially BDNF) and to protect nerve cells during oxygen deprivation; unlike ACTH, it does not stimulate the adrenal gland to release cortisol. It is administered as nasal drops.
- **EU approval:** no.
- **US approval:** no. From 2023, Semax was on the FDA list of substances with potential safety risks for pharmacy compounding ("Category 2"); it was removed from that list in April 2026, and in July 2026 the advisory committee PCAC narrowly recommended its inclusion in the list of permitted bulk substances for compounding. That is not approval as a medicine.
- **Russia and some neighboring countries:** registered as a medicine (nasal drops; 0.1% solution as a nootropic, 1% solution for stroke).
- **WADA:** not listed by name. Since Semax is approved in Russia, it does not clearly fall under the catch-all category S0 for non-approved substances (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **C** – a few small, mostly nonrandomized studies from Russian centers.

### How it is supposed to work

ACTH is a hormone of the pituitary gland that stimulates the adrenal gland to produce cortisol. As early as the 1970s, it was noticed that short fragments of this hormone – without the effect on the adrenal gland – influence attention and learning in animals. Such fragments, however, are broken down in the body within minutes. The developers of Semax therefore extended the fragment ACTH(4–7) with the sequence Pro-Gly-Pro, which is more stable against protein-cleaving enzymes.

For the mechanism of action, the developers rely mainly on animal data:

- **Nerve growth factors:** In rats, specific binding sites for labeled Semax were found in the basal forebrain; after intranasal administration, the content of BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), a protein that promotes the adaptability of nerve connections, rose there within three hours. This was not the case in the cerebellum (Dolotov 2006, PMID 16635254).
- **Gene activity after stroke:** In a rat stroke model, Semax altered the activity of numerous genes, especially those of the immune system and blood vessel formation (Medvedeva 2014, PMID 24661604).

A dedicated receptor for Semax has not been identified. Whether the BDNF increases measured in rats occur in comparable form in humans and whether they are sufficient for a benefit is open.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded only 4 publications classified as human studies; in total, PubMed lists around 210 papers with "Semax" in the title or abstract, the vast majority of them animal and laboratory studies. Not a single study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. A targeted follow-up search found a few further small clinical papers. Virtually all come from Russian institutions, many from the developers' circle, and have been published only in Russian with an English abstract.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gusev 1997, PMID 11517472 | 30 people with acute ischemic stroke, compared with 80 people on standard therapy, nonrandomized | 5–10 days of treatment | "some influence" on the speed of recovery of neurological deficits, no effect sizes in the abstract |
| Gusev 2005, PMID 15792140 | 187 people with chronic cerebrovascular insufficiency | not stated | according to the authors, clinical improvement and fewer strokes; design and figures not in the abstract |
| Gusev 2018, PMID 29798983 | 110 people after stroke, early or late rehabilitation, each with or without Semax | about 5 months | higher BDNF level in the blood, faster improvement in the Barthel index |

| Polunin 2000, PMID 10741256 | people with optic nerve diseases, three groups including control | not stated | according to the authors, better visual acuity and visual field; no figures in the abstract |

#### Stroke

Stroke is the main indication for which Semax is registered in Russia. The basis for this is thin. In the first clinical study, 30 people with acute stroke received Semax in addition to standard treatment; 80 people who received only standard treatment served as the comparison. The groups were not assigned by randomization. The authors report "some influence" on the regression of deficits, especially paralysis (Gusev 1997, PMID 11517472). A follow-up paper by the same group examined inflammatory messengers in the blood and was explicitly a retrospective analysis (Miasoedova 1999, PMID 10358912).

The largest study to date, with 110 people after a stroke, compared early and late rehabilitation, each with or without Semax. On Semax, the BDNF level in blood plasma rose and remained elevated during the observation period; the Barthel index, a measure of independence in daily life, improved faster (Gusev 2018, PMID 29798983). The abstract mentions neither randomization nor blinding, and the differences between groups are not given as effect sizes. That is not sufficient to prove a benefit.

For comparison: among other agents intended to "protect nerve cells," almost all have so far failed in stroke in large, blinded international trials. No such trial exists for Semax.

#### Other uses

- **Motor neuron diseases:** In an open-label study with 27 people, Semax changed neither the course of nerve damage on electromyography nor the clinical rating scales. Quality-of-life scores improved, especially mood and motivation, most strongly on day ten (Serdiuk 2007, PMID 18379501). Without a placebo group, an expectation effect cannot be ruled out.
- **Optic nerve diseases:** A controlled study reports better visual function with add-on Semax treatment, without figures in the abstract (Polunin 2000, PMID 10741256).
- **Sequelae of oxygen deprivation:** In 73 people with brain damage after oxygen deprivation, the authors describe an effect on memory disorders. In some, episodes of seizure-like activity appeared on the EEG after administration; the authors therefore advise giving the first dose under EEG monitoring (Alekseeva 1999, PMID 10199046).
- **Stomach ulcer:** In a paper on treatment-resistant stomach ulcers, 89.5% of ulcers healed after 14 days with Semax in addition to standard therapy, compared with 30.8% in the control group (Ivanikov 2002, PMID 12459874). The abstract does not state group size or allocation; the authors themselves call for further studies.

#### Healthy people and "nootropic"

On the internet, Semax is offered mainly as an agent to enhance concentration and memory in healthy people. Robust studies on this are lacking. A review by the developers from the 1990s reports that Semax improves memory and attention in healthy men "under extreme conditions" but gives no study data in the abstract (Asmarin 1997, PMID 9173745). Two small imaging studies in healthy people showed altered activity patterns on functional MRI after a single dose (Lebedeva 2018, PMID 30225715; Panikratova 2020, PMID 32342318, 52 healthy people with Semax, Selank or placebo). Altered brain activity in the scanner, however, is not proof of better performance in everyday life.

### Animal and laboratory studies

The preclinical literature is extensive and likewise comes mainly from Moscow:

- In rats with experimentally induced stroke, Semax altered the activity of genes of the immune and vascular systems (Medvedeva 2014, PMID 24661604).
- In rats whose dopamine system had been damaged with the neurotoxin MPTP, Semax reduced movement and anxiety disorders (Levitskaya 2004, PMID 15341218).
- In genetically modified mice with Alzheimer-like deposits, Semax and a derivative improved behavior in memory tests and reduced the number of amyloid deposits (Radchenko 2025, PMID 41479572).
- In laboratory experiments, Semax binds copper ions, which dampened the formation of harmful oxygen radicals by amyloid-copper complexes (Tomasello 2025, PMID 40496623).

These results are animal and laboratory findings. Many agents that were protective in such models later failed in humans.

### Risks and side effects

**Established:** Systematically collected safety data from larger studies do not exist. The Russian papers describe Semax as well tolerated, in some cases explicitly also in older people (Gusev 2005, PMID 15792140). A signal to be taken seriously is the observation of seizure-like EEG changes after administration in individual people with preexisting brain damage (Alekseeva 1999, PMID 10199046).

**Theoretical:** Semax is said to influence growth factors and immune genes; the long-term consequences of such interventions have not been studied. Data are lacking for pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, epilepsy and mental illness.

**Gray market:** Outside Russia, Semax is mostly sold as a "research peptide" or as a self-mixed nasal solution. The content, purity and sterility of such products are unverified. In 2017 and 2018, a medicines control laboratory identified Semax and Selank in seized, unknown preparations and found through an online search that such "nootropic peptides" are freely offered as powder for injection or as nasal spray (Vanhee 2020, PMID 31667971). More on this in Part I and Part IV.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** Semax is not approved. As a substance with an intended effect on bodily functions, it is considered a medicine; sale and advertising without approval are not permitted. Importing it from Russia as a medicine is subject to the rules for individual imports (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **USA:** not approved. In April 2026, the FDA removed Semax from its "Category 2" list; in July 2026, the advisory committee PCAC voted 8 to 5 to add Semax to the list of substances that pharmacies may use for compounding. FDA staff had advised against it; the agency's decision was pending at the time of going to press (as of October 2026, please check for updates).
- **Russia:** registered as a medicine and available in pharmacies.
- **Sport:** not listed by name on the WADA list (as of 2026, please check for updates).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established:** No study with Semax is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (search October 2026). Research in Russia continues to focus on animal models, for example on gene activity after stroke and on Alzheimer's models.

**Assessment:** Without a sufficiently large, blinded, internationally registered trial, Semax is unlikely to achieve approval outside Russia. The discussion in the USA revolves around pharmacy compounding, not around proof of efficacy.

### Conclusion

Semax is a peptide derived from an ACTH fragment that has been used in Russia for decades and increased nerve growth factors in rats. The human studies are small, mostly nonrandomized and come almost exclusively from the developers' circle; in stroke, they report faster recovery and higher BDNF levels, without effect sizes of international standard. For performance enhancement in healthy people, there are no meaningful data. The data are not sufficient to assess a benefit or long-term safety.
