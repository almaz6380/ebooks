## Retatrutide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** synthetic peptide with an attached fatty acid chain for once-weekly injection under the skin; development code LY3437943.
- **Origin:** developed by Eli Lilly as the successor to tirzepatide.
- **Mechanism of action:** "triple agonist" at three receptors: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon. Often called "Triple G" in the media.
- **Approval EU:** no (as of October 2026; Phase 3 program TRIUMPH partly completed).
- **Approval USA:** no (as of October 2026, please check for updates).
- **WADA:** not listed by name; however, as a substance not approved anywhere, retatrutide falls under class S0 "Non-approved substances" and is therefore prohibited in sport at all times (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **B** – several large Phase 3 trials published, but no approval yet and no outcome data on the heart and kidneys. With an approval, level A would be reached.

### How it is supposed to work

Retatrutide builds on the principle of tirzepatide (see there) and adds a third target: the glucagon receptor. At first this sounds contradictory, because glucagon is the "counter-hormone" to insulin – it makes the liver release sugar and raises blood glucose. But glucagon has other effects as well: it increases energy expenditure, promotes fat breakdown in the liver and contributes to satiety. The idea behind retatrutide is to use these favorable effects while the GLP-1 and GIP components offset the glucose-raising effect.

In cell experiments, retatrutide acts with roughly equal strength at the glucagon and GLP-1 receptors and more strongly at the GIP receptor. In obese mice, the glucagon component enhanced weight loss through increased energy expenditure, while GIP and GLP-1 reduced calorie intake (Coskun 2022, PMID 35985340). Whether energy expenditure also rises appreciably in humans has not yet been adequately studied; a corresponding Phase 1 trial is registered (NCT06313528).

In humans, participants in a Phase 2 trial reported less hunger and less tendency toward "loss of control" when eating at higher doses; these changes were associated with weight loss (Kanu 2025, PMID 40916752).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 10 human studies, supplemented by a targeted search for the Phase 1 and Phase 3 publications. On ClinicalTrials.gov, 32 trials were registered (an obviously fictitious sample entry is not counted), including the approval program TRIUMPH (obesity) and TRANSCEND (type 2 diabetes). For a substance without approval, this is an unusually solid body of data – however, it comes almost entirely from manufacturer-funded studies.

#### Early studies

After a single-dose study in healthy people, in which body weight remained reduced for up to 43 days after a single injection (Coskun 2022, PMID 35985340), a twelve-week Phase 1b trial followed with 72 people with type 2 diabetes. The half-life was about six days, which allows weekly dosing. In the highest dose group, weight loss compared with placebo was up to almost 9 kg (Urva 2022, PMID 36354040).

#### Phase 2: obesity and type 2 diabetes

The Phase 2 trial in obesity made headlines in 2023 (Jastreboff 2023, PMID 37366315). 338 adults received various doses or placebo for 48 weeks. In the highest dose group, body weight decreased by an average of 24.2% (placebo: −2.1%); 83% of this group lost at least 15%. These were the highest values measured in an obesity drug trial up to that point. Gastrointestinal side effects increased with dose and were lower with slower dose escalation. Heart rate rose in a dose-dependent manner, peaked after 24 weeks and declined thereafter.

The parallel Phase 2 trial in 281 people with type 2 diabetes showed HbA1c reductions of up to 2.02 percentage points after 24 weeks (dulaglutide: −1.41) and weight loss of up to 16.94% after 36 weeks (Rosenstock 2023, PMID 37385280). In a substudy with body composition measurement (DXA), fat mass fell by 23–26% at the higher doses (Coskun 2025, PMID 40609566).

A substudy in 98 participants with fatty liver (at least 10% liver fat) found a relative reduction in liver fat of up to 82.4% after 24 weeks; at the highest dose, 86% had normal liver fat levels afterward, compared with no one on placebo (Sanyal 2024, PMID 38858523). Liver fat content is a surrogate parameter – whether this reduces liver inflammation, fibrosis and cirrhosis has not yet been shown.

Post hoc analyses of the Phase 2 trials showed improvements in blood lipids and inflammatory markers, for example non-HDL cholesterol up to 26.9% lower and a CRP level 54.8% lower in the obesity trial (Ruotolo 2026, PMID 42608321). These, too, are laboratory values, not hard endpoints.

#### Phase 3: the TRIUMPH and TRANSCEND programs

The approval program for obesity, TRIUMPH, comprises four trials with a combined total of more than 5,800 participants. A special feature is the "basket" design: within the large weight trials, subgroups with knee osteoarthritis or sleep apnea were analyzed with their own primary endpoints (Giblin 2026, PMID 41090431).

**TRIUMPH-1** (Jastreboff 2026, PMID 42814954) is the main trial. In 2,339 adults with obesity without diabetes, body weight decreased by an average of 25.0% after 80 weeks at the highest dose. For comparison: in SURMOUNT-1, tirzepatide at the highest dose achieved 20.9% after 72 weeks. Such a cross-trial comparison between studies of different duration and population is, however, only a rough guide; an ongoing trial is intended to provide the direct comparison (see Outlook).

In the embedded substudies:

- **Knee osteoarthritis** (574 participants): The pain score (WOMAC, scale 1 to 10) fell by 3.5 and 3.6 points on 9 and 12 mg, respectively, compared with 1.9 points on placebo.
- **Sleep apnea** (243 participants): The apnea-hypopnea index decreased by around 32 to 34 events per hour, and by just under 10 on placebo.

**TRIUMPH-2** (Bellido 2026, PMID 42810372) confirmed the familiar pattern that people with type 2 diabetes lose less weight: −18.8% at the highest dose compared with −5.1% on placebo. This trial also provided the most precise safety data to date (see below).

**TRANSCEND-T2D-1** (Bajaj 2026, PMID 42250575) tested retatrutide as the sole diabetes treatment in people with a short duration of diabetes. HbA1c fell by up to 1.94 percentage points and weight by up to 15.3%; no severe hypoglycemia occurred.

According to the registry, TRIUMPH-3 (obesity with cardiovascular disease, NCT05882045) and TRIUMPH-4 (knee osteoarthritis, NCT05931367) have been completed; a peer-reviewed publication was not yet available at the editorial deadline for these chapters.

### Animal and laboratory studies

The preclinical foundations come predominantly from the manufacturer. In cell cultures, retatrutide activates all three receptors; in obese mice it lowered weight and blood glucose, with the glucagon component increasing energy expenditure (Coskun 2022, PMID 35985340). One study in obese mice describes that transplanted pancreatic and lung tumors took hold less often and grew more slowly under retatrutide (Marathe 2025, PMID 40094000) – an animal finding from which nothing can be inferred for humans.

For doping analysis, a detection method was developed in 2026. For this purpose, blood and urine samples were examined from four people who had injected retatrutide themselves outside of trials. No intact retatrutide was found in urine; in blood it was detectable up to about 56 days after the first injection (Carneiro 2026, PMID 42745421). The authors refer to reports of its use among recreational athletes.

### Risks and side effects

**Documented in studies:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints** are the most common side effects and clearly dose-dependent. In TRIUMPH-2, diarrhea occurred in 27–34% (placebo 13%) and nausea in 14–28% (placebo 8%) (Bellido 2026, PMID 42810372).
- **Treatment discontinuations:** In TRIUMPH-2, 12% (9 mg) and 8% (12 mg) discontinued treatment due to side effects or death, compared with 4% on 4 mg and 5% on placebo.
- **Dysesthesia** – unpleasant abnormal skin sensations such as tingling, burning or sensitivity to touch – occurred in TRIUMPH-2 in a dose-dependent manner in 4–7% compared with 1% on placebo. This signal is comparatively new for retatrutide; its cause is unclear.
- **Low blood pressure** (hypotension): 1–6% compared with under 1% on placebo (TRIUMPH-2).
- **Heart rate:** In the Phase 2 trial, pulse rose in a dose-dependent manner and declined again after 24 weeks (Jastreboff 2023, PMID 37366315). A higher resting heart rate is known for all GLP-1 drugs; whether it is clinically significant with the triple agonist is to be clarified by the outcome trials.
- **Loss of lean mass:** as with any substantial weight loss (see Tirzepatide).

**Theoretical or not yet adequately studied:** The glucagon component could in the long term affect amino acid metabolism, heart rhythm and bone; there are no robust long-term data on this. For the class risks of GLP-1 drugs (gallbladder, pancreas, thyroid C-cells in animal studies), there are still too few patient-years for retatrutide to draw separate conclusions.

**Gray market:** Since the Phase 2 publication, retatrutide has been offered online as a "research peptide"; the journal JAMA reported in 2026 on a growing black market for retatrutide and zenagamtide (Rubin 2026, PMID 42853576). These products are not medicines, their content is not tested, and the dose-escalation schedules used in trials cannot be reliably reproduced with them. The accumulation of side effects with rapid dose escalation in the trials shows that this very point is decisive for tolerability. See Part I and Part IV on this.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved. Retatrutide may only be used within clinical trials. Selling it as a "research chemical" for use in humans is impermissible under pharmaceutical law.
- **USA:** not approved (as of October 2026). An expanded access program before approval ("Expanded Access") is entered in the registry (NCT07629401). Whether and when an application for approval will be or has been submitted – please check for updates.
- **Sport:** As a non-approved substance, retatrutide falls under WADA class S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates). A specific detection method in blood has been described.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed (registered trials, as of October 2026):**

- **TRIUMPH-Outcomes** (NCT06383390): outcome trial on cardiovascular and kidney events with around 10,000 participants with obesity, planned completion 2029.
- **Retatrutide vs. tirzepatide** (NCT06662383): direct comparison in 800 adults with obesity, planned study completion December 2026.
- **Retatrutide vs. semaglutide** in type 2 diabetes (NCT06260722).
- **Weight maintenance** after weight loss (NCT06859268), **kidney function** in type 2 diabetes with renal impairment (NCT06297603), **chronic back pain** in obesity (NCT07035093).
- Retatrutide is also part of a Phase 3 master protocol on fatty liver disease (NCT07165028).

**Assessment:** Following the TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 data, applications for approval in the USA and the EU are to be expected; a market launch in 2027 or 2028 appears plausible but is not certain. Whether additional weight loss compared with tirzepatide outweighs the somewhat higher discontinuation rates and the new signal of dysesthesia will be shown by the direct comparison. For many people, moderate, well-tolerated weight loss is more important than a record value.

### Conclusion

Retatrutide is a triple agonist at the GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors and achieved an average weight loss of 25.0% after 80 weeks in the Phase 3 trial TRIUMPH-1 – the highest value measured to date in a large drug trial. Clear effects were also seen in type 2 diabetes, knee osteoarthritis and sleep apnea. Side effects mainly affect the gastrointestinal tract; abnormal skin sensations are new, and discontinuation rates rise at high doses. Data on cardiovascular events and an approval are still pending. Gray-market products labeled "Retatrutide" are not a safe shortcut to this medicine.
