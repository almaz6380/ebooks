## Epitalon (Epithalon)

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic tetrapeptide of four amino acids: Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (abbreviated AEDG). Spellings: Epitalon, Epithalon, Epithalone.
- **Origin:** developed in the 1980s and 1990s at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology under Vladimir Khavinson. The amino acid sequence was derived from the composition of **Epithalamin**, an extract from the pineal gland (epiphysis) of cattle. Epitalon and Epithalamin are chemically different substances.
- **Mechanism of action:** as a "peptide bioregulator," it is said to normalize the function of the pineal gland, promote melatonin production in old age and activate the enzyme telomerase, which lengthens the ends of the chromosomes (telomeres).
- **EU approval:** no.
- **US approval:** no. Removed from FDA "Category 2" in April 2026; in July 2026, the advisory committee PCAC voted 7 to 4 for inclusion in the list of permitted compounding substances, contrary to the recommendation of FDA staff. That is not approval as a medicine (as of October 2026, please check for updates).
- **WADA:** not listed by name; as a substance not approved as a medicine anywhere, Epitalon falls in our assessment under the catch-all category S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **C** – only one small controlled study with the synthetic peptide in humans; the remaining human data concern the extract Epithalamin or cells in the test tube.

### How it is supposed to work

The pineal gland is a small organ in the brain that produces the hormone melatonin at night and thereby helps regulate the day-night rhythm. Melatonin production declines with age. The St. Petersburg group assumed that short peptides from organs can act as "bioregulators" to restore the function of precisely those organs – from the pineal gland for the pineal gland, from the thymus for the immune system (see also the chapter Thymalin in Part II · B).

Several mechanisms are described for Epitalon, almost all from laboratory and animal experiments by the developer group:

- **Telomerase and telomeres:** Telomeres are protective end pieces of the chromosomes that become slightly shorter with each cell division. The enzyme telomerase can lengthen them again; in most body cells it is switched off. In cultures of human fibroblasts (connective tissue cells) from fetuses, Epitalon reportedly switched on telomerase and lengthened the telomeres (Khavinson 2003, PMID 12937682).
- **Chromatin and genes:** In lymphocytes from people aged 76 to 80, Epitalon reportedly loosened "condensed" genetic material again in the test tube and activated silenced genes (Khavinson 2003, PMID 14647006). The group postulates that short peptides bind directly to DNA.
- **Melatonin:** In cell cultures and in animals, Epitalon influenced melatonin production.

A 2025 review summarizes that, despite 25 years of research, it remains unclear whether these mechanisms are the only ones, and that there are only a few fundamental physicochemical and structural studies of the peptide (Araj 2025, PMID 40141333).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded only **one** publication on Epitalon classified as a human study; in total, PubMed lists around 120 papers for the search terms, the vast majority animal and cell studies from St. Petersburg. One study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (see Outlook). A recent independent review reaches the same conclusion: human data on synthetic Epitalon are limited to small physiological or biomarker studies and an older controlled study in retinitis pigmentosa; no sufficiently large randomized trial has demonstrated therapeutic efficacy (Jain 2026, PMID 42884164).

#### The one study with Epitalon

In the only clinical study with synthetic Epitalon, people with retinitis pigmentosa were treated, a hereditary disease in which the retina gradually dies. The abstract reports a "positive clinical effect in 90% of cases" but states neither the number of participants nor the criterion for "positive" or the results of the control group (Khavinson 2002, PMID 12195242). The finding has not been independently confirmed; Epitalon plays no role in the treatment of retinitis pigmentosa.

#### The longevity study with Epithalamin

The most-cited human study on the internet – "pineal peptides extend human life" – concerns not Epitalon but the animal extract Epithalamin. Researchers from St. Petersburg and Kyiv followed 266 older people for six to eight years; in the first two to three years, parts of the group received Thymalin, Epithalamin or both. According to the abstract, mortality fell 1.6- to 1.8-fold on Epithalamin, 2.5-fold on the combination, and with annual combination treatment over six years even 4.1-fold (Khavinson 2003, PMID 14523363).

Such effects would be extraordinary – greater than for almost any measure of modern medicine. All the more important are the limitations:

- The abstract describes neither randomization nor blinding. It is not apparent how the control group was selected.
- The group sizes of the individual arms and absolute numbers of deaths are not stated.
- The study was never registered, and there is no independent replication.
- The results come from the developers of the preparations.

In nonrandomized observations, even small differences between groups – such as who was healthier or better cared for – can produce large apparent effects on mortality. In any case, nothing can be inferred from this study about Epitalon itself, because a different substance was studied.

#### Fact-checking the telomere claim

On the internet, Epitalon is frequently advertised as an agent that "lengthens telomeres" and thereby "turns back the biological clock." What part of this is established?

1. **Cell culture from St. Petersburg:** In human fetal fibroblasts, Epitalon triggered telomerase activity and telomere lengthening (Khavinson 2003, PMID 12937682); in a follow-up paper, treated cells underwent ten additional divisions beyond their usual limit (Khavinson 2004, PMID 15455129). Both papers are published very briefly, without detailed methods in the abstract.
2. **Independent cell study:** In 2025, a research group outside Russia likewise found dose-dependently longer telomeres in normal human epithelial and connective tissue cells. In breast cancer cell lines, the telomeres also lengthened – there via an alternative mechanism (ALT) that is typical of cancer cells (Al-Dulaimi 2025, PMID 40908429).
3. **Human blood cells in the test tube:** In blood cells from eleven men, the telomeres lengthened after contact with the peptide in five cases and shortened in two cases; the authors interpret this as "normalization" (Khavinson 2019, PMID 31761987).
4. **In living humans:** There is no published controlled study showing that Epitalon changes telomere length in human blood or tissue – let alone one demonstrating a health benefit of such a change.

In addition: although telomere length is linked to age, it is not a reliable measure of an individual person's "biological clock." And a persistently activated telomerase is a hallmark of the vast majority of cancers. The ability to lengthen telomeres is therefore not automatically an advantage (see Risks).

### Animal and laboratory studies

- **Lifespan in mice:** In the most important animal study, 54 female mice each received Epitalon or saline monthly throughout life. The **mean lifespan** did **not** differ; only the last 10% of survivors lived 13.3% longer, and the maximum lifespan was 12.3% higher. The total number of spontaneous tumors was the same; leukemias occurred less often (Anisimov 2003, PMID 14501183).
- **Fruit flies:** A longer lifespan was also reported in fruit flies (Khavinson 2000, PMID 11087911).
- **Retina:** In rats with hereditary retinal degeneration, retinal structure was better preserved on Epitalon (Khavinson 2003, PMID 12910293).
- **Oocytes:** In cultures of aged mouse oocytes, Epitalon reduced damage from oxidative stress (Yue 2022, PMID 35413689).

The majority of these studies come from the group around Khavinson and Anisimov, mostly in Russian journals or in journals in which the group itself frequently published. Independent replications are rare.

### Risks and side effects

**Established:** There are no systematically collected safety data on Epitalon in humans.

**Theoretical:**

- **Cancer:** Telomerase activation is a central feature of cancer cells. A substance intended to switch on telomerase could theoretically favor the growth of already existing, undetected cancer cells. In the independent cell study, the telomeres of breast cancer cells also lengthened (Al-Dulaimi 2025, PMID 40908429). The mouse study found no increase in spontaneous tumors (Anisimov 2003, PMID 14501183), but animal data cannot be extrapolated to decades of human use.
- **Hormonal system:** An influence on melatonin and the pineal gland can alter sleep and hormonal rhythms; this has not been studied in humans.

**Gray market:** Epitalon is one of the best-selling "longevity peptides" and is offered as powder for injection or as nasal spray. Purity, content and sterility are unverified. An independent review cites unresolved questions about product quality, immune reactions and cancer risk (Jain 2026, PMID 42884164).

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** not approved; as a substance with a pharmacological effect, Epitalon is considered a medicine, and selling it for use in humans is not permitted (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **USA:** not approved. Following the vote of the advisory committee PCAC in July 2026, Epitalon could in future be permitted for use in pharmacy compounding if the FDA follows it; the process had not been completed at the time of going to press (as of October 2026, please check for updates).
- **Russia:** In Russia, peptide bioregulators are in some cases marketed as dietary supplements; to our knowledge, Epitalon itself is not registered there as a medicine (please check for updates).
- **Sport:** in our assessment WADA category S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established:** An early study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov in which "systemic and local" rejuvenation measures are to be evaluated using so-called aging clocks (epigenetic measurement methods); 48 participants are planned, starting October 2026 (NCT07888595). According to the registry entry, Epitalon is only one of five components of a peptide serum applied to the skin; in addition, medications and dietary supplements are taken. The study has only one group, no blinding and no placebo control. It therefore cannot provide any information about the effect of Epitalon itself.

**Assessment:** Inclusion in the US compounding list would increase its use without improving the evidence. What would matter is an independent, randomized trial that measures at least telomere length, melatonin and safety parameters in humans over a longer period.

### Conclusion

Epitalon is a synthetic tetrapeptide from St. Petersburg that activated telomerase in cell cultures and extended the maximum, but not the mean, lifespan in mice. The frequently cited study on longer life concerned the bovine extract Epithalamin, was not randomized and was never independently replicated. That Epitalon lengthens telomeres or slows aging in humans has not been demonstrated; telomerase activation also raises an unresolved cancer question. The data are not sufficient to assess benefit or safety in humans.
