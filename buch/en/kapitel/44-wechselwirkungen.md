## Interactions and Special Risk Groups

The studies on which the approval of a drug is based almost always exclude certain people: pregnant women, children, very old people or people with multiple conditions, people with eating disorders or severe mental illness. What applies to the study participants can therefore be transferred to these groups only to a limited extent. Added to this are interactions with other drugs and situations such as anesthesia, in which an otherwise harmless effect can suddenly become dangerous.

This chapter brings these questions together. It does not replace medical advice or the prescribing information. For approved drugs, the prescribing information is binding regarding contraindications and interactions; for unapproved peptides, there are as a rule no data at all on these groups.

### Pregnancy and breastfeeding

For almost all peptides in this book: in pregnancy, they have not been studied or are not indicated. The prescribing information for the GLP-1 drugs states that they should not be used during pregnancy and should be stopped in good time before a planned pregnancy (chapters Semaglutide, Tirzepatide). For maridebart cafraglutide this is particularly important, because in an early study its effect persisted for up to 150 days after the last dose (chapter Maridebart Cafraglutide).

Because GLP-1 drugs are used by many women of childbearing age and weight loss can improve fertility, unplanned pregnancies nevertheless occur during treatment. For this situation, data are now available that are predominantly reassuring:

- In a large study from four Nordic countries, the US, and Israel, the rate of major malformations in children of women with type 2 diabetes who had received GLP-1 receptor agonists around conception was not higher than with insulin (adjusted relative risk 0.95) (Cesta 2024, PMID 38079178).
- An analysis from six teratology information services found major malformations in 2.6% of 168 pregnancies with GLP-1 exposure in the first trimester, compared with 2.3% and 3.9% in the comparison groups (Dao 2024, PMID 38663923).
- A US study with 3,572 pregnancies compared women who continued to fill prescriptions for the drug into the first trimester with women who stopped it. No clearly increased risk of miscarriage or stillbirth, growth disorders, or malformations was found; however, the estimates for malformations were so imprecise that a meaningful difference cannot be ruled out either (Brown 2026, PMID 42258827).
- A meta-analysis of seven cohort studies with over 40,000 exposed pregnancies found no statistically significant increase in major malformations; a signal for urinary tract malformations was based only on unadjusted data. The authors rate the certainty of the evidence as low (Uysal 2026, PMID 42420519).

These data help in assessing an *accidental* exposure. They are not evidence that planned use during pregnancy would be safe. For breastfeeding, the data are even thinner; a small study with ten women on the transfer of bremelanotide into breast milk has been completed (NCT06867835).

Some peptides are dangerous in pregnancy because of their mechanism of action. Oxytocin induces labor; use as a nasal spray outside medical supervision is risky during pregnancy (chapter Oxytocin). For gray-market peptides such as BPC-157, TB-500, Epitalon, or the growth hormone secretagogues, there are no data whatsoever on pregnancy and breastfeeding.

### Oral contraception and gastric emptying

GLP-1 drugs, amylin analogs such as pramlintide, and also bremelanotide slow the emptying of the stomach. As a result, tablets taken at the same time can enter the bloodstream later or in different amounts. This mainly affects drugs for which a rapid onset of action or a steady blood level is important.

The contraceptive pill has been studied particularly closely. For semaglutide, a study with 43 women found no reduced absorption of the two hormones of a common combined pill; the level of the progestin was even 20% higher with semaglutide (Kapitza 2015, PMID 25475122). Tirzepatide is different: a review of six studies found a significant reduction in the amount of hormone absorbed only with tirzepatide, not with the other GLP-1 receptor agonists. As an explanation, the authors cite the stronger delay in gastric emptying after the first dose and after dose increases (Skelley 2024, PMID 37940101). The prescribing information for tirzepatide therefore contains a separate note on contraception with tablets; the details are given there and should be discussed with your physician.

Regardless of the agent: severe vomiting or diarrhea – common with GLP-1 drugs – can impair the absorption of any tablet, including the pill.

| Tirzepatide | less hormone absorbed from the pill | note in the prescribing information | Tirzepatide |
| Bremelanotide | lower levels of indomethacin and naltrexone | effect of these drugs may decrease | Bremelanotide |

The interaction of bremelanotide with indomethacin and naltrexone is described in the approval data (Clayton 2022, PMID 35147466). The QT prolongation caused by macimorelin was measured in a thorough ECG study (Lissy 2021, PMID 32961034).

### Anesthesia, endoscopy, and aspiration

Before anesthesia, the stomach should be empty. The reason: if stomach contents enter the airways during anesthesia (aspiration), severe pneumonia can follow. Because GLP-1 drugs slow gastric emptying, the stomach may still contain food residue despite the prescribed fasting period.

The studies on this paint a two-sided picture:

- **More residual stomach content is well established.** A meta-analysis of 23 observational studies with 262,018 people undergoing upper endoscopy found an approximately four-and-a-half-fold increased risk of food residue in the stomach with GLP-1 receptor agonists (odds ratio 4.54) and likewise more frequently aborted examinations. Aspiration pneumonia did not occur more frequently (odds ratio 0.96) (Baig 2025, PMID 39694296).
- In a study using gastric ultrasound before planned surgery, 49% of people on semaglutide had a "full stomach," but only 18% of comparable control persons; solid food residue was found in 42% versus 7%. This was the case even though a dose had been skipped before the operation (Vlaeminck 2026, PMID 41631344).
- **Actual aspirations are rare.** In a large US database study with 296,389 matched pairs of people with type 2 diabetes, respiratory complications after surgery were even less frequent with GLP-1 receptor agonists (0.09% versus 0.34%), and aspirations occurred in 0.01% versus 0.03%. The authors emphasize that it is unclear whether precautions had already been taken in these cases (Wu 2026, PMID 40940281).

Aspiration rates may be low precisely because anesthesia teams are aware of the risk and adjust their approach. Whether pausing the drug before an endoscopy reduces the problem is to be clarified by a large study in China (NCT07276763). For those being treated, this means above all: before any surgery, anesthesia, or endoscopy, state that you are using a GLP-1 drug – even if it comes from a compounding pharmacy or the gray market. How to handle the drug before the procedure is decided by the treatment team according to the applicable recommendations. Anyone who does not regard a gray-market product as a drug and therefore does not mention it deprives the anesthesia team of this opportunity.

### Combination with diabetes drugs and hypoglycemia

As described in the chapter "Side Effects by Organ System," GLP-1 drugs alone hardly cause dangerous hypoglycemia. This changes in combination with insulin or sulfonylureas, which lower blood sugar independently of the current level. In a study of exenatide added to metformin and a sulfonylurea, mild to moderate hypoglycemia occurred in up to 28%, compared with 13% on placebo; with a low-dose sulfonylurea it was less frequent (Kendall 2005, PMID 15855571). With pramlintide, which is used together with insulin, severe hypoglycemia occurred more frequently than with placebo (Edelman 2006, PMID 17003291). The prescribing information therefore provides for reviewing the dose of the other diabetes drugs at the start; implementation belongs in a physician's hands.

For several unapproved peptides, additional lowering of blood sugar is theoretically conceivable, for example for MOTS-c or IGF-1 LR3 (chapters MOTS-c, IGF-1 LR3). This has not been studied. Conversely, the peptides of the growth hormone axis can raise blood sugar and upset an existing diabetes treatment (chapters Ibutamoren, Tesamorelin).

### Children and adolescents

Semaglutide and liraglutide are also approved in the EU and the US for adolescents aged twelve and over with obesity (as of 2026, please check for current status). The approval trials show a side effect profile similar to that in adults:

| Study | Participants | Gastrointestinal complaints | Other findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| STEP TEENS (Weghuber 2022), PMID 36322838 | 201 adolescents, semaglutide, 68 weeks | 62% vs. 42% | gallstones in 4%; serious events 11% vs. 9% |
| SCALE Teens (Kelly 2020), PMID 32233338 | 251 adolescents, liraglutide, 56 weeks | 64.8% vs. 36.5% | discontinuation due to side effects 10.4% vs. 0% |

In the liraglutide study, BMI rose again more after stopping than with placebo, and there was one suicide in the liraglutide group, which the investigators classified as probably not treatment-related (Kelly 2020, PMID 32233338). A meta-analysis of nine randomized and two observational studies in adolescents found no overall increase in suicidal behavior or depression (Silva 2026, PMID 42115753).

The data from US poison control centers paint a different picture. Among children and adolescents between 6 and 17 years of age, reported cases involving GLP-1 drugs rose most sharply between 2017 and 2024. They were hospitalized more often than adults (relative risk 2.66), vomited more often, and their cases were about eight times as often due to intentional misuse (Senthilkumar 2026, PMID 41784916). This suggests that adolescents sometimes use these drugs outside of medical treatment.

For the peptides from the fitness and bodybuilding scene, it is unclear how widespread they are among adolescents. A review points to advertising on social media and indications of growing use also by younger recreational athletes, but emphasizes that studies on prevalence are lacking (Coutinho 2026, PMID 41880199). For adolescents whose growth and hormonal system are not yet complete, intervening in the growth hormone or sex hormone axis with unapproved substances is particularly risky. The approved peptides for children – such as mecasermin for severe IGF-1 deficiency – are used exclusively under specialist supervision (chapter Mecasermin).

### Older people

With age, the risk increases that side effects will be more severe. For older people, four points are particularly important with peptides:

- **Fluid loss.** Vomiting and diarrhea with GLP-1 drugs can lead more quickly to dehydration and kidney problems in older people.
- **Muscle mass.** Someone who already has little muscle copes poorly with further loss (see the following section).
- **Fluid retention and the heart.** Growth hormone led more frequently to tissue swelling, joint pain, and carpal tunnel syndrome in healthy older people (Liu 2007, PMID 17227934). A study of ibutamoren in older people after hip fracture was stopped because of a signal for heart failure (Adunsky 2011, PMID 21067829). So precisely the peptides advertised as "anti-aging" agents tend to place a burden on older hearts.
- **Multiple medications.** Older people often take many drugs at the same time. Delayed gastric emptying can alter their absorption, and combinations with blood sugar–lowering drugs increase the risk of hypoglycemia and falls.

### Muscle loss with substantial weight loss

With any weight loss, fat-free mass, which includes muscles, organs, and water, is lost in addition to fat. With the highly effective GLP-1 drugs, the question is whether this proportion is unusually high and whether it is harmful.

- In a substudy of SURMOUNT-1 with 160 participants, the weight lost with tirzepatide consisted of about 75% fat and 25% fat-free mass – in the same proportion as with placebo, only with a considerably larger total loss. These proportions remained similar in most subgroups, including when separated by age (Look 2025, PMID 39996356).
- A network meta-analysis of 22 studies likewise found that around 25% of weight loss was fat-free mass. Semaglutide and tirzepatide led to the greatest weight loss, but were also among the agents with which the most fat-free mass was lost (Karakasis 2025, PMID 39719170).
- A systematic review of 35 studies arrived at a somewhat higher proportion: at the median, 28.3% of weight loss was accounted for by muscle-related measures, and in 65% of the studies the proportion was above a benchmark of about 25%. Not a single study had objectively measured physical performance (Batsis 2026, PMID 41996180).
- Another meta-analysis emphasizes the other side: because fat decreases more than muscle, the *proportion* of fat-free mass in body weight rose by 1.81 percentage points. The authors recommend accompanying treatment with nutrition and exercise programs (Laverde 2026, PMID 42321502).

In summary: muscle loss with GLP-1 drugs does not appear to be substantially greater relative to total weight loss than with other forms of weight reduction – but in absolute kilograms it is considerable because of the large loss. Whether this has consequences for strength, falls, or independence has not been studied. For older people, people with little muscle mass, and people with frailty, this is an open question. Whether cagrilintide combinations affect muscle loss is currently being studied (chapter Cagrilintide).

### Eating disorders

GLP-1 drugs dampen appetite and the sensation of hunger. For people with anorexia nervosa or bulimia, precisely this effect can be dangerous: it can reinforce restrictive eating behavior and worsen the illness. A case report describes the misuse of semaglutide in atypical anorexia (Guerdjikova 2024, PMID 38329147), and a US cross-sectional study examined how widespread use and misuse of GLP-1 drugs are among people with eating disorders (Peiper 2026, PMID 42340738).

In 2026, a panel of 45 experts and people with lived experience developed recommendations in a consensus process. According to these, before starting GLP-1 treatment, current and past eating disorders should be asked about using a short, validated questionnaire, symptoms should be monitored during treatment, and in active anorexia, atypical anorexia, or bulimia with severe food restriction, these drugs should as a rule not be used. Caution is also warranted with past eating disorders (Keshen 2026, PMID 42742590).

For binge-eating disorder, in which eating binges occur without compensatory behaviors, there are, by contrast, first indications that GLP-1 drugs could reduce binge episodes (Keshen 2026, PMID 42742590). This shows how important an accurate diagnosis is. Via the gray market and via online providers with little medical supervision, precisely this inquiry is lost.

### Competitive sports and doping controls

For athletes who are subject to doping controls, the situation with peptides is particularly delicate. According to the Prohibited List of the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA), roughly the following applies (as of 2026, please check for current status):

- **Prohibited (S2, peptide hormones and growth factors):** all growth hormone secretagogues and IGF-1 variants, thymosin beta-4 and TB-500, ARA-290, and in men gonadorelin and LH releasers such as kisspeptin.
- **Prohibited (S0, non-approved substances):** substances without approval as human medicines, such as BPC-157, Melanotan II, and the GLP-1 multi-agonists still in trials such as retatrutide.
- **Prohibited (S4):** MOTS-c as an activator of the enzyme AMPK.
- **Not prohibited:** the approved GLP-1 drugs. Semaglutide and tirzepatide have, however, been included in WADA's monitoring program in order to detect possible misuse; laboratories have already developed detection methods for blood and dried blood spots (Borschel 2026, PMID 42874322).

The details are given in the respective chapters. Three points are important across all peptides.

**Contaminated products.** An athlete is responsible for every prohibited substance in their body – even if it comes from a mislabeled product. The laboratory analyses in the chapter "Gray-Market Products" show that gray-market goods frequently contain different or additional substances and that prohibited agents also turn up in products presented as dietary supplements (Barrios 2025, PMID 40551438).

**Designer variants.** Modified peptides such as Gly-GHRP-2 or Gly-ipamorelin are presumably intended to evade tests (Gajda 2019, PMID 30136411). Their effect and safety are completely unknown. Experience shows that anti-doping laboratories update their methods; samples are also stored long-term and can be reanalyzed later.

**Questionable benefit.** A review of incretin drugs in sport concludes that clinical studies do not show a consistent improvement in physical performance in humans and that 20 to 30% of weight loss is accounted for by fat-free mass (La Vignera 2026, PMID 42511463). For the regenerative peptides and the growth hormone secretagogues, a sports medicine review concludes that they remain experimental, with an uncertain safety profile, questionable product quality, and far-reaching doping bans (Villegas Meza 2026, PMID 42160466).

### Key points in brief

- In pregnancy, almost all peptides have not been studied or are not indicated. For accidental exposure to GLP-1 drugs, the data are predominantly reassuring, but uncertain.
- Tirzepatide can reduce the absorption of the pill; this was not found with semaglutide. Vomiting and diarrhea impair the absorption of any tablet.
- With GLP-1 drugs, the stomach more often remains full. Before anesthesia and endoscopies, the treatment team must know about this.
- In combination with insulin or sulfonylureas, the risk of hypoglycemia rises.
- Adolescents stand out in poison control data because of intentional misuse; in eating disorders, GLP-1 drugs should as a rule not be used.
- Around a quarter of the weight lost with GLP-1 drugs is fat-free mass; whether this has functional consequences is open.
- For athletes, most peptides are prohibited, and gray-market products additionally carry the risk of undeclared doping agents.
