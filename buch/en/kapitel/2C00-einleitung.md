# Part II · C – The Growth Hormone Axis

Growth hormone (GH) is one of the most mythologized hormones of all. In gyms it is considered a muscle-building and fat-burning hormone, and in anti-aging practices a "fountain of youth." Genuine growth hormone is a large, expensive, prescription-only protein. The peptides in this part promise a seemingly more elegant route: they are meant to get the body to release *more of its own* growth hormone – or they supply the downstream messenger IGF-1 directly.

### The axis in four stations

1. **GHRH** (growth hormone-releasing hormone) from the diencephalon signals the pituitary gland to release growth hormone. Its counterpart somatostatin acts as a brake.
2. **Ghrelin**, a hormone from the stomach, enhances release via its own receptor (GHS-R1a) and at the same time makes you hungry (Kojima 1999, PMID 10604470).
3. **Growth hormone** is released by the pituitary gland in pulses, mainly at night.
4. **IGF-1** (insulin-like growth factor 1) is produced mainly in the liver in response to growth hormone and mediates many of its anabolic effects. High IGF-1 levels in turn put a brake on the axis.

The substances in this part act at different stations: **GHRH analogs** (sermorelin, tesamorelin, CJC-1295) at the GHRH receptor, **ghrelin mimetics** (ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, hexarelin, macimorelin and, for comparison, the non-peptide ibutamoren) at the ghrelin receptor, **IGF-1 and its variants** (mecasermin, IGF-1 LR3) directly at the end of the chain.

### Why this group is so popular

Growth hormone breaks down fat and builds lean mass – this is well documented. A review of randomized trials in young, trained people found an increase in lean mass of 2.1 kg, but no improvement in strength or endurance, and instead more fluid retention and fatigue (Liu 2008, PMID 18347346). In healthy older people, fat mass fell by 2.1 kg and lean mass rose by 2.1 kg; at the same time, soft tissue swelling, joint pain, carpal tunnel syndrome and breast enlargement in men occurred more frequently (Liu 2007, PMID 17227934). Because growth hormone release decreases with age, the idea of "turning it back" suggests itself. The releasers are considered a gentler alternative because the body's own regulation is preserved. In addition, many of them are available cheaply online as "research peptides."

The pattern that runs through almost all chapters: IGF-1 and lean mass rise, but strength, function or disease courses hardly improve in controlled trials – while blood glucose, fluid retention and, with some substances, stress hormones increase.

### The most important open question: cancer

IGF-1 promotes cell division and keeps cells alive. Whether a sustained increase raises the risk of cancer is the central safety question of the group. The evidence, soberly summarized:

- **Naturally high IGF-1:** In observational studies, a higher level is associated with a moderately increased risk of prostate cancer and premenopausal breast cancer (Renehan 2004, PMID 15110491). Genetic analyses with hundreds of thousands of participants suggest that the association could be causal for breast and colorectal cancer (Murphy 2020, PMID 32169310; Murphy 2020, PMID 31884074).
- **Naturally excessive growth hormone (acromegaly):** A meta-analysis of 23 studies found a cancer rate increased by about 50%, but also points to biases in older studies (Dal 2018, PMID 29590449).
- **Lifelong IGF-1 deficiency (Laron syndrome):** Cancer occurred strikingly rarely (Guevara-Aguirre 2011, PMID 21325617).
- **Growth hormone as a medicine:** In a European cohort of 23,984 treated children, there was no generally increased risk in children without pre-existing conditions, but there were abnormalities for individual cancer types and in children who had previously had cancer (Swerdlow 2017, PMID 28187225). In 8,418 treated adults with hypopituitarism, the cancer risk was not increased (Child 2015, PMID 25810462).

Conclusion: in people with a genuine deficiency, replacement treatment is, according to current knowledge, not associated with a markedly higher cancer risk. For healthy people who raise a normal level over years, data are lacking – and the biology argues more for than against some risk. Part IV explores the topic in more depth.

### Reading guide

If you are interested in applications with genuine benefit, start with tesamorelin, macimorelin and mecasermin. If you want to know what lies behind the gray-market classics, read CJC-1295, ipamorelin and IGF-1 LR3. Ibutamoren is not a peptide, but in large trials it shows particularly clearly what sustained stimulation of the axis does. All substances in this group are on the Prohibited List of the World Anti-Doping Agency (group S2; as of 2026, please check for updates).
