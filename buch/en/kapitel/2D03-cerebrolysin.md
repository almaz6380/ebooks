## Cerebrolysin

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** not a single peptide but a mixture of short peptides and free amino acids. It is obtained by enzymatic cleavage of purified proteins from pig brain. The exact composition has not been fully described; the manufacturer standardizes the preparation through its manufacturing process and biological assays.
- **Origin:** manufactured for decades by an Austrian company (today EVER Neuro Pharma, Unterach am Attersee).
- **Mechanism of action:** said to mimic the effects of endogenous nerve growth factors – protecting nerve cells (neuroprotection) and promoting repair and remodeling processes (neuroregeneration). It is administered as an infusion or intravenous injection, usually in courses of 10 to 21 days.
- **EU approval:** no centralized EU approval. Nationally approved in Austria and a number of other, mainly Central and Eastern European, countries (depending on the country, for stroke, traumatic brain injury and dementia). To our knowledge, it is not marketed in Germany (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **US approval:** no.
- **Other countries:** widely used in Russia, other successor states of the Soviet Union, China and other Asian countries.
- **WADA:** not listed (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **B** – numerous randomized, placebo-controlled trials, including a large one with over 1,000 participants; however, the results are inconsistent, and the two major independent Cochrane reviews reach cautious conclusions.

### How it is supposed to work

Nerve growth factors such as BDNF, NGF or CNTF are proteins that keep nerve cells alive, stimulate the sprouting of new connections and promote the formation of new nerve cells in certain brain regions. As medicines they are difficult: they are large, barely cross the blood-brain barrier and act at many sites simultaneously.

Cerebrolysin takes a different approach. Controlled cleavage of pig brain proteins produces a mixture of small fragments. The manufacturer and researchers associated with it assume that some of these fragments trigger signals similar to those of the endogenous growth factors. It is therefore referred to as a "multimodal" agent – that is, one intended to influence many pathways at once.

For readers, this means two things: first, the mechanism is derived mainly from animal and cell experiments. Second, with a mixture it is hard to say which component is responsible for what and how much of it actually reaches the brain.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 88 publications classified as human studies, plus 15 meta-analyses and systematic reviews (including eight versions of Cochrane reviews). On ClinicalTrials.gov, 43 studies were registered, most on stroke, some on traumatic brain injury, dementia, intracerebral hemorrhage, delirium and – during the pandemic – loss of smell after COVID-19. A large proportion of the studies was funded or supported by the manufacturer; many smaller papers come from Russia, Romania, China and Iran.

A word on the statistics used in many Cerebrolysin trials: instead of a single primary endpoint, they often test a combination of several scales using a so-called Mann-Whitney estimator (MW). It gives the probability that a randomly chosen person from the Cerebrolysin group does better than one from the placebo group. An MW of 0.50 means no difference; values around 0.56 are considered a small, around 0.64 a medium and from 0.71 a large advantage. This method is legitimate, but it makes the results harder to compare with other stroke trials, which usually report the proportion of people without lasting disability.

#### Acute ischemic stroke

In ischemic stroke, a clot blocks a brain vessel. Established treatments are dissolving the clot with medication (thrombolysis) and removing it with a catheter (thrombectomy). "Neuroprotective" add-on agents are intended to protect the surrounding tissue – an approach in which dozens of agents worldwide have failed in large trials.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ladurner 2005, PMID 15583955 | 146 people, RCT vs. placebo, start within 24 h | 90 days | no improvement in neurological scale, Barthel index and global impression; one cognitive test better |
| CASTA (Heiss 2012), PMID 22282884 | 1,070 people in Asia, double-blind vs. placebo, start within 12 h | 90 days | primary endpoint neutral; post hoc subgroup of severely affected with trend in favor of Cerebrolysin |
| Lang 2013, PMID 23009193 | 119 people, in addition to thrombolysis, double-blind | 90 days | stopped after interim analysis without benefit in primary endpoint; early improvement on NIHSS more frequent |
| CARS (Muresanu 2016), PMID 26564102 | people in early rehabilitation, double-blind vs. placebo | 90 days | arm function (ARAT) markedly better: MW 0.71 |
| Gharagozli 2017, PMID 29075343 | 100 people, double-blind vs. placebo | 30 days | NIHSS better: MW 0.66 |
| CEREHETIS (Khasanova 2023), PMID 36973684 | 341 people with thrombolysis, open-label randomized | 90 days | fewer symptomatic hemorrhages (OR 0.248); no difference in mRS after 90 days |

**CASTA – the largest trial.** The *Cerebrolysin Acute Stroke Treatment in Asia* trial was published in advance with its study protocol (Hong 2009, PMID 19765131) and is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT00868283). 1,070 people with acute stroke received Cerebrolysin or saline within twelve hours for ten days, each in addition to aspirin. The prespecified primary endpoint – a combination of the modified Rankin Scale (mRS, degree of disability), Barthel index (independence in daily life) and NIHSS (severity of neurological deficits) after 90 days – showed **no difference**. In a post hoc analysis of the more severely affected (NIHSS above 12), mortality after 90 days was 10.5% on Cerebrolysin compared with 20.2% on placebo (Heiss 2012, PMID 22282884). The authors themselves write that this finding would need to be confirmed in a further trial. Such a large confirmatory trial specifically in severe strokes has not been published to date.

**Together with thrombolysis.** In a trial with 119 people, Cerebrolysin was started one hour after thrombolysis. After the third interim analysis, there was no advantage in the primary endpoint (mRS after 90 days), and the trial was stopped. In the first 30 days, more people improved markedly on the NIHSS on Cerebrolysin (Lang 2013, PMID 23009193). This pattern – early improvements on neurological scales but no clear difference in disability after three months – runs through several trials. The CEREHETIS trial found fewer symptomatic hemorrhages after thrombolysis in 341 people but likewise no difference in mRS after 90 days; it was open-label, that is, not blinded (Khasanova 2023, PMID 36973684).

**Thrombectomy.** One study compared 50 people who received Cerebrolysin after catheter treatment with 50 previously treated people (historical controls). After 90 days, 68% versus 44% were functionally independent (mRS 0–2), and secondary brain hemorrhages occurred less often (Staszewski 2025, PMID 40325343). Without randomization and with comparison against earlier patients, however, this is only an indication; the authors themselves call for randomized trials.

#### Rehabilitation after stroke: CARS and follow-up trials

A second approach does not start in the first hours but in early rehabilitation: Cerebrolysin is meant to make the brain "more capable of learning" while physiotherapy takes place at the same time.

The **CARS trial** (*Cerebrolysin and Recovery After Stroke*) treated people 24 to 72 hours after stroke with Cerebrolysin or placebo for 21 days, in addition to a standardized rehabilitation program. The primary endpoint, the Action Research Arm Test (ARAT) for arm function after 90 days, showed a large advantage (MW 0.71; 95% confidence interval 0.63–0.79); across twelve scales, a small to medium advantage (MW 0.62) (Muresanu 2016, PMID 26564102). The authors explicitly describe the trial as exploratory with a relatively small sample size. A prespecified pooled analysis with the identically designed trial CARS-2 (442 people in total) yielded an MW of 0.62 for the ARAT (Guekht 2017, PMID 28707130).

Further smaller studies show a mixed picture:

- A study with 60 people found large advantages on the NIHSS after 10 and 30 days (Stan 2017, PMID 29362596).
- A study with 70 people found **no** difference in motor function overall; only in the subgroup with severe paralysis did Cerebrolysin perform better (Chang 2016, PMID 26934986).
- The Romanian ESCAS pilot study in language disorders (nonfluent aphasia) with 132 people combined Cerebrolysin with speech therapy. After 90 days, language function on the Western Aphasia Battery improved by about 35.6 points compared with 20.8 points on placebo (Homberg 2025, PMID 39957612).

In 2021, a guideline of the European Academy of Neurology and the European Federation of Neurorehabilitation Societies, based on a meta-analysis, recommended Cerebrolysin – alongside the antidepressant citalopram – to support early motor rehabilitation after ischemic stroke (Beghi 2021, PMID 34152062). Other major guidelines, for example in the USA, contain no such recommendation.

#### The meta-analyses: two readings of the same data

With Cerebrolysin, the summaries diverge widely – depending on who wrote them and which endpoints are the focus.

| Review | Data included | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| Cochrane (Ziganshina 2023), PMID 37818733 | 7 RCTs, 1,773 people, start within 48 h | no effect on mortality (RR 0.96); more nonfatal serious adverse events (RR 2.39) |
| Bornstein 2018, PMID 29248999 | 9 RCTs, 1,879 people | NIHSS better after 30 days (MW 0.60); NNT 7.7 for clinically relevant improvement |
| Zhang 2017, PMID 28656143 | 7 RCTs, 1,779 people | no superiority in mRS and Barthel index; safety neutral |
| Patel 2025, PMID 41018475 | 14 RCTs, 2,884 people | NIHSS improvement 1.39 points greater; functional independence not significant (RR 1.31) |

The **Cochrane review** – an independent, methodologically rigorous form of summary – has been updated several times since 2010. The 2020 version included seven randomized trials with 1,601 people and found no effect on mortality (RR 0.90; 95% CI 0.61–1.32). The total number of serious adverse events did not differ substantially, but the number of people with **nonfatal** serious adverse events was higher on Cerebrolysin (RR 2.15; 95% CI 1.01–4.55) (Ziganshina 2020, PMID 32662068). The 2023 update added a trial with the Russian bovine brain preparation Cortexin (1,773 people in total) and confirmed these findings: no effect on mortality (RR 0.96), more nonfatal serious events (RR 2.39; 95% CI 1.10–5.23), even more pronounced with a ten-day treatment regimen (Ziganshina 2023, PMID 37818733). The Cochrane authors rated the risk of bias of many trials as unclear or high and pointed out that the manufacturer had supported several of the large trials. Importantly: none of the included trials reported the endpoint that matters most from the patient's perspective, "death or dependency," in the form the review had planned.

The **manufacturer-affiliated meta-analyses**, by contrast, look mainly at early neurological improvement on the NIHSS and find a moderate advantage there (Bornstein 2018, PMID 29248999). A safety meta-analysis with twelve trials and 2,202 people found no significant differences from placebo in side effects (Strilciuc 2021, PMID 34959697) – in contrast to the Cochrane signal, which, however, is based on few events.

How can this be reconciled? The honest answer is: in several trials, Cerebrolysin improves neurological scale scores in the first weeks. Whether this leads to less lasting disability, less dependency or a longer life has not been demonstrated. There is no coherent explanation for the signal of nonfatal serious side effects; it is based on few events and is therefore uncertain, but it was found in two Cochrane versions.

#### Registry data from everyday practice

The manufacturer has also funded large observational studies. The registry study C-REGS2 recorded 1,865 people with moderate stroke in 16 countries; 1,769 were analyzed (1,021 with Cerebrolysin, 748 without). After 90 days, the Cerebrolysin group did better on the modified Rankin Scale (MW 0.6157) (Vosko 2025, PMID 40851188; NCT03480698). The study uses sophisticated statistical methods to make the groups comparable. But it remains an observational study: those who receive Cerebrolysin in everyday practice may differ in treatment, hospital or country from those who do not. It cannot replace randomization.

#### Intracerebral hemorrhage and subarachnoid hemorrhage

In intracerebral hemorrhage, a study with 100 people found no significant differences after 21 days, but also no safety problems (Bajenaru 2010, PMID 20968198). In bleeding from a bulge in a brain vessel (aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage), a double-blind pilot study with 50 people showed no difference in functional outcome after six months (Woo 2020, PMID 33143640; NCT01787123). A pilot study on lobar hemorrhages (CLINCH) is planned or ongoing (Kobayashi 2025, PMID 41180520).

#### Traumatic brain injury: CAPTAIN

In moderate to severe traumatic brain injury, two trials tested Cerebrolysin in addition to standard treatment:

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAPTAIN I (Poon 2020), PMID 31494820 | 46 people, double-blind vs. placebo, multicenter | 90 days | primary endpoint narrowly missed (ITT: MW 0.63); significant in the per-protocol analysis |
| CAPTAIN II (Muresanu 2020), PMID 31897941 | 142 people (139 analyzed), double-blind, single center | 90 days | small to medium advantage in the combined endpoint of 13 scales (MW 0.59) |
| CAPTAIN meta-analysis (Vester 2021), PMID 33620612 | 185 people from both trials | 90 days | MW 0.60 after 30 and 90 days |

CAPTAIN I was terminated early with only 46 participants (NCT01606111, registry status "terminated"). Further meta-analyses on traumatic brain injury are based mainly on observational studies and found better scores on the Glasgow Outcome Scale, but no effect on mortality (Ghaffarpasand 2019, PMID 30643411; Jarosz 2023, PMID 36979317). Overall, the data point to a possible small advantage; the number of people studied in randomized fashion is too small for a firm conclusion.

#### Dementia

**Alzheimer's disease.** Several placebo-controlled trials were conducted in the 1990s and 2000s. In a trial with 192 people, the clinical global impression (CIBIC+) after twelve weeks was better on Cerebrolysin (76% versus 57% "responders"); however, the groups already differed at baseline in age and hallucinations (Panisset 2002, PMID 12111446). In a trial with 197 people, Cerebrolysin was as effective as the approved Alzheimer's drug donepezil on global impression; there were no significant differences between groups in cognition, daily functioning and behavior (Alvarez 2011, PMID 21679156). A meta-analysis of six trials found an advantage in clinical global impression after four weeks and six months, but in cognition only after four weeks, not after six months (Gauthier 2015, PMID 25832905). Researchers who work with the manufacturer were involved in this meta-analysis.

**Vascular dementia.** The largest trial, with 242 people, reported an improvement on the ADAS-cog+ of 10.6 points after 24 weeks compared with 4.4 points on placebo (Guekht 2011, PMID 20656516) – an unusually large effect for dementia trials. The Cochrane review on vascular dementia summarized six trials with 597 people and found advantages in cognition and global impression, but rated the certainty of the evidence as "very low." The authors write that possible effects may be too small to be clinically meaningful and that the evidence base is weak, even though Cerebrolysin continues to be promoted (Cui 2019, PMID 31710397).

#### Other uses

Smaller studies exist on ALS (20 people, improvement on the ALS functional rating scale after one month; Firstenfeld 2023, PMID 38585517), preterm infants, children with cerebral palsy, loss of smell after COVID-19 and psychiatric disorders. In healthy men, Cerebrolysin altered EEG patterns in a small study with 48 participants; in short-term memory, there was a trend toward poorer word recall in the first hours on placebo, but not on Cerebrolysin (Funke 1998, PMID 9700674). There is no evidence for use as a "nootropic" in healthy people.

### Animal and laboratory studies

- In rats with experimentally induced stroke, Cerebrolysin dose-dependently improved neurological recovery after 28 days in a randomized, blinded animal study (Zhang 2016, PMID 26763925).
- In rats with mild brain injury, Cerebrolysin reduced axonal damage and promoted the formation of new nerve cells in the hippocampus (Zhang 2019, PMID 30499355).
- In a mouse model of the hereditary disease CADASIL, Cerebrolysin extended lifespan and improved spatial memory without affecting the typical changes in the white matter (Kastberger 2024, PMID 38062874).

A considerable proportion of the preclinical studies come from collaborations with the manufacturer. Good animal results have so far rarely translated into clinical benefit for stroke drugs.

### Risks and side effects

**Established:**

- In the trials, side effects overall were about as frequent as on placebo. Those mentioned include headache, dizziness and restlessness (Panisset 2002, PMID 12111446).
- The Cochrane review on stroke found more **nonfatal serious adverse events** on Cerebrolysin (RR 2.39; Ziganshina 2023, PMID 37818733); the manufacturer-affiliated safety meta-analysis did not (Strilciuc 2021, PMID 34959697).
- **Allergic reactions:** A thoroughly documented case report describes a life-threatening anaphylactic reaction in an 85-year-old man after a Cerebrolysin infusion; the authors emphasize that such cases are rare but significant in critically ill people (Trimmel 2024, PMID 39055722).

**Theoretical:** As a preparation made from animal brain tissue, Cerebrolysin is inherently subject to questions of purity and transmissibility of pathogens; manufacturing processes and regulatory requirements are intended to minimize this risk. Specific cases of transmission are not described in the sources reviewed here.

**Gray market:** In nootropics forums, Cerebrolysin is discussed as a "brain booster" and obtained from countries where it is available. Intravenous self-administration without medical supervision carries the risk of severe allergic reactions for which no emergency care is then available. More on gray-market products in Part I and Part IV.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** no centralized EU approval. In Austria, Cerebrolysin is nationally approved as a prescription medicine; indications and reimbursement are governed by the Austrian prescribing information. Other EU member states have their own national approvals. To our knowledge, Cerebrolysin is not marketed in Germany; at most, it could be obtained as an individual import on a physician's prescription (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **USA:** not approved.
- **Sport:** not on the WADA list (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- For approved use: refer to the prescribing information and medical supervision.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established (registered studies, as of October 2026):**

- **CERebrolysin In CADASIL** (NCT05755997): phase 2 trial with 30 participants in the hereditary vascular disease CADASIL, active, recruitment completed.
- **Aphasia after stroke** (NCT06897176): randomized, placebo-controlled trial with 40 participants in Korea, recruiting.
- **Cerebrolysin after extended thrombectomy** (NCT06339411): phase 2 trial with 100 planned participants in Taiwan, starting 2026.
- Further smaller studies on delirium after heart surgery and in the intensive care unit, on intracerebral hemorrhage and on early rehabilitation are registered.

**Assessment:** None of the registered studies is large enough to answer the central open question: does Cerebrolysin reduce lasting disability or mortality after stroke? That would require an independently conducted, sufficiently large trial with a clear, prespecified primary endpoint such as the modified Rankin Scale after 90 days. As long as it is lacking, assessments will remain divided between "established in Eastern Europe and Asia" and "insufficiently proven in the West."

### Conclusion

Cerebrolysin is a peptide mixture derived from pig brain that is used in Austria and many countries of Eastern Europe and Asia for stroke, traumatic brain injury and dementia. Numerous randomized trials show early improvements on neurological scales, but the largest trial (CASTA), with over 1,000 people, missed its primary endpoint. The independent Cochrane reviews find no effect on mortality, a signal for more nonfatal serious side effects in stroke, and only very uncertain benefits in vascular dementia. Manufacturer-affiliated analyses reach considerably more positive results. The data are not sufficient to reliably demonstrate a clinically meaningful long-term benefit; Cerebrolysin is unsuitable for self-treatment or as a "nootropic."
