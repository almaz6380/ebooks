## Sermorelin

Sermorelin is a piece of pharmaceutical history that is living a second life online. In the 1990s it was approved as a medicine in the USA – first as a test substance for diagnosing growth hormone deficiency, then for the treatment of children with short stature. Since the end of the 2000s, the original product has disappeared from the market. Today, sermorelin is mainly prescribed as a compounded preparation in American "anti-aging" and "men's health" clinics and offered on the gray market. This chapter shows what the old studies actually prove and what they do not.

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic peptide of 29 amino acids; it corresponds to the front, active segment of the endogenous growth hormone-releasing hormone, GHRH(1-29)NH2. It is the shortest synthetic peptide with full GHRH activity (Prakash 1999, PMID 18031173).
- **Origin:** derived from natural GHRH in the 1980s; marketed by Serono as Geref.
- **Mechanism of action:** binds to the GHRH receptor of the pituitary gland and triggers the release of growth hormone there; the body's own regulation (brake via somatostatin, feedback via IGF-1) is preserved.
- **Approval EU:** no (as of 2026). **Approval USA:** formerly yes (Geref as a diagnostic agent and for the treatment of children with growth hormone deficiency); the original product has no longer been available since the end of the 2000s.
- **WADA:** prohibited, group S2 – sermorelin is explicitly named as a GHRH analog.
- **Evidence level: B** – individual small randomized or controlled trials, mostly open-label and from the 1980s and 1990s; no modern large trial.

### How it is supposed to work

The pituitary gland releases growth hormone in pulses. The starting signal comes from GHRH from the diencephalon. As produced in nature, GHRH is 44 amino acids long; in the 1980s, researchers found that the first 29 amino acids are sufficient for the full effect. Sermorelin was developed from this.

The advantage over injected growth hormone is supposed to be that the pituitary gland "has a say": if there is enough IGF-1 in the blood, the body itself applies the brake via somatostatin. Overdoses as with direct GH administration are therefore supposed to be less likely. The disadvantage: sermorelin only works if the pituitary gland can still produce growth hormone, and its effect is short: the half-life in human blood is only about 10 to 20 minutes (Esposito 2003, PMID 14499707). It is therefore effective mainly in disorders located in the diencephalon, not in the pituitary itself.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence (search October 2026):** The automated search for this book under the keyword "sermorelin" found **no** human study (`humanstudien_alle`: 0) and **27** entries on ClinicalTrials.gov. Both figures are misleading: most clinical papers appeared under designations such as "GHRH(1-29)NH2," "GRF 1-29" or "Geref" and are not captured by a pure name search. Conversely, many of the 27 registry entries do not concern sermorelin at all, but tesamorelin or GHRH tests in general. A targeted follow-up search found several dozen clinical studies, mostly small and from the period before 2000.

#### Children with growth hormone deficiency

The main area of application was growth disorder in children. In an open-label, multicenter study by the Geref study group, 110 previously untreated prepubertal children received sermorelin once daily in the evening for one year. Growth velocity rose from 4.1 cm per year to 8.0 cm after six months and 7.2 cm after twelve months; 74% were considered good responders after six months. No abnormal laboratory changes occurred (Thorner 1996, PMID 8772599).

An early British study with 18 children already showed the limits: only in 8 children did growth velocity increase by more than 2 cm per year in the first six months; 14 children developed antibodies against the peptide, without any discernible effect on growth (Ross 1987, PMID 2879138). In a randomized trial with 43 children, the children grew most slowly on low-dose sermorelin; only on growth hormone did height improve relative to bone age (Neyzi 1993, PMID 8329826). A review noted that sermorelin is effective in some of the children, but weaker than growth hormone, and that data on final height are lacking (Prakash 1999, PMID 18031173).

In children with idiopathic short stature (i.e., without demonstrable hormone deficiency), growth velocity also increased with twice-daily injections in a small study with 18 children; after discontinuation it fell back below baseline, and fasting glucose and insulin rose during treatment (Kirk 1994, PMID 7955460).

#### Older adults

For today's marketing ("anti-aging"), studies in older people are decisive. There are only a few, and they are very small:

- 19 healthy women and men between 55 and 71 years of age received a sermorelin analog for 16 weeks after four weeks of placebo. Growth hormone and IGF-1 rose, skin thickness increased in both sexes, and lean mass and insulin sensitivity only in men. Men reported improved well-being and increased libido; sleep quality did not change. As a side effect, a temporary increase in blood lipids occurred (Khorram 1997, PMID 9141536). The same group described changes in immune cells (Khorram 1997, PMID 9360512). The study was only single-blind and had no parallel control group.
- In 11 healthy men between 64 and 76 years of age, six weeks of nightly injections increased nocturnal GH release, but neither IGF-1 nor muscle or fat mass changed; two of six strength measurements improved (Vittone 1997, PMID 9005976).

Two later studies on GHRH in older people were stopped after 5 and 13 participants, respectively (NCT00807365, NCT01410799).

#### Diagnostics

As a test substance, sermorelin was given intravenously to check whether the pituitary gland responds to GHRH. Combined with the amino acid arginine, this test was considered an alternative in adults to the burdensome insulin hypoglycemia test (Prakash 1999, PMID 18031173). After the diagnostic agent was no longer available in the USA, an ongoing study of a new test (macimorelin) had to change its design (Garcia 2013, PMID 23559086).

#### Modern "men's health"

A retrospective chart review from a US men's clinic described 14 young men on testosterone therapy who were prescribed a combination of sermorelin, GHRP-2 and GHRP-6. IGF-1 rose on average from 159.5 to 239.0 ng/ml (Sigalos 2017, PMID 28830317). Of an original 105 men, only 14 met the inclusion criteria; the paper allows no conclusions about muscle mass, fat loss or safety.

### Animal and laboratory studies

Sermorelin itself was studied in the laboratory mainly in connection with its discovery and with variants with a longer duration of action, for example variants coupled to polyethylene glycol, which acted longer than the unmodified peptide in rats and pigs (Esposito 2003, PMID 14499707). A purely computational study that matched gene activity data from glioma patients against a drug database named sermorelin as a possible candidate against recurrent brain tumors (Chang 2021, PMID 33842627). This is a computer hypothesis without laboratory or patient confirmation – and stands in striking contrast to the concern that stimulating the growth axis might rather promote tumors.

### Risks and side effects

**Documented (from approval and study data):** temporary facial flushing and pain at the injection site were the most common side effects (Prakash 1999, PMID 18031173). Antibodies against sermorelin frequently occurred in children, without discernible consequences (Ross 1987, PMID 2879138). In individual studies, blood glucose, insulin or blood lipids rose temporarily (Kirk 1994, PMID 7955460; Khorram 1997, PMID 9141536).

**Long-term risks:** There are no systematic long-term data on sermorelin in adults. A case report describes a 62-year-old recreational bodybuilder who had used growth hormone and GH releasers (including sermorelin with GHRP-6) for around 20 years and developed swallowing difficulties due to a large bone spur on the cervical spine; the authors consider a connection possible but not proven (Perez 2026, PMID 42465868). On the fundamental question of whether a sustained increase in IGF-1 raises the risk of cancer, see the introduction to this group and Part IV.

**Gray market:** In a European customs operation, sermorelin products were found among seized injectable preparations alongside growth hormone and melanotan II – without labeling and in some cases without active substance (Venhuis 2016, PMID 26456392).

### Legal status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **Background of the US approval:** Geref was initially approved in the USA as a diagnostic agent and, from the 1990s, for the treatment of children with growth hormone deficiency. The manufacturer discontinued distribution toward the end of the 2000s; according to publicly available information, for manufacturing or commercial reasons and not because of a newly identified safety problem. That the diagnostic agent disappeared around this time is documented in a study (Garcia 2013, PMID 23559086). There is no finished medicinal product with a valid approval today.
- **USA today:** Sermorelin is produced by compounding pharmacies and prescribed mainly in anti-aging practices. Whether and under what conditions this is permissible depends on the changing FDA rules for compounding substances, which are debated controversially in professional circles (Willis 2026, PMID 42752426).
- **EU, Germany, Austria:** no approval, no finished medicinal product on the market. Supplying it for use in humans without approval is impermissible under the respective medicines act; "for research purposes only" labels do not change this.
- **WADA:** prohibited (S2). Detection methods for sermorelin and its breakdown products in urine have been developed (Memdouh 2021, PMID 34665524).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

As of October 2026, no ongoing clinical trial with sermorelin itself is listed on ClinicalTrials.gov; the registry entries under this search term concern completed GHRH studies or tesamorelin. *Author's assessment:* A return as an approved medicine is not in sight. Demand will continue to be driven by clinics and online suppliers, not by new study data.

### Conclusion

Sermorelin reliably stimulates the body's own release of growth hormone and was approved in the USA for children with growth hormone deficiency, but was weaker there than growth hormone itself. The studies in older adults each include only about ten to twenty people and show at most small, inconsistent effects. Evidence of a benefit as an "anti-aging" agent or for performance enhancement is lacking, as are long-term safety data.
