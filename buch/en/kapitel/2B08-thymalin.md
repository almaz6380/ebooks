## Thymalin and Thymogen

Thymalin is an extract from the thymus gland of calves; Thymogen is a synthetic dipeptide derived from it. Both originate from Soviet military medicine of the 1970s and 1980s and are still on the market as medicines in Russia today. In Western longevity circles, they are marketed as "bioregulators" intended to "rejuvenate" the immune system and extend life. The basis for these promises is almost exclusively the research of a single school around the St. Petersburg gerontologist Vladimir Khavinson. This chapter puts this research into perspective – and explains why its results should be read with great caution.

### Profile

- **Thymalin:** mixture of peptides obtained by acid extraction from the thymus of calves (Morozov 1997, PMID 9637345); not a uniformly defined substance. The dipeptides Lys-Glu (KE) and Glu-Trp (EW) are named as the most important active components (Linkova 2023, PMID 37686182).
- **Thymogen:** synthetically manufactured dipeptide L-Glu-L-Trp, isolated from thymalin (Anisimov 2000, PMID 11707921); also available as a nasal spray in Russia.
- **Mechanism of action (claimed):** stimulation of T-cell maturation, "normalization" of immune function and blood clotting; according to Khavinson's theory, short peptides act as "bioregulators" directly on gene activity.
- **Approval:** approved as medicines in Russia and some successor states of the Soviet Union; **EU and USA:** no.
- **WADA:** not explicitly listed (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level: C** – predominantly small, open-label or inadequately described studies; only a single study in PubMed described as randomized and placebo-controlled.

### How it is supposed to work

The idea follows the same logic as with thymosin alpha-1 (see there): the thymus trains T cells and shrinks with age, so thymic messengers should strengthen the body's defenses. However, while thymosin alpha-1 is a precisely defined peptide with a known sequence, thymalin is a mixture of many components of animal origin.

From this mixture, the researchers isolated the dipeptide Glu-Trp and brought it to market synthetically as Thymogen. It is said to promote T-cell differentiation and antigen recognition and to activate phagocytes (Morozov 1997, PMID 9637345).

Khavinson developed a more comprehensive theory from this: short peptides from various organs are said to act in a tissue-specific way and "regulate" gene activity – peptides from the pineal gland for the pineal gland, from the thymus for the immune system and so on (Khavinson 2002, PMID 12374906). Epitalon, which is dealt with in Part II · D, also comes from this school. This theory has hardly been tested outside the research group and does not correspond to the general understanding of how peptide messengers act – namely, as a rule, via receptors on the cell surface. Newer work by the group bases the mechanism mainly on computer simulations of binding to genetic material and on cell cultures (Linkova 2023, PMID 37686182).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence (search October 2026):** The automated search found **17** human studies in PubMed and **0** entries on ClinicalTrials.gov. In total, PubMed lists around 290 publications on thymalin or Thymogen; of these, about 250 are in Russian, and only a single one mentions "randomized" or "placebo" in the title or abstract. For most of the human studies, PubMed contains only a short Russian or Ukrainian abstract, often without information on the number of participants, the control group or blinding.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Extension of life" (Khavinson 2003, PMID 14523363) | 266 older people, thymalin and/or epithalamin vs. control; allocation and blinding not described | 6–8 years of observation | mortality in the thymalin group 2.0- to 2.1-fold lower than in the control |
| Before abdominal surgery (Smirnov 2011, PMID 21957588) | older people with abdominal tumors, double-blind RCT vs. placebo, Thymogen as nasal drops; number of participants not stated in the abstract | 7 days before surgery | fewer complications after surgery |
| Severe COVID-19 (Kuznik 2022, PMID 36169363) | three groups: standard therapy, plus tocilizumab, plus thymalin; allocation not described | hospital stay | in-hospital mortality 40.9%, 28.4% and 20.6% |
| Treatment-resistant schizophrenia (Govorin 1990, PMID 2163147) | 36 people, no control group | not stated | improvement in psychiatric symptoms reported |
| Parkinson's disease (Musienko 1999, PMID 10424021) | 15 people, no control group | one course of treatment | improvement in well-being and symptoms reported |

#### The long-term study on life extension

The best-known paper is at the same time the most problematic. Over six to eight years, 266 older people in St. Petersburg and Kyiv were observed, some of whom received thymalin, epithalamin or both during the first two to three years. A 2.0- to 2.1-fold lower mortality on thymalin and a 4.1-fold lower mortality with annual combination treatment over six years are reported (Khavinson 2003, PMID 14523363).

Such effects would be spectacular – larger than with almost any established medicine. However, the abstract describes neither how participants were allocated to the groups, nor whether there was blinding, nor how many people were in each group. People who are healthier and more active are more likely to take part in years of treatment; without random allocation, such a difference can arise from this alone. There are no independent replications.

#### COVID-19

During the pandemic, the research group reported on thymalin in patients severely ill with COVID-19. In one analysis, in-hospital mortality was 40.9 percent on standard therapy, 28.4 percent with additional tocilizumab and 20.6 percent with additional thymalin (Kuznik 2022, PMID 36169363). How the people were allocated to the groups and how large the groups were is not apparent from the abstract. Another paper reports faster decreases in inflammatory and coagulation values (Khavinson 2021, PMID 33575961). Given the experience with thymosin alpha-1, where early favorable observational data were not confirmed in larger trials (see there), these figures are not reliable without randomized confirmation.

#### The only placebo-controlled study

The only study described as double-blind, randomized and placebo-controlled examined Thymogen nasal drops in older people before abdominal tumor surgery. Fewer complications after surgery and a shorter healing course are reported (Smirnov 2011, PMID 21957588). The Russian abstract does not give the number of participants or effect sizes.

#### Other studies

The remaining human studies date predominantly from the 1980s and 1990s and cover a strikingly broad spectrum: tuberculosis, dysentery, erysipelas, purulent wounds, peritonitis in children, pancreatitis, ulcerative colitis, inflammation of the female reproductive organs, schizophrenia and Parkinson's disease. Almost always, thymalin or Thymogen was given in addition to standard treatment, without placebo and mostly without random allocation. Laboratory values of the immune system were frequently measured, not hard endpoints such as healing or survival.

### Animal and laboratory studies

The animal studies likewise come predominantly from Russian and Ukrainian research groups. In 76 rats that received Glu-Trp (Thymogen) or saline for twelve months, mean lifespan was the same; the longest-living ten percent lived longer on Thymogen, and tumors occurred less frequently (Anisimov 2000, PMID 11707921). In cultures of human blood stem cells, thymalin influenced their differentiation (Khavinson 2020, PMID 33237528). Further papers describe effects on blood clotting, cancer development after radiation or chemicals, and infections in rodents.

### Quality of the research: a critical assessment

For the assessment of thymalin and Thymogen, the origin of the data is more important than the individual results:

1. **One school, hardly any independent confirmation.** A large part of the work comes from Khavinson, his co-workers or cooperating institutes. According to PubMed, Khavinson is involved in over 80 of the around 290 publications; many others come from closely affiliated groups. Independent Western replication studies are lacking.
2. **Language and accessibility.** Around 250 of the papers are in Russian, often with only a short abstract in PubMed. Full texts are difficult to access, and methods can hardly be checked.
3. **Study design.** Almost no study is randomized, blinded and preregistered according to today's standards. Not a single study is entered on ClinicalTrials.gov.
4. **Unusually large effects in a great many diseases.** A preparation that works equally in tuberculosis, schizophrenia, Parkinson's disease, COVID-19 and aging and halves mortality is, from a methodological point of view, a warning sign, not a recommendation.
5. **Conflicts of interest.** The research group developed the preparations itself; to the author's knowledge, "bioregulators" from this school are marketed internationally as dietary supplements. Independent testing by researchers without such ties is lacking.

This does not mean that all results must be wrong. It means that, by the standards of this book, they cannot count as evidence of a benefit as long as they have not been confirmed in properly planned, independent studies.

### Risks and side effects

#### Documented

Systematically collected safety data according to today's standards are not available. The existing studies hardly report on side effects – which says little in small, unblinded studies.

#### Theoretical

- **Animal source material:** Thymalin is an extract from calf thymus. With animal extracts, there are fundamental risks from contaminants, batch-to-batch variation and allergic reactions. The transmission of pathogens from bovine tissue is also a theoretical risk that depends on manufacturing quality.
- **Immune stimulation:** In autoimmune diseases or after organ transplantation, stimulating the immune system could cause harm. Data on this are lacking.
- **Gray market and "bioregulators":** Outside Russia, thymalin, Thymogen and related products are distributed online, partly as solutions for injection, partly as capsules labeled "bioregulator." The origin, content and quality of these products cannot be verified.

### Legal status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **Russia and some successor states of the Soviet Union:** thymalin and Thymogen are approved as medicines.
- **EU, Germany, Austria:** no approval. Import and supply for use in humans are permitted only under the narrow conditions of the respective pharmaceutical law. Capsules containing organ extracts sold as dietary supplements may not promise effects on diseases; whether they are to be classified as medicines depends on the individual case.
- **USA:** no FDA approval.
- **WADA:** not listed by name.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed:** As of October 2026, no trial on thymalin or Thymogen is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov.

**Author's assessment:** Approval in the EU or the USA is out of the question without new studies conducted according to international standards, and such studies are not in sight. Thymalin and Thymogen will therefore probably remain a phenomenon of the Russian-speaking health market and the international longevity scene.

### Conclusion

Thymalin is a calf thymus extract, Thymogen a dipeptide derived from it; both are approved in Russia and are promoted in the West as "bioregulators" for life extension. The evidence comes almost entirely from a single Russian research school, is predominantly published in Russian and consists of small, mostly non-randomized studies. The much-cited halving of mortality in older people (Khavinson 2003, PMID 14523363) is based on a study whose design is not described comprehensibly in the abstract. By today's standards, a benefit has not been proven, and the quality of freely sold products is unknown.
