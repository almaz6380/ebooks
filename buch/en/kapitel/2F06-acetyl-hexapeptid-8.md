## Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 (Argireline)

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** hexapeptide with the sequence glutamate-glutamate-methionine-glutamine-arginine-arginine (Ac-EEMQRR-NH₂), acetylated at the start and amidated at the end. Formerly called acetyl hexapeptide-3; trade name of the raw material: Argireline.
- **Origin:** synthetic, deliberately designed in the early 2000s. The sequence is modeled on the initial segment of the nerve cell protein SNAP-25.
- **Mechanism of action:** supposed to weaken the release of the messenger acetylcholine at the junction between nerve and muscle and thus smooth expression lines – a target similar to that of botulinum toxin, only much weaker. Hence the advertising term "Botox in a tube."
- **Approval EU:** not a medicine; cosmetic ingredient.
- **Approval USA:** not a drug; cosmetic ingredient.
- **WADA:** not listed (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **B** (weak) – a few small randomized studies with mostly short durations and inconsistent results.

### How it is supposed to work

For a muscle to contract, a nerve cell must release the messenger acetylcholine. To do this, small vesicles filled with the messenger fuse with the cell membrane. This fusion is mediated by a protein complex, the SNARE complex, in which the protein SNAP-25, among others, is involved. Botulinum toxin type A cleaves SNAP-25 and thereby paralyzes the release of the messenger for months – the muscle relaxes and the overlying wrinkle smooths out.

Argireline was designed as a mimic of the initial segment of SNAP-25. The idea: the short peptide competes with the real SNAP-25 for its place in the SNARE complex and disrupts its formation. In the first description, Argireline inhibited the release of messengers in cell experiments with a *potency* similar to botulinum toxin A, but, as expected, achieved a much lower *efficacy* – that is, a considerably smaller maximum effect (Blanes-Mira 2002, PMID 18498523).

The decisive catch: to work, Argireline from a cream would have to pass through the epidermis and dermis to the nerve endings at the facial muscles. The molecule is water-soluble, electrically charged and comparatively large for the skin. In a laboratory experiment with skin from body donors, most of it remained on the skin surface after 24 hours; 0.22% of the applied peptide was found in the stratum corneum, 0.01% in the epidermis, and nothing was detectable in the dermis and below (Kraeling 2015, PMID 24754410). A 2025 review states that it remains unclear whether the peptide reaches the neuromuscular junction at all (Zdrada-Nowak 2025, PMID 40565185).

This does not rule out effects on the skin – for example on moisture or via nerve endings in the upper layers of the skin – but it makes the advertised "Botox mechanism" unlikely when applied to the skin.

### Human studies

The PubMed search evaluated for this book (acetyl hexapeptide or Argireline in title or abstract, search date October 2026) yielded three papers classified as human studies; ClinicalTrials.gov lists seven registry entries. Review turned up a few more small studies.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blanes-Mira 2002, PMID 18498523 | healthy women, emulsion with 10% Argireline (details in abstract sparse) | 30 days | Wrinkle depth reduced by up to 30% |
| Wang 2013, PMID 23417317 | 60 Chinese adults with periorbital wrinkles, Argireline vs. placebo in a 3:1 ratio | 4 weeks | Subjective "overall efficacy" 48.9% vs. 0%; roughness values in skin replicas decreased |
| Raikou 2017, PMID 28150423 | 24 healthy people, four groups: Argireline, tripeptide-10 citrulline, both or neither | 60 days | Some wrinkle parameters and transepidermal water loss better with Argireline |
| Henseler 2023, PMID 38024099 | 19 women, split-face comparison: hyaluronic serum with vs. without Argireline, double-blind | 4 weeks | No significant difference between the halves of the face |
| Aruan 2023, PMID 36909866 | 21 women, acetyl hexapeptide-3 vs. palmitoyl pentapeptide-4 vs. placebo | 8 weeks | Palmitoyl pentapeptide-4 performed better than Argireline and placebo |

**Putting the positive studies in context:**

- The first description from 2002 comes from the developers, and the abstract states neither the number of participants nor a control group. It tested a concentration of 10% – far above what a US safety assessment later considered adequately studied (see Risks).
- The Chinese study (Wang 2013) is the largest. The groups were of unequal size (about 45 versus 15), the main assessment was subjective, and the same clinical data appear together with mouse experiments in another publication by the same group (Wang 2013, PMID 23607739). In comparisons with baseline, only the Argireline group showed decreasing roughness values – a direct statistical comparison of the groups is not reported in the abstract.
- The Raikou study had only six participants per group and a large number of individual comparisons; some differences were significant, others were not.

**The negative study:** A double-blind split-face study from a plastic surgery practice with objective camera measurement (Henseler 2023) found no difference between the half of the face with and without Argireline. The investigators concluded that an effect had not been demonstrated and that Argireline should not be classified as an alternative to botulinum toxin. With 19 participants, however, this study was also small.

#### Other Applications

- **Eyelid spasm (blepharospasm):** In this neurological disorder, the eyelid muscles cramp involuntarily; standard treatment is the injection of botulinum toxin every three months. In a pilot study with 24 patients, an Argireline cream was supposed to prolong the duration of effect of the injection. There was a non-significant trend (3.7 versus 3.0 months until symptoms returned) and no relevant side effects (Lungu 2013, PMID 23146065; registered as NCT00942851). A follow-up study was terminated after eight participants (NCT01750346).
- **Microneedle patches:** In a Korean split-face study with 52 women, a hyaluronic microneedle patch with Argireline improved wrinkles more than the patch alone (An 2019, PMID 33911590). Here the peptide is delivered into the skin by tiny needles – the result cannot be transferred to normal creams.
- **Combination products:** Many studies test serums that contain Argireline together with retinol, hyaluronic acid or other peptides. Their results cannot be attributed to the individual peptide.
- **Registered small studies:** ClinicalTrials.gov lists, among others, a completed study on oily skin with 14 participants (NCT02597777) and a study on periorbital wrinkles described as phase 3 with 70 participants (NCT01381484); whether the latter corresponds to the Chinese study mentioned above could not be clearly determined.

### Animal and laboratory studies

- **Cell experiments:** inhibition of messenger release and disruption of SNARE complex formation (Blanes-Mira 2002, PMID 18498523). In cell cultures of human skin fibroblasts and other cell lines, Argireline inhibited cell proliferation in a dose-dependent manner, but only at concentrations 18 to 10,000 times higher than those of the comparator substance doxorubicin, a cancer drug (Grosicki 2014, PMID 24644551).
- **Animal experiments:** In mice with artificially accelerated aging (induced by D-galactose), the proportion of type I collagen fibers in the skin increased under Argireline (Wang 2013, PMID 23464592).
- **Skin penetration:** In addition to the study on body donor skin (Kraeling 2015, PMID 24754410), numerous laboratory studies are investigating emulsions, microneedles, iontophoresis and chemical modifications to deliver more active ingredient into the skin.

### Risks and side effects

**As a cream (established):** No relevant side effects were observed in the studies; skin irritation and allergies are possible, as with any cosmetic ingredient. The independent US expert panel on the safety of cosmetic ingredients (Cosmetic Ingredient Review) concluded in 2025 that acetyl hexapeptide-8 is safe at the concentrations currently in use of up to 0.005%. For higher concentrations, the available data were insufficient for a safety statement (Johnson 2025, PMID 40673537).

**As an injection:** Argireline is sometimes also injected into the skin, for example in beauty salons or via gray-market products. There is no approval and there are no studies for this. A case report describes a 45-year-old woman who developed redness, nodules and abscesses after Argireline injections into the forehead and temples; the cause was an infection with the difficult-to-treat bacterium *Mycobacterium abscessus*, which required five months of antibiotic therapy (Chen 2021, PMID 33748252). The risk here lies less in the peptide itself than in non-sterile products and procedures.

**Theoretical:** If Argireline reached neuromuscular junctions in significant amounts, undesirable muscle weakness would be conceivable – as with botulinum toxin. There is no indication of this with topical use.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** Acetyl hexapeptide-8 is a permitted cosmetic ingredient under the EU Cosmetics Regulation (Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009). Advertising claims must be substantiated in accordance with Regulation (EU) No 655/2013. Comparisons with botulinum toxin ("like Botox") are misleading if they suggest a comparable effect. An injection is not a cosmetic use; a product intended for injection would be an unapproved medicine or medical device and may not be marketed as such.
- **USA:** cosmetic ingredient without prior review by the FDA; claims that promise an effect on muscles can legally turn a product into a drug.
- **Sport:** irrelevant (as of 2026, please check for updates).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established:** On ClinicalTrials.gov (as of October 2026), there is no ongoing study with acetyl hexapeptide-8 as a single active ingredient; the listed entries have been completed or terminated. Cosmetic efficacy studies are rarely registered.

**Assessment:** Argireline is an example of a rationally designed peptide whose laboratory finding is convincing but whose path to the site of action remains unresolved. Research is therefore focusing on delivery systems such as microneedles and nanocarriers. Whether these achieve an effect that is visible to users and goes beyond that of a good moisturizer must be shown by larger, independent studies. Based on current knowledge, a genuine alternative to botulinum toxin is not to be expected.

### Conclusion

Acetyl hexapeptide-8 is a synthetic peptide that in the laboratory inhibits the release of messengers at nerve cells similarly to botulinum toxin, but much more weakly. From creams, it barely penetrates the skin; in a laboratory experiment, it remained almost entirely in the superficial layers. Small, short studies – some by the developers, some with combination products – report fewer fine lines; a blinded split-face study with objective measurement, by contrast, found no difference. As a cream, it is considered safe at the usual concentrations; injections have been neither studied nor approved and can lead to serious infections. The advertising term "Botox in a tube" is not supported by the data.
