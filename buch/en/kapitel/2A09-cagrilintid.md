## Cagrilintide and CagriSema

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** Cagrilintide is a synthetic, fatty-acid-modified analog of the hormone amylin for once-weekly injection under the skin. **CagriSema** is the fixed combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide in one injection device.
- **Origin:** developed by Novo Nordisk, the manufacturer of semaglutide.
- **Mechanism of action:** Cagrilintide activates amylin receptors and additionally the related calcitonin receptor (a "dual amylin and calcitonin receptor agonist"). In CagriSema, the GLP-1 effect of semaglutide is added.
- **Approval EU:** no (as of October 2026).
- **Approval USA:** no (as of October 2026; an approval procedure for CagriSema has been announced or may already be under way – please check for updates).
- **WADA:** prohibited as a non-approved substance under S0 (as of 2026, please check for updates).
- **Evidence level:** **B** – several large Phase 3 trials published, but no approval yet. With approval, CagriSema would reach level A.

### How it is supposed to work

Amylin is a hormone released by the beta cells of the pancreas together with insulin. It acts mainly in the brainstem: it promotes the feeling of satiety, slows gastric emptying and curbs glucagon release after meals. People with type 1 diabetes lack not only insulin but also amylin.

Natural amylin is unsuitable as a medicine because it tends to clump (it forms so-called amyloid fibrils) and lasts only minutes in the blood. The first amylin drug, pramlintide (see separate chapter), therefore has to be injected several times a day. Cagrilintide was specifically modified so that it remains stable and, thanks to a fatty acid chain, acts for one week (Kruse 2021, PMID 34288673). In Phase 1 data, the half-life was 159 to 195 hours, i.e., about one week (Enebo 2021, PMID 33894838).

The combination with semaglutide follows a simple idea: amylin and GLP-1 act on satiety via different receptors and partly different brain regions – the effects could add up. Studies in genetically modified mice show that cagrilintide requires amylin receptor subtypes 1 and 3 in the brain for its weight-lowering effect (Carvas 2025, PMID 40609154). In rats, CagriSema mainly lowered energy intake, while energy expenditure was largely preserved (Jacobsen 2025, PMID 40629149).

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence:** The automated PubMed search (October 2026) yielded 15 human studies on cagrilintide or CagriSema. On ClinicalTrials.gov, 44 trials were registered. The approval program is called **REDEFINE** (weight management) and **REIMAGINE** (type 2 diabetes).

#### Cagrilintide alone

In the Phase 2 dose-finding trial (Lau 2021, PMID 34798060), 706 adults with overweight or obesity received cagrilintide in five doses for 26 weeks, 99 received liraglutide and 101 placebo. Weight fell in a dose-dependent manner by 6.0 to 10.8% (placebo 3.0%). The highest dose was slightly superior to liraglutide 3.0 mg (10.8% vs. 9.0%). Nausea occurred in 20–47% (placebo 18%). Cagrilintide alone thus reaches roughly the range of older GLP-1 drugs, not that of semaglutide or tirzepatide. It is now also being tested as a single agent in Phase 3 (NCT07220642).

#### CagriSema: early studies

In a Phase 1b trial in 95 healthy people with overweight, participants on semaglutide plus cagrilintide lost up to 17.1% of their weight after 20 weeks, and 9.8% with semaglutide plus placebo (Enebo 2021, PMID 33894838). Cagrilintide did not alter semaglutide blood levels.

A small Phase 2 trial in 92 people with type 2 diabetes compared CagriSema directly with both individual substances (Frias 2023, PMID 37364590). After 32 weeks, weight loss was 15.6% with CagriSema, 5.1% with semaglutide and 8.1% with cagrilintide. For HbA1c, CagriSema at −2.2 percentage points was not statistically superior to semaglutide (−1.8).

**REDEFINE 1** (Garvey 2025, PMID 40544433) is the main trial. In 3,417 adults without diabetes, body weight decreased after 68 weeks by an average of 20.4% compared with 3.0% on placebo. The trial also included smaller groups with semaglutide alone and cagrilintide alone. In a post hoc analysis, 30.3% on CagriSema, 19.1% on semaglutide, 9.0% on cagrilintide and 3.3% on placebo achieved both a BMI below 27 and a favorable waist-to-height ratio (Busetto 2026, PMID 42503495). Systolic blood pressure fell by 10.9 mmHg on CagriSema (placebo −2.8) (Verma 2026, PMID 41328546).

For context: the 20.4% achieved is in the range of tirzepatide in SURMOUNT-1 (20.9% after 72 weeks at the highest dose). Such a cross-trial comparison between different studies is only a rough guide; the direct comparison is still pending (see below).

**REDEFINE 2** (Davies 2025, PMID 40544432) confirmed the pattern in people with type 2 diabetes: −13.7% compared with −3.4%. 73.5% reached an HbA1c of 6.5% or less (placebo 15.9%).

**REDEFINE 5** (Yamauchi 2026, PMID 42009015) is the first published blinded Phase 3 trial in direct comparison with semaglutide 2.4 mg: in Japan and Taiwan, participants lost 18.4% with CagriSema compared with 11.9% with semaglutide alone. Gastrointestinal complaints were similarly frequent at 53% vs. 51%.

**REIMAGINE 2** (Buse 2026, PMID 42251859) showed in type 2 diabetes that CagriSema lowers HbA1c only slightly more than semaglutide 2.4 mg alone (−1.91 vs. −1.75 percentage points). The added benefit of cagrilintide therefore lies mainly in weight, hardly in blood glucose.

The direct comparative trial against tirzepatide in obesity (REDEFINE 4, NCT06131437) and two comparative trials against tirzepatide in type 2 diabetes (NCT06221969, NCT06534411) have been completed according to the registry; peer-reviewed publications were not yet available at the time of research.

### Animal and laboratory studies

Structural biology studies show how cagrilintide binds to amylin and calcitonin receptors (Cao 2025, PMID 40204768). In mice lacking amylin receptor subtypes 1 and 3, the effect was greatly attenuated (Carvas 2025, PMID 40609154). In rats, CagriSema lowered food intake by 39%; according to the authors' calculations, about one third of the weight loss was due to energy expenditure not falling to the usual extent (Jacobsen 2025, PMID 40629149). In another rat study, combining cagrilintide with retatrutide enhanced weight loss (Petersen 2026, PMID 42744908) – an indication that amylin agents could in future also be combined with other incretin drugs. All of these results come from animal experiments.

### Risks and side effects

**Documented in studies:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints** – nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain – affected 79.6% on CagriSema compared with 39.9% on placebo in REDEFINE 1; they were mostly transient and mild to moderate (Garvey 2025, PMID 40544433). In REDEFINE 2, the figures were 72.5% compared with 34.4% (Davies 2025, PMID 40544432).
- **Hypoglycemia:** In REIMAGINE 3, no severe hypoglycemia occurred despite concomitant insulin treatment (Rosenstock 2026, PMID 42251856). In combination with insulin or sulfonylureas, however, the risk fundamentally remains.
- **Heart rhythm:** In a dedicated study with 105 healthy people, cagrilintide did not prolong the QT interval on the ECG to a clinically relevant extent (Gabe 2024, PMID 39279639).
- **Kidney and liver function:** Cagrilintide blood levels did not change substantially in impaired kidney or liver function (Nielsen 2026, PMID 42228334).

**Theoretical or not yet adequately studied:** The calcitonin receptor is also found in bone; how continuous stimulation affects bone metabolism is currently being studied (NCT07010432). It is likewise unclear whether the combination affects the loss of muscle mass (NCT07527195). For the class risks of semaglutide (gallbladder, pancreas, thyroid C-cells in animal studies), see the chapter Semaglutide.

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** neither cagrilintide nor CagriSema is approved; use only in trials. Gray-market offerings of "cagrilintide" are not medicines and are not tested.
- **USA:** not approved (as of October 2026, please check for updates).
- **Sport:** WADA class S0 (not approved), as of 2026, please check for updates.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Confirmed (registered trials, as of October 2026):**

- **REDEFINE 3** (NCT05669755): cardiovascular outcome trial with 7,076 randomized people with established cardiovascular disease and overweight; planned completion 2027. Design and baseline data have been published (Cavender 2026, PMID 42838477).
- **Comparisons against tirzepatide** (NCT06131437, NCT06221969, NCT06534411): completed, publication pending.
- **Higher CagriSema doses** vs. semaglutide (NCT07564414) and a long-term trial on weight maintenance (NCT06780449).
- **Children and adolescents** (NCT07253285) as well as cagrilintide alone (NCT07220642).
- Smaller trials on bone (NCT07010432), muscle (NCT07527195) and on people who do not tolerate GLP-1 drugs (NCT07607587).

**Assessment:** CagriSema is likely to come to market as the first amylin-GLP-1 combination. Whether it prevails against tirzepatide and the upcoming triple agonists depends on the comparative trials and on tolerability. The scientifically more interesting aspect is the expansion of the toolbox: after GLP-1, GIP and glucagon, amylin is the fourth hormonal pathway being opened up for obesity treatment (see also Amycretin).

### Conclusion

Cagrilintide is a long-acting amylin analog that, on its own, lowered weight by up to 10.8% in Phase 2. In combination with semaglutide (CagriSema), it achieved a weight loss of 20.4% after 68 weeks in REDEFINE 1 and was clearly superior to semaglutide alone in East Asia (18.4% vs. 11.9%). Gastrointestinal complaints affect about four out of five people treated. CagriSema is not yet approved; data on cardiovascular events and on the direct comparison with tirzepatide are pending.
