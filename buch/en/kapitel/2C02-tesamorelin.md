## Tesamorelin

Tesamorelin is the only peptide in this group that is currently approved in the United States as a medicine for long-term treatment – albeit for a very specific situation: excess abdominal fat in people with HIV. That is precisely what makes it instructive. Tesamorelin shows what increasing the body's own growth hormone secretion actually achieves in clean, placebo-controlled trials – and what it does not. On the internet, by contrast, it is often promoted as a "belly fat peptide" for everyone. This chapter separates the proven use from the hopes.

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic peptide of 44 amino acids; it corresponds to the complete human growth hormone–releasing hormone, GHRH(1-44) (Falutz 2010, PMID 20101189), with an additional chemical group attached to its beginning that slows its rapid breakdown in the blood.
- **Origin:** developed by the Canadian company Theratechnologies (development name TH9507).
- **Mechanism of action:** like the body's own GHRH, it binds to the GHRH receptor in the pituitary gland and thereby increases the body's own, still pulsatile, secretion of growth hormone (GH); as a consequence, the messenger IGF-1 rises.
- **EU approval:** no. **US approval:** yes (Egrifta, since 2010) for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV and lipodystrophy (FDA approval reported in Traynor 2010, PMID 21115997).
- **WADA:** prohibited, class S2 (peptide hormones, growth factors, related substances) – explicitly covered as a GHRH analog.
- **Evidence level: A** – two large phase 3 trials with a combined 806 participants, several further randomized trials, approval in the United States.

### How it is supposed to work

Growth hormone is not released by the pituitary gland (hypophysis) evenly but in bursts, mainly at night. This is controlled by two opposing hormones from the hypothalamus: GHRH steps on the gas, somatostatin hits the brakes. Growth hormone itself breaks down fat – especially the fat between the organs in the abdominal cavity (visceral fat) – and stimulates the liver to produce IGF-1, which mediates many of its anabolic effects.

Under certain HIV therapies, especially the older ones, some people accumulate a striking amount of abdominal fat while their arms and legs tend to become thinner. This redistribution of fat is called lipodystrophy. In those affected, growth hormone secretion is often reduced. The idea behind tesamorelin: instead of injecting growth hormone from outside, you stimulate the body's own pituitary. Because the body's own feedback loops remain intact, excessive levels and their side effects should be less common than with direct GH administration – an assumption that the trials support in part, but not completely.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence (search October 2026):** This book's automated search found **23** human studies in PubMed (`humanstudien_alle`) and **24** entries on ClinicalTrials.gov; one of them (sponsor "Hudson Biotech," title suffix "Mock Study") is evidently a fictitious sample entry and is not counted here, leaving **23** genuine registered studies. In addition, there are three systematic reviews with meta-analyses.

#### The approval trials

Approval is based on two almost identically designed phase 3 trials in people with HIV and increased abdominal fat. In both, participants were randomized 2:1 to receive daily subcutaneous injections of tesamorelin or a dummy medication (placebo); after 26 weeks there was an extension phase in which part of the tesamorelin group continued treatment and another part was switched to placebo.

In the first trial, with 412 participants (86% men), visceral fat measured by computed tomography decreased by 15.2% on tesamorelin and increased by 5.0% on placebo. Triglycerides (blood fats) fell by 50 mg/dL compared with an increase of 9 mg/dL. IGF-1 rose by 81.0%. Blood glucose values did not differ significantly; however, more participants in the tesamorelin group dropped out because of side effects (Falutz 2007, PMID 18057338). Over 52 weeks, the reduction in abdominal fat with continuous treatment remained at about −18%; after stopping, the fat returned (Falutz 2008, PMID 18690162).

The second trial, with 404 participants, confirmed this: −10.9% visceral fat on tesamorelin compared with −0.6% on placebo after six months, no change in subcutaneous fat on the arms, legs and abdomen, and a rapid loss of the effect after switching to placebo (Falutz 2010, PMID 20101189).

The pooled analysis of both trials with 806 people showed a treatment effect of −15.4% in visceral fat after 26 weeks, improvements in triglycerides and in the ratio of total to HDL cholesterol, and in satisfaction with the appearance of the abdomen. The authors found no clinically meaningful differences in blood glucose values (Falutz 2010, PMID 20554713). Not everyone benefits equally: a follow-up analysis defined a reduction in abdominal fat of at least 8% in advance as a "response." In these responders, triglycerides fell considerably more, and blood glucose and HbA1c remained largely stable; in non-responders, fasting glucose and HbA1c rose slightly (post hoc analysis) (Stanley 2012, PMID 22495074).

#### Liver fat

The effect on fatty liver is particularly interesting. In a smaller study of 50 people with HIV, liver fat decreased along with abdominal fat; fasting glucose rose temporarily after two weeks, but after six months there was no longer a significant difference (Stanley 2014, PMID 25038357). A subsequent randomized, double-blind trial specifically studied 61 people with HIV and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease over twelve months with liver biopsies. The liver fat fraction fell by 4.1 percentage points more in absolute terms than on placebo, 37% in relative terms; 35% of the tesamorelin group but only 4% of the placebo group were below the fatty liver threshold of 5% at the end (Stanley 2019, PMID 31611038). Whether this prevents liver cirrhosis or liver cancer in the long term has not been studied.

#### Other uses

Outside of HIV, only smaller studies are available:

- In 60 people with abdominal obesity and reduced GH secretion (without HIV), tesamorelin lowered visceral fat, triglycerides, C-reactive protein and the thickness of the carotid artery wall over twelve months without worsening blood glucose values (Makimura 2012, PMID 23015655).
- In 53 people with type 2 diabetes, neither insulin response nor HbA1c changed over twelve weeks compared with placebo (Clemmons 2017, PMID 28617838).
- In a study of 152 older adults (55 to 87 years), 66 of whom had mild cognitive impairment, a favorable effect on the overall rating of cognitive performance, especially on executive functions, was seen after 20 weeks. Side effects – mostly mild – were reported by 68% on tesamorelin compared with 36% on placebo (Baker 2012, PMID 22869065). A confirmatory trial is lacking.
- In 73 people with HIV, abdominal obesity and cognitive impairment, an open-label phase 2 trial found no significant difference in cognitive performance compared with standard care (Ellis 2025, PMID 39813152).

#### Meta-analyses

Two meta-analyses from 2026 summarize the randomized trials in HIV lipodystrophy. One (four trials, 909 people) found a reduction in visceral fat, in waist circumference by 1.61 cm and in trunk fat, as well as an increase in lean mass of 1.42 kg; the rate of discontinuation due to side effects tended to be higher (risk ratio 2.25, narrowly not statistically significant) (Ditta 2026, PMID 42538058). The other (five trials) confirmed the reduction in liver fat by 4.28 percentage points and named joint and muscle pain, abnormal sensations (paresthesia) and skin reactions at the injection site as typical side effects (Badran 2026, PMID 41545261).

### Animal and laboratory studies

For tesamorelin itself there is comparatively little independent animal research; its development built on the well-studied endogenous GHRH. A review discusses whether stimulating the growth hormone axis could promote the regeneration of injured nerves, relying mainly on animal and mechanistic studies (Tuffaha 2016, PMID 27192539). A small phase 2 trial on this in humans is registered (NCT03150511).

A tissue analysis of the liver biopsies from the fatty liver trial found that on tesamorelin, gene sets for energy production in the mitochondria were more active and gene sets for inflammation, tissue remodeling and cell division were less active than on placebo (Fourman 2020, PMID 32701508). These are findings from human tissue samples, but not clinical endpoints.

### Risks and side effects

**Established from the trials:**

- Reactions at the injection site (redness, itching) – more frequent than on placebo in the fatty liver trial, but not serious (Stanley 2019, PMID 31611038).
- Typical growth hormone effects such as joint and muscle pain and abnormal sensations (Badran 2026, PMID 41545261).
- Blood glucose: in the large trials no clinically relevant worsening on average, but a temporary rise in fasting glucose in the first weeks (Stanley 2014, PMID 25038357) and a slight increase in people whose abdominal fat did not respond (Stanley 2012, PMID 22495074). People with diabetes should be medically monitored.
- More treatment discontinuations due to side effects than on placebo (Falutz 2007, PMID 18057338).
- The effect is not permanent: after stopping, the abdominal fat returns (Falutz 2008, PMID 18690162).

**Open or theoretical:** Tesamorelin raises IGF-1 markedly – by 81% in the first phase 3 trial. High IGF-1 levels are associated in observational studies with a somewhat higher risk of some types of cancer (details in the introduction to this group and in Part IV). No increased cancer risk can be read from the tesamorelin trials themselves, but they were too short for that. A long-term observational study by the manufacturer with 391 participants (NCT01579695) and a study on diabetic retinopathy (NCT01591902) were terminated early; I have not found published results for them in PubMed. According to the prescribing information, tesamorelin should not be used in people with active cancer; details are governed by the prescribing information, and its use belongs in medical hands.

### Legal status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **EU:** not approved. Tesamorelin is not marketed as a finished medicinal product in any EU member state.
- **Germany and Austria:** no approval. At most, it could be obtained through a physician-supervised individual import of a medicine approved abroad; supplying gray-market products for use in humans is not permitted.
- **USA:** approved as Egrifta (prescription only) exclusively for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-associated lipodystrophy. Any other use – for example "for weight loss" or "against aging" – is off-label.
- **WADA:** prohibited (S2), in and out of competition. Anti-doping laboratories have developed detection methods for tesamorelin and other GHRH analogs in urine (Memdouh 2021, PMID 34665524).

What is *established*, therefore, is only that research continues in small phase 2 trials. *Author's assessment:* Fatty liver outside of HIV would be the most obvious new area of use, but there other agents with much larger trial programs are now competing. An approval in the EU is not foreseeable in the near future.

### Conclusion

Tesamorelin is the best-studied peptide of the growth hormone axis: two large phase 3 trials show that in people with HIV it reduces visceral abdominal fat by about 15 to 18% and also reduces liver fat. The effect lasts only as long as treatment continues, and not everyone responds. For healthy people, athletes, or as an "anti-aging" agent, there are no robust data. Long-term safety, especially with regard to the elevated IGF-1, has not been conclusively established.
