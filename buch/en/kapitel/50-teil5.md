# Part V – The Future

The preceding parts have described what is known about peptides today. This final part looks ahead: Which agents are in the large trials that will end in the next few years? Will injections be replaced by tablets? What do expiring patents mean for prices and access? And what developments can be expected by 2035?

Statements about the future are strictly separated in this book. **Established** is what has been entered in a trial registry, published in a peer-reviewed study, or decided by authorities. Even that is only a snapshot: trials are extended, scaled down, or terminated, and planned completion dates often shift by years. Everything else is the **author's assessment** and is explicitly labeled as such. The research reflects the status as of October 2026.

## The Pipeline: What Is in Phase 2 and 3

The pharmaceutical industry uses the term "pipeline" to refer to the totality of agents in development. The stages are explained in Part I: in **phase 1**, an agent is usually tested for tolerability in a small number of healthy people; in **phase 2**, in a few hundred patients for efficacy and appropriate dose; in **phase 3**, in thousands for approval. **Phase 4** refers to studies of drugs that have already been approved. If you want to know what could come onto the market in the next five years, you need to look above all at phase 3.

### How the analysis was done

The basis is the registry listings of ClinicalTrials.gov, which were queried for the 58 agents in this book in October 2026 (see Methodology in the appendix). A small analysis program filtered out all entries that

- describe an **interventional** study, that is, a study in which an agent is deliberately administered (not purely observational), and
- are marked as **ongoing**: recruiting, active but not recruiting, not yet recruiting, or enrolling by invitation only.

Studies that appear under several agents – for example, a comparison of retatrutide with tirzepatide – were counted only once.

**What was excluded – and why:**

- **Eight entries from the sponsor "Hudson Biotech"** (one each for BPC-157, GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, MOTS-c, retatrutide, TB-500, tesamorelin, and tirzepatide). One entry from this sponsor describes itself in its own description as a fictitious example (see the chapters BPC-157 and TB-500). None of these entries is counted as a genuine study in this book.
- **All 1,000 hits for the search term "gonadorelin."** They overwhelmingly concern synthetic variants of the hormone GnRH (such as leuprorelin or triptorelin) in the treatment of prostate and breast cancer or in fertility medicine – that is, other drugs. The few entries on natural GnRH are discussed in the chapter Gonadorelin; none of them is an ongoing phase 3 trial.

**What was not excluded, but checked:** For glutathione, collagen, and carnosine, the search terms are so general that many hits mention the substance only as a component of a mixture, as a comparator, or as a measured parameter. For example, a large dengue study appears under glutathione because it tests N-acetylcysteine – a precursor of glutathione (NCT07543458). Under larazotide, two studies on an entirely different agent for Fabry disease appear (NCT04020055, NCT06904261). These hits are included in the total figures but are not counted as peptide studies in the assessment.

**Two limitations of the analysis:** First, not all sponsors report the completion of their study promptly; some entries with a completion date in 2025 are still listed as "ongoing" in the registry. Second, the query covers only the 58 agents in this book. New molecules without their own chapter appear only if they are tested against one of these agents. This is why a targeted additional search was carried out for the new mechanisms of action further below.

Of the 157 ongoing phase 3 trials, 68 were still recruiting, 54 were active without further enrollment, and 35 had not yet started. Adding the combined phase 2/3 studies, there are 176 late-stage studies; 157 of them (142 in phase 3, 15 in phase 2/3) – almost nine out of ten – belong to Group A.

In Group A, roughly one in three of these late-stage studies is run by universities and hospitals, the rest by companies. The planned completion dates are distributed as follows: 21 studies are scheduled to end in 2026, 49 in 2027, 41 in 2028, and 44 between 2029 and 2033. The real wave of new results is therefore still to come in 2027 and 2028.

**Groups B to F** look completely different. After reviewing the entries, only five ongoing phase 3 trials remain in which an agent from this book is actually the investigational product: thymosin alpha-1 after colorectal cancer surgery (NCT05086614), thymosin beta-4 eye drops for neurotrophic keratopathy (NCT05555589), elamipretide for dry macular degeneration (NCT06373731), afamelanotide for vitiligo (NCT06109649), and ibutamoren, which is not a peptide, in children with growth hormone deficiency (NCT06948214). In addition, there are some combined phase 2/3 studies, for example on oxytocin nasal spray for chronic pain (NCT04903002) and on thymosin alpha-1 for recurrent implantation failure after in vitro fertilization (NCT07675980). For the "research peptides" most popular on the internet – BPC-157, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, Epitalon, Semax, Selank – not a single phase 3 trial is ongoing.

### What the registry reveals about the direction

**From the scale to hard endpoints.** The first generation of weight-loss trials mainly measured kilograms. The large trials of the coming years ask whether heart attacks, strokes, heart failure, kidney failure, or liver cirrhosis become less frequent. All six ongoing phase 3 trials in Group A with more than 5,000 participants each are such outcome trials. For health insurers deciding on reimbursement, these results are more important than any record in weight loss.

**Head-to-head comparisons.** New agents increasingly have to measure up against the established ones, not just against placebo: retatrutide against tirzepatide (NCT06662383), zenagamtide against semaglutide (AMAZE 7, NCT07668414), maridebart cafraglutide against semaglutide in type 2 diabetes (MARITIME-T2D-3, NCT07866066). New companies are also competing directly: Roche is testing the GLP-1/GIP dual agonist enicepatide against tirzepatide (NCT07889648), the company Kailera the agent ribupatide against semaglutide (KaiNETIC-3, NCT07284979), and AstraZeneca the once-daily oral agent elecoglipron against the semaglutide tablet (Eluminate-2, NCT07662213).

**Maintaining weight rather than just losing it.** Several studies explicitly examine the period after weight loss: retatrutide for weight maintenance (TRIUMPH-6, NCT06859268), a long-term CagriSema study over two years plus extension (NCT06780449), switching from other GLP-1 drugs to the monthly maridebart cafraglutide (NCT07575399) or to berobenatide, a monthly injectable investigational agent from Pfizer (NCT07845500). The background is studies such as SURMOUNT-4: those who stopped tirzepatide after 36 weeks regained an average of 14.0% in the following year, while weight fell by a further 5.5% with continued treatment (Aronne 2024, PMID 38078870).

**New countries, new molecules.** A large proportion of the new phase 3 trials comes from Chinese companies testing their own GLP-1-based agents against semaglutide or dulaglutide – for example, bofanglutide, injected every two weeks (BALANCE-OBS, NCT07622810), or the exendin-4 fusion protein JY09 (NCT06257966). The market is therefore becoming broader, not just stronger.

**Children and adolescents.** For almost all major agents, studies are ongoing in adolescents, and in some cases also in children, for example for tirzepatide (NCT06439277, NCT07817875), orforglipron (NCT06672939), and CagriSema (NCT07253285, planned completion 2033).

**And outside metabolism?** Here the pipeline is thin. Where a peptide cannot be patented, is already sold as a dietary supplement, or exists only on the gray market, hardly anyone finances expensive approval trials. This explains why the very peptides most discussed on the internet are practically absent from the registries.

### New mechanisms of action

#### Triple agonists

Retatrutide activates three receptors at once: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. The glucagon component is intended to increase energy expenditure and fat breakdown in the liver. In the phase 2 trial with 338 adults, weight fell by 24.2% after 48 weeks at the highest dose studied, compared with 2.1% on placebo (Jastreboff 2023, PMID 37366315). The first phase 3 trial, TRIUMPH-1, with 2,339 adults confirmed this: after 80 weeks, weight loss was 17.6 to 25.0% depending on dose, compared with 3.9% on placebo. In participants with knee osteoarthritis, pain decreased, and in those with sleep apnea, the number of breathing pauses (Jastreboff 2026, PMID 42814954). Whether this additional weight loss also leads to fewer cardiac and kidney events is to be shown by TRIUMPH-Outcomes by 2029 (NCT06383390). Details and side effects are described in the chapter Retatrutide.

#### Amylin: the fourth hormone pathway

Amylin is released together with insulin and acts in the brainstem to promote satiety. Three lines of development can be distinguished:

- **Combination in one injection:** CagriSema combines the amylin analog cagrilintide with semaglutide. In REDEFINE 1 with 3,417 adults, weight fell by 20.4% after 68 weeks, compared with 3.0% on placebo; gastrointestinal complaints occurred in 79.6% versus 39.9% (Garvey 2025, PMID 40544433).
- **One molecule for two receptors:** Amycretin, since 2026 zenagamtide, activates GLP-1 and amylin receptors simultaneously. In a small phase 1b/2a study with 125 participants, weight fell by 24.3% after 36 weeks at the highest dose level (Dahl 2025, PMID 40550231). The phase 3 program AMAZE has been running since 2026.
- **Amylin alone:** Here the hope is better tolerability. Eloralintide, a selective amylin receptor agonist from Lilly, led to 9 to 20% weight loss after 48 weeks in a phase 2 trial with 263 adults, compared with 0.4% on placebo; nausea and fatigue were, depending on the dose, considerably more frequent than with placebo (Billings 2025, PMID 41207310). Petrelintide from Zealand Pharma lowered weight by 7.9 to 9.8% after 28 weeks in the phase 2 trial ZUPREME 1 with 493 participants (placebo 1.7%). Nausea occurred in 20% versus 6%, but vomiting was not more frequent than with placebo (Garvey 2026, PMID 42810355).

Both pure amylin agents are now in phase 3: eloralintide in ENLIGHTEN-1 with 1,980 planned participants (NCT07321886, completion July 2030), petrelintide in ZUPREME-3 with 3,900 planned participants under the leadership of Roche (NCT07843498, completion March 2029). Lilly is also testing a combination of eloralintide and tirzepatide (AMPLIFY-1, NCT07881432, 2,115 planned participants).

#### Antibody–peptide conjugates

Peptides are rapidly broken down in the body and excreted via the kidneys. One solution is to couple them to an antibody that remains in the blood for weeks. A review describes such antibody–peptide conjugates as a distinct new class of agents: unlike fusion proteins, the peptide is produced chemically and only then attached to the antibody, so that it can also contain unusual building blocks (Yie 2026, PMID 42592044).

The best-known example is maridebart cafraglutide (MariTide). It combines an antibody that blocks the GIP receptor with GLP-1-like peptides that activate the GLP-1 receptor. In the phase 2 trial with 592 participants, it was injected every four or eight weeks. After 52 weeks, weight loss without diabetes was 12.3 to 16.2% (placebo 2.5%), and with type 2 diabetes 8.4 to 12.3% (placebo 1.7%). Gastrointestinal complaints were common, but less frequent when the dose was increased slowly (Jastreboff 2025, PMID 40549887). The concept is: fewer injections rather than maximum effect.

#### Preserving muscle during weight loss

With any substantial weight loss, muscle and bone mass are also lost. One approach is the antibody **bimagrumab**, which blocks activin type II receptors – switches that slow muscle growth. Bimagrumab is not a peptide, but it is being specifically combined with peptide drugs.

- In a phase 2 trial with 75 adults with type 2 diabetes and overweight, bimagrumab alone reduced fat mass by 20.5% after 48 weeks, while fat-free mass increased by 3.6% (placebo −0.5% and −0.8%) (Heymsfield 2021, PMID 33439265).
- In a phase 2 trial with 507 adults with obesity, weight loss after 48 weeks was 9.3 kg with bimagrumab alone, 14.2 kg with semaglutide alone, and 17.8 kg with the combination (placebo 3.3 kg). Common side effects of bimagrumab were muscle cramps, diarrhea, and acne (Heymsfield 2026, PMID 41772149).

Lilly is now testing bimagrumab with tirzepatide (NCT06643728, 252 participants, completion January 2027). A smaller study at Massachusetts General Hospital is examining the same combination with regard to body composition and bone (NCT05933499). Whether more muscle mass on a scan also means more strength, fewer falls, or fewer fractures has not yet been demonstrated. No phase 3 trials are listed in the registry.

#### Tablets

Perhaps the most consequential change concerns not the receptor but the route into the body: tablets containing peptides and small molecules that act like peptides. The next chapter is devoted to this topic.

### Key points in brief

- After excluding fictitious and unrelated entries, 730 interventional studies are ongoing on the agents in this book, including 157 in phase 3.
- Almost nine out of ten late-stage studies concern metabolism and weight. For the popular gray-market peptides, not a single phase 3 trial is ongoing.
- The large cardiovascular, kidney, and liver trials will end between 2027 and 2032. Only they will show whether the new agents achieve more than lowering weight.
- New mechanisms of action – triple agonists, amylin, antibody–peptide conjugates, muscle preservation – are supported by phase 2 trials, and most are in phase 3. Approval is not assured for any of these new agents.
