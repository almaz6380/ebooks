# Important Notice

### No Substitute for Medical Advice

This book is intended solely for general information and education. It does not replace medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Before you make decisions about your health or start, stop, or change a medication, please talk to a physician or a pharmacist. If you have acute symptoms, contact an emergency medical service or call the emergency number.

### No Recommendations for Use

This book deliberately contains no recommendations on the use of peptides, no dosages, no instructions for taking or injecting them, no usage cycles, and no recommendations for combinations. When a dose is mentioned in the text, it describes only what was investigated in a specific study. It is not a recommendation and cannot be applied to your personal situation. For approved medicines, the summary of product characteristics (prescribing information), the patient leaflet, and medical supervision are authoritative.

### Many Substances Are Not Approved

Many of the peptides described in this book are **not** approved as medicines in the European Union, in Austria, in Germany, or in the USA. For many of them, only animal or laboratory studies are available. The presentation of such substances in this book is not a statement about their efficacy or safety in humans and is not an invitation to use them. Products offered on the internet as "research peptides" or "for research purposes only" may be incorrectly dosed, contaminated, or counterfeit. Using them can have serious health and legal consequences.

### Doping

Many of the peptides described are on the Prohibited List of the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA). For athletes who are subject to doping controls, their use can lead to suspensions. In Austria and Germany, trafficking, passing on, and in certain cases even possessing doping substances can be a criminal offense.

### Transparency: How This Book Was Created

This book was researched and written with the support of artificial intelligence (AI) and subsequently revised by the author. The AI was used to search for studies in the PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov databases, to evaluate study summaries, and to produce draft texts.

To avoid errors, strict rules apply: Every study mentioned is cited with its PubMed number (PMID) or its ClinicalTrials.gov registry number (NCT number). Every figure comes from the summary of the respective publication or from the registry entry. All cited numbers were additionally checked automatically against the databases. This allows you to look up every source yourself and verify the information. How to do this is explained in the following section; details on the research can be found in the appendix "Methodology."

# About This Book

### What This Book Is For

Peptides are on everyone's lips. Weight-loss injections such as semaglutide and tirzepatide have changed the treatment of overweight, and dozens of other peptides are offered on the internet – for faster healing, more muscle, better skin, more energy, or a longer life. Between the promises made in forums, on social media, and in online shops and what has actually been studied in humans, there is often a wide gap.

This book aims to make this gap visible. For each peptide covered, it sets out soberly which human studies exist, what they show and what they do not, which risks are known, and what the legal situation is. It compares the peptides with one another and assesses how robust the evidence is in each case. It is meant to help you critically examine claims and have informed conversations with physicians and pharmacies.

### Who This Book Is Written For

- For **interested lay readers** who want to know what lies behind the peptide boom.
- For people who have been prescribed an **approved peptide medication** or are considering one and want to understand the evidence.
- For **athletes** and coaches who want to assess advertising claims and doping risks.
- For **professionals** in pharmacy, medicine, nutrition counseling, cosmetics, and journalism who are looking for a quick, source-based overview.

No prior knowledge is required. Technical terms are explained when they first appear and are also listed in the glossary.

### How the Book Is Structured

**Part I – Fundamentals** explains what peptides are, how peptide medicine has developed, how peptides get into the body, how to read studies, what happens on the gray market, and which legal framework applies. If you are not familiar with these topics, you should start here.

**Part II – The Peptides** presents the individual substances in six groups: metabolism and weight; healing, regeneration, and the immune system; the growth hormone axis; brain, sleep, and longevity; hormones, sexuality, and pigmentation; collagen, nutrition, and cosmetics. Each chapter follows the same structure: profile, mechanism of action, human studies, animal and laboratory studies, risks and side effects, legal status, ongoing studies and outlook, conclusion. This makes it easy for you to compare peptides with one another and to look things up in a targeted way.

**Part III – The Big Comparison** places all peptides side by side and compares the GLP-1 medications directly.

**Part IV – Risks** summarizes side effects, cancer and immune risks, problems with gray-market products, and special risk groups.

**Part V – The Future** looks at the development pipeline, new dosage forms, patent expirations, and prices. Established facts and assessments are clearly separated.

In the **appendix** you will find the glossary, the research methodology, and the index of the studies evaluated.

### The Evidence Levels

Each peptide is assigned an evidence level in its profile. It describes how good the data are **in humans**:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | several large randomized studies in humans, approval as a medicine |
| B | individual randomized studies in humans |
| C | only small or uncontrolled studies in humans |
| D | only animal and laboratory studies |

The evidence level is not a recommendation and not a statement about whether a substance is safe. Even a substance at level A has side effects, and a substance at level D is not necessarily ineffective – it simply has not yet been adequately studied in humans. What lies behind the terms "randomized," "controlled," or "surrogate endpoint" is explained in the chapter "Reading Studies Correctly."

### How Sources Are Cited

Each study is cited in the text with its first author, year of publication, and PubMed number, for example:

(Wilding 2021, PMID 33567185)

Registered studies, especially ongoing ones, are cited with their number in the US study registry ClinicalTrials.gov, for example:

(NCT03548935)

There are no footnotes and no separate bibliography at the end of each chapter; the complete index of studies is in the appendix.

### How to Look Up a Source

**PMID**: The PubMed identifier is a unique number for each entry in PubMed, the freely accessible literature database of the US National Library of Medicine.

1. Open the page pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov in your browser.
2. Enter the number in the search field, for example 33567185, and start the search.
3. You will be taken directly to the entry with the title, authors, journal, and usually a summary (abstract).

Alternatively, you can append the number directly to the address: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33567185. Many entries contain a link to the full text, which is sometimes freely available and sometimes available only for a fee.

**NCT number**: You can find registered studies on clinicaltrials.gov by entering the number in the search field or appending it to the address clinicaltrials.gov/study/, for example clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03548935. There you will see the study design, number of participants, endpoints, and current status.

### What This Book Deliberately Does Not Contain

- **No dosages or instructions for use.** There is no information on how much, how often, or for how long a peptide should be used, no instructions for reconstituting or injecting, and no combinations of several substances. For approved medicines, the book refers to the prescribing information and medical supervision.
- **No sources of supply.** No shops, manufacturers, pharmacies, or countries where peptides can be purchased are named, and no hints are given on how to circumvent controls.
- **No promises of cures.** The book reports what was observed in studies – not what a substance will do for you.
- **No product recommendations.** Neither brands nor suppliers are recommended. Brand names of approved medicines are mentioned only when they are necessary for understanding.

### A Final Word

This book is a tool for reflection, not a guide to self-treatment. If you have more questions after reading it than before, that is not a bad sign: often the most honest answer to the question "Does this peptide work?" is "Nobody knows yet."
