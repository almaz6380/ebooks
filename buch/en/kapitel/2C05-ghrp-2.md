## GHRP-2 (Pralmorelin)

GHRP-2 is a small synthetic peptide of six amino acids that stimulates the pituitary gland to release growth hormone more powerfully than almost any other known stimulus. This potency has made it an approved medicine in Japan – not as a therapy but as a test substance: a single injection can determine whether an adult has growth hormone deficiency. On the gray market, the same peptide is sold as a "muscle and anti-aging peptide." There is no evidence for that.

### Profile

- **Type and structure:** synthetic hexapeptide (D-Ala-D-2-Nal-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH2) with several non-natural amino acids; development names KP-102 and GPA-748, international nonproprietary name pralmorelin (Doi 2004, PMID 15646370).
- **Origin:** developed in the 1990s from the series of GH-releasing peptides founded by Cyril Bowers (Tulane University, USA); rights held by Kaken (Japan) (anonymous drug profile in Drugs R D 2004, PMID 15230633).
- **Mechanism of action:** agonist at the ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a) in the pituitary gland and hypothalamus; releases growth hormone and, to a lesser extent, ACTH, cortisol and prolactin; increases appetite.
- **EU approval:** no. **US approval:** no. **Japan:** approved as a diagnostic agent for pituitary function (as of 2026, please check).
- **WADA:** prohibited, class S2 – GHRP-2 (pralmorelin) is explicitly named as a growth hormone–releasing peptide.
- **Evidence level: B** – numerous small randomized studies on hormone physiology and a validation study as a diagnostic agent; no randomized trial of a therapeutic benefit.

### How it is supposed to work

GHRP-2 mimics ghrelin, the hunger hormone from the stomach that also controls growth hormone secretion (Kojima 1999, PMID 10604470). Historically, the order was reversed: the synthetic GHRPs came first, their receptor was found, and only then was ghrelin discovered as its natural key.

GHRP-2 acts on two levels: directly on the pituitary gland and indirectly via the hypothalamus, where it weakens the brake somatostatin and supports the body's own GHRH. For its full effect it therefore requires an intact hypothalamus: in rats without a pituitary gland it had no effect at all, and in rats with a severed connection between the hypothalamus and pituitary only a weakened effect (Doi 2004, PMID 15646370). When GHRP-2 and GHRH are given together, the release is considerably greater than the sum of the individual effects.

### Human studies

**Scope of the evidence (search October 2026):** This book's automated search found **55** human studies in PubMed (`humanstudien_alle`) and **no** entry on ClinicalTrials.gov. The large number is deceptive: most papers (especially from a research group at the Mayo Clinic) use GHRP-2 as a tool to study the regulation of growth hormone secretion in women and men, under sex hormones or in old age. They say nothing about a health benefit.

#### Diagnosis of growth hormone deficiency

The most important use is the GHRP-2 test. In a validation study, 77 healthy people and 58 patients with confirmed severe growth hormone deficiency received a single intravenous dose. The GH peak averaged 84.6 µg/L in the healthy people and 1.36 µg/L in those affected. The test was highly reproducible and was only slightly influenced by age and excess weight; a cutoff of 15 µg/L corresponded to the customary cutoff of the insulin hypoglycemia test (Chihara 2007, PMID 17609397). In a comparison in 71 people with pituitary tumors, the GHRP-2 test achieved a sensitivity of 81.3% and a specificity of 94.5% relative to the insulin test (Kinoshita 2013, PMID 23079545).

Because GHRP-2 also releases ACTH, it was examined whether the test can additionally detect underactivity of the adrenal axis. The results are mixed: in a study of 47 people it achieved a sensitivity of 89.7% (Arimura 2016, PMID 27020037); in a larger analysis of 254 people it was not sufficiently reliable overall (sensitivity 64%, specificity 79%), performing well only in men without a hormone-secreting tumor (Hayakawa 2018, PMID 30324179).

#### Treatment attempts in children

In the 1990s, GHRP-2 was tested as a therapy for children of short stature. In six children with growth hormone deficiency, nighttime GH secretion rose dose-dependently, but IGF-1 did not; growth velocity was higher during treatment (Mericq 1998, PMID 9661608). As a nasal spray, GHRP-2 increased growth velocity in 15 children from 3.7 to 6.1 cm per year, again without a rise in IGF-1 (Pihoker 1997, PMID 9390009). Of ten children who received GHRP-2 as a tablet for twelve months, seven reported markedly increased appetite in the first months (Mericq 2003, PMID 14513874). Further development as a therapy was discontinued in the United States (anonymous drug profile in Drugs R D 2004, PMID 15230633).

#### Appetite and intensive care

In 19 lean and overweight adults, an infusion of GHRP-2 dose-dependently increased freely chosen food intake by 10.2% and 33.5%, respectively, compared with placebo (Laferrère 2006, PMID 16861611). In 14 long-stay intensive care patients, a five-day infusion of GHRP-2 together with thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) reactivated the suppressed GH and thyroid axes and shifted metabolic markers toward anabolism (Van den Berghe 1999, PMID 10199772). Whether this changes the chances of survival was never tested in an outcome trial.

### Animal and laboratory studies

In conscious rats, GHRP-2 released more growth hormone than GHRH and was also effective in dogs, in which GHRH failed; in rats it increased ACTH and corticosterone but not prolactin, and accelerated growth over three weeks (Doi 2004, PMID 15646370). In pigs, GHRP-2 was particularly potent in a comparative study but, unlike ipamorelin, also released ACTH and cortisol (Raun 1998, PMID 9849822).

### Risks and side effects

**Established:** In the studies, GHRP-2 was well tolerated in the short term. Repeatedly described are increased appetite (Mericq 2003, PMID 14513874; Laferrère 2006, PMID 16861611) and the release of ACTH and cortisol (Arimura 2016, PMID 27020037). A review of GH releasers also mentions a possible rise in blood glucose due to reduced insulin sensitivity and calls for long-term data on cancer and mortality (Sigalos 2018, PMID 28400207).

**Unknown:** There are no data on use over months or years in adults. On the question of whether persistently elevated growth hormone/IGF-1 increases cancer risk, see the introduction to this group and Part IV.

**Gray market:** "Gly-GHRP-2," that is, GHRP-2 with an additional, undeclared amino acid, has been found in seized products (Krug 2018, PMID 29864719; Gajda 2019, PMID 30136411).

### Legal status

*As of 2026, please check for updates.*

- **Japan:** approved as a diagnostic agent; used exclusively in the context of medical function tests.
- **EU, Germany, Austria:** no approval; supplying it for use in humans is prohibited.
- **USA:** no approval.
- **WADA:** prohibited (S2). After administration as a nasal spray to a volunteer, GHRP-2 and its metabolites were detectable in urine for up to 47 hours (Semenistaya 2015, PMID 25869809).

### Ongoing studies and outlook

As of October 2026, no study with GHRP-2 or pralmorelin is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. *Author's assessment:* GHRP-2 will remain a diagnostic tool in Japan and a tool of hormone research. In other countries, as a test substance it competes with macimorelin, which is available in oral form.

### Conclusion

GHRP-2 is one of the most potent known triggers of growth hormone secretion and is established in Japan as a diagnostic agent for growth hormone deficiency. Treatment attempts in children showed only modest effects and were not pursued further. For muscle building, fat loss or "anti-aging" in healthy people there are no studies; what is documented, by contrast, are side effects such as food cravings and activation of stress hormones.
