## Tirzepatide

### Profile

- **Type/structure:** synthetic peptide whose backbone is modeled on the body's own gut hormone GIP; an attached fatty acid chain binds it to albumin in the blood and extends its duration of action to the point that one injection per week is sufficient.
- **Origin:** developed by Eli Lilly, listed under the code LY3298176 in early studies.
- **Mechanism of action:** dual agonist (activator) at two receptors: the GIP receptor and the GLP-1 receptor. Hence also called a "twincretin" or "dual incretin agonist."
- **EU approval:** yes (Mounjaro) for type 2 diabetes and for weight management in obesity and in overweight with comorbidities.
- **US approval:** yes (Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes; Zepbound for obesity and for moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in obesity).
- **WADA:** not on the Prohibited List (status as of 2026; please check for current information).
- **Evidence level:** **A** – numerous large randomized trials, outcomes trials on the heart and heart failure, approval in many countries.

### How it is supposed to work

After a meal, the gut releases two hormones known as incretins: GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) and GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide). Both tell the pancreas "food is coming" and enhance insulin release – but only as long as blood sugar is elevated. This is why incretin medications on their own rarely cause hypoglycemia. GLP-1 additionally slows gastric emptying and acts on satiety centers in the brain. The role GIP plays in appetite is less clear and was long disputed; in some older papers, GIP was even regarded as more of a "fattening hormone."

Tirzepatide combines both signals in a single molecule. Laboratory studies in cell lines revealed two special features (Willard 2020, PMID 32730231): first, tirzepatide is "imbalanced" – at the concentrations reached with therapeutic doses, it occupies the GIP receptor more strongly than the GLP-1 receptor. Second, at the GLP-1 receptor it preferentially activates a particular signaling pathway (cAMP) and triggers less receptor internalization, in which the receptor is drawn into the cell interior and thus temporarily switched off. Whether this "biased" signaling plays a role in humans is a hypothesis based on laboratory data.

In overweight mice, tirzepatide improved insulin sensitivity more than a pure GLP-1 agonist, partly independently of weight loss – mediated via the GIP receptor and better glucose uptake in adipose tissue (Samms 2021, PMID 34003802). In humans, tirzepatide mainly reduces food intake; in a study of 117 people with type 2 diabetes, appetite, energy intake, and fat mass decreased on tirzepatide compared with placebo. In terms of appetite, however, it did not differ measurably from semaglutide, even though weight loss was greater – the authors therefore consider additional mechanisms possible (Heise 2023, PMID 36857477).

Put simply: tirzepatide acts like a GLP-1 medication with an additional GIP "amplifier." The studies show that this package achieves more for weight and blood sugar than the pure GLP-1 agonists available previously – exactly why is not yet fully understood.

### Human studies

**Scale of the evidence:** The automated PubMed query (October 2026) yielded 144 publications classified as human studies with "tirzepatide" in the title or abstract, plus a very large number of reviews and meta-analyses. ClinicalTrials.gov listed 307 registered studies, more than 100 of them still recruiting (an obviously fictitious sample entry from an unknown sponsor is not counted). Tirzepatide is thus among the best-studied drugs in this book. The main weight is borne by two approval programs: **SURPASS** (type 2 diabetes) and **SURMOUNT** (obesity), supplemented by studies on heart failure, sleep apnea, and fatty liver disease.

#### From the Laboratory to the Clinic: Phase 1 and 2

The first human data come from a combined phase 1 study with 142 people who received tirzepatide, dulaglutide, or placebo. In people with type 2 diabetes, higher doses markedly lowered fasting blood sugar after four weeks; the most common side effects affected the gastrointestinal tract (Coskun 2018, PMID 30473097).

In the phase 2 trial, 318 people with type 2 diabetes received one of four tirzepatide doses, dulaglutide, or placebo for 26 weeks. Long-term blood sugar (HbA1c) fell in a dose-dependent manner by up to 1.94 percentage points (dulaglutide: −1.21; placebo: −0.06). Body weight changed by −0.9 to −11.3 kg on tirzepatide and by −2.7 kg on dulaglutide (Frias 2018, PMID 30293770). This magnitude of weight loss – in a diabetes trial – was the impetus for the large obesity program.

#### SURPASS: Type 2 Diabetes

The SURPASS program tested tirzepatide at three doses (5, 10, and 15 mg per week) against placebo, against semaglutide, and against insulins. HbA1c reflects average blood sugar over the past two to three months; a value below 7% is considered the treatment target for many people with diabetes.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| SURPASS-1 (Rosenstock 2021), PMID 34186022 | 478 adults, diet/exercise only, vs. placebo | 40 weeks | HbA1c −1.87 to −2.07 points vs. +0.04; weight −7.0 to −9.5 kg |
| SURPASS-2 (Frías 2021), PMID 34170647 | 1,879 adults on metformin, open-label vs. semaglutide 1 mg | 40 weeks | HbA1c −2.01 to −2.30 vs. −1.86; additional weight −1.9 to −5.5 kg |
| SURPASS-3 (Ludvik 2021), PMID 34370970 | 1,444 adults, open-label vs. insulin degludec | 52 weeks | HbA1c −1.93 to −2.37 vs. −1.34; weight −7.5 to −12.9 kg vs. +2.3 kg |
| SURPASS-4 (Del Prato 2021), PMID 34672967 | 2,002 adults with high cardiovascular risk, open-label vs. insulin glargine | 52 to 104 weeks | HbA1c −2.43/−2.58 vs. −1.44; MACE-4 not increased (HR 0.74) |
| SURPASS-5 (Dahl 2022), PMID 35133415 | 475 adults on insulin glargine, vs. placebo | 40 weeks | HbA1c −2.11 to −2.40 vs. −0.86; weight −5.4 to −8.8 kg vs. +1.6 kg |
| SURPASS-PEDS (Hannon 2025), PMID 40975112 | 99 adolescents (10–17 years) with type 2 diabetes, vs. placebo | 30 weeks (+22 open-label) | HbA1c −2.23 vs. +0.05; BMI −7.4 to −11.2% |

Several points deserve emphasis:

- **In the direct comparison with semaglutide** (SURPASS-2), tirzepatide was noninferior and superior for HbA1c at all three doses. However, the trial used semaglutide at a dose of 1 mg; the higher diabetes dose approved later was not the comparator. The frequency of nausea (17–22% vs. 18%) and vomiting (6–10% vs. 8%) was similar; serious adverse events occurred in 5–7% on tirzepatide compared with 3% on semaglutide (Frías 2021, PMID 34170647).
- **Compared with insulin**, tirzepatide led to greater blood sugar reduction and at the same time to weight loss instead of weight gain. Hypoglycemia (below 54 mg/dl or severe) was considerably less frequent in SURPASS-3: 1–2% versus 7% on insulin degludec (Ludvik 2021, PMID 34370970).
- **Children and adolescents:** SURPASS-PEDS is one of the few incretin trials in youth-onset type 2 diabetes. The effect was maintained in the open-label extension up to week 52; the side effect profile corresponded to that in adults (Hannon 2025, PMID 40975112).
- **Type 1 diabetes:** A small phase 2 trial with 24 adults with type 1 diabetes and obesity showed weight loss of 10.3 kg versus 0.7 kg on placebo after twelve weeks and a 35% lower insulin requirement (Snaith 2026, PMID 41264593). Tirzepatide is not approved for type 1 diabetes; larger trials are ongoing (see Outlook).

A prespecified meta-analysis of all seven SURPASS trials lasting at least 26 weeks (4,887 people on tirzepatide, 2,328 in control groups) found no increased risk of major cardiovascular events (MACE-4, hazard ratio 0.80; 95% confidence interval 0.57–1.11) (Sattar 2022, PMID 35210595). This is a statement about safety, not benefit – for that, the outcomes trial SURPASS-CVOT was needed.

#### SURPASS-CVOT: Cardiovascular Endpoints

For diabetes medications, the authorities require proof that they do not endanger the heart. SURPASS-CVOT went one step further: instead of placebo, tirzepatide competed against dulaglutide, a GLP-1 medication that had itself reduced cardiovascular events in the REWIND trial. 13,299 people with type 2 diabetes and established atherosclerotic disease were randomized; 13,165 were analyzed (Nicholls 2025, PMID 41406444).

The primary endpoint – cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, or stroke – occurred in 801 people (12.2%) on tirzepatide and in 862 (13.1%) on dulaglutide (hazard ratio 0.92; 95.3% CI 0.83–1.01). Tirzepatide was thus **noninferior**; formal superiority was narrowly missed (p = 0.09). Gastrointestinal side effects were more frequent on tirzepatide.

A post hoc analysis using a broader endpoint of six components – death from any cause, myocardial infarction, stroke, revascularization, hospitalization for heart failure, and kidney events – found events in 23.7% versus 27.4% after a median treatment duration of almost four years (hazard ratio 0.84; 95% CI 0.79–0.90) (Nissen 2026, PMID 41903177). Post hoc analyses are hypothesis-generating; they cannot replace a prespecified primary analysis.

How large the benefit would be compared with *no* treatment cannot be read directly from a comparison against an effective medication. A prespecified but indirect analysis calculated a "placebo arm" from the data of the REWIND trial and arrived at a hazard ratio of 0.72 (95% CI 0.55–0.94) for tirzepatide versus this calculated placebo for cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, or stroke (Sattar 2026, PMID 41940793). Such model calculations are based on assumptions and do not replace a genuine placebo comparison.

#### SURMOUNT: Obesity

In the obesity program, tirzepatide was studied in people with a body mass index (BMI) of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher plus a comorbidity, always in addition to diet and exercise counseling.

| Study | Participants and design | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff 2022), PMID 35658024 | 2,539 adults without diabetes, vs. placebo | 72 weeks | −15.0 / −19.5 / −20.9% weight (5/10/15 mg) vs. −3.1% |
| SURMOUNT-2 (Garvey 2023), PMID 37385275 | 938 adults with type 2 diabetes, vs. placebo | 72 weeks | −12.8 / −14.7% vs. −3.2% |
| SURMOUNT-3 (Wadden 2023), PMID 37840095 | 579 adults after a successful 12-week lifestyle intervention | 72 weeks | additional −18.4% vs. +2.5% |
| SURMOUNT-4 (Aronne 2024), PMID 38078870 | 670 adults after 36 weeks of tirzepatide, continuation vs. discontinuation | 52 weeks (after lead-in) | −5.5% continued vs. +14.0% regain |
| SURMOUNT-5 (Aronne 2025), PMID 40353578 | 751 adults without diabetes, open-label vs. semaglutide 2.4 mg | 72 weeks | −20.2% vs. −13.7% |
| SURMOUNT-1, 3-year data (Jastreboff 2025), PMID 39536238 | 1,032 adults with obesity and prediabetes | 176 weeks | −12.3 to −19.7% vs. −1.3%; type 2 diabetes 1.3% vs. 13.3% |

**SURMOUNT-1** was the trial that made tirzepatide known as an obesity medication. With an average baseline weight of 104.8 kg, participants at the highest dose lost on average 20.9% of their body weight. 57% in the 15 mg group achieved at least 20% weight loss, compared with 3% on placebo. Because of side effects, 4.3–7.1% on tirzepatide and 2.6% on placebo discontinued treatment (Jastreboff 2022, PMID 35658024).

**People with type 2 diabetes** lose less weight than people without diabetes with all incretin medications. SURMOUNT-2 showed this as well: the difference from placebo was 9.6 and 11.6 percentage points, respectively (Garvey 2023, PMID 37385275).

**What happens after discontinuation?** SURMOUNT-4 answered this question with a randomized withdrawal design: all participants first received tirzepatide for 36 weeks and lost on average 20.9%. Then it was determined by lot who would continue to receive tirzepatide and who would unknowingly receive placebo. Over the following 52 weeks, the placebo group regained 14.0%, while the tirzepatide group lost a further 5.5%. Over the entire study period, weight loss was 25.3% versus 9.9% (Aronne 2024, PMID 38078870). The authors' message: obesity behaves like a chronic disease whose treatment usually has to be long term.

**Lower maintenance dose:** Whether one can switch to a lower dose after losing weight was investigated in SURMOUNT-MAINTAIN with 378 randomized people in the USA. After 60 weeks at the maximum dose, they were allocated to three groups. At week 112, weight loss since baseline was −21.9% (maximum dose continued), −16.6% (reduction to 5 mg), and −9.9% (placebo). Rescue therapy because of a regain of at least 50% was needed by 8%, 25%, and 67%, respectively (Horn 2026, PMID 42119587). Dose reduction is therefore an intermediate solution with very variable individual success.

**Diabetes prevention:** In the three-year analysis of SURMOUNT-1 in people with prediabetes (a precursor of diabetes), 1.3% on tirzepatide and 13.3% on placebo developed type 2 diabetes. Even 17 weeks after discontinuation, the difference remained marked (2.4% vs. 13.7%). No new safety signals emerged during these three years (Jastreboff 2025, PMID 39536238).

#### SURMOUNT-5: Tirzepatide Versus Semaglutide

The direct comparison in obesity without diabetes that many had been waiting for was delivered by SURMOUNT-5 (Aronne 2025, PMID 40353578). 751 adults received, open-label (i.e., without blinding), the highest tolerated dose of either tirzepatide (10 or 15 mg) or semaglutide (1.7 or 2.4 mg). After 72 weeks:

- Weight change: −20.2% with tirzepatide versus −13.7% with semaglutide.
- Waist circumference: −18.4 cm versus −13.0 cm.
- More participants on tirzepatide reached each of the thresholds of 10, 15, 20, and 25%.
- Side effects in both groups were predominantly gastrointestinal complaints, mostly mild to moderate and mainly during the dose-escalation phase.

A follow-up analysis showed that 44% on tirzepatide and 21% on semaglutide had already lost at least 15% by week 24 ("early responders"). These had somewhat more gastrointestinal and biliary events but did not discontinue treatment more often (Aronne 2026, PMID 41865857).

For context: SURMOUNT-5 was funded by the manufacturer of tirzepatide and conducted open-label. Nevertheless, the difference is large enough that it can hardly be explained by these limitations alone. A comparison of all GLP-1 medications can be found in Part III.

#### Heart Failure: SUMMIT

Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) is a common form of heart failure in which the heart pumps out normally but fills poorly. Obesity is an important driver. In SUMMIT, 731 people with HFpEF and a BMI of 30 or higher were randomized to tirzepatide or placebo and followed for a median of 104 weeks (Packer 2025, PMID 39555826).

- Cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure: 9.9% on tirzepatide vs. 15.3% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.62; 95% CI 0.41–0.95).
- This difference was due to fewer worsening events (8.0% vs. 14.2%). Cardiovascular deaths were rare and numerically even somewhat more frequent on tirzepatide (2.2% vs. 1.4%), with a very wide range of uncertainty.
- Quality of life (KCCQ questionnaire) improved more: +19.5 versus +12.7 points.
- Discontinuations because of side effects: 6.3% vs. 1.4%.

A cardiac MRI substudy showed a reduction in left ventricular muscle mass and in the fat tissue around the heart (Kramer 2025, PMID 39566869). With 731 people and 92 endpoint events, SUMMIT is a comparatively small outcomes trial; the results are encouraging but not as robust as data from trials with thousands of participants.

#### Obstructive Sleep Apnea: SURMOUNT-OSA

In obstructive sleep apnea, the upper airway repeatedly collapses during sleep; breathing stops (apnea) or becomes shallow (hypopnea). The apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) counts these events per hour; from 30 upward, sleep apnea is considered severe. Overweight is a major risk factor.

SURMOUNT-OSA consisted of two parallel trials in people with obesity and moderate to severe sleep apnea: without positive airway pressure therapy (trial 1) and with it (CPAP, trial 2). Baseline AHI was about 50 events per hour. After 52 weeks (Malhotra 2024, PMID 38912654):

- Trial 1: AHI −25.3 events/hour with tirzepatide vs. −5.3 with placebo.
- Trial 2: AHI −29.3 vs. −5.5.
- In addition, hypoxic burden, inflammatory markers (hsCRP), blood pressure, and sleep complaints reported by participants improved.

On this basis, tirzepatide was approved in the USA as the first medication for treating sleep apnea in obesity. It does not automatically replace a breathing mask: even after the reduction, AHI in many participants was still in the range requiring treatment. The decision on therapy belongs in the hands of sleep medicine specialists.

#### Fatty Liver Disease (MASH)

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH, formerly NASH) is an inflammatory form of fatty liver disease that can progress to scarring (fibrosis) and cirrhosis. In the phase 2 trial SYNERGY-NASH, 190 people with biopsy-confirmed MASH and moderate to severe fibrosis received tirzepatide or placebo. After 52 weeks, MASH resolved without worsening of fibrosis in 44–62% on tirzepatide versus 10% on placebo; an improvement in fibrosis of at least one stage was seen in 51–55% versus 30% (Loomba 2024, PMID 38856224). A phase 3 trial is ongoing (see Outlook).

#### Other Areas of Use and Meta-Analyses

The literature on tirzepatide is growing very rapidly. In addition to the large programs, there are studies on psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis in combination with biologics: in an open-label phase 3b trial with 274 people, 27.1% on ixekizumab plus tirzepatide simultaneously achieved complete clearance of skin lesions (PASI 100) and at least 10% weight loss, compared with 5.8% on ixekizumab alone (Lebwohl 2026, PMID 42139049). Further work addresses polycystic ovary syndrome, kidney function, and Asia-specific populations (SURMOUNT-J, SURMOUNT-CN, SURPASS-AP-Combo). A complete presentation of all individual studies would go beyond the scope of this book; they are listed in the index of studies in the appendix.

On body composition: in a DXA substudy of SURMOUNT-1 with 160 people, about 75% of the weight lost consisted of fat and about 25% of fat-free mass (which includes muscle) – in the same ratio as in the placebo group (Look 2025, PMID 39996356). The concern about muscle loss is therefore justified, but it is not an effect specific to tirzepatide; rather, it is typical of any substantial weight loss.

### Animal and laboratory studies

The preclinical development is unusually well published. In cell cultures, tirzepatide activates both receptors; in mice, it lowered weight and food intake more than a pure GLP-1 agonist (Coskun 2018, PMID 30473097). Studies in mice without a functioning GLP-1 receptor showed that the GIP component improves insulin sensitivity in adipose tissue (Samms 2021, PMID 34003802). Studies on distribution and breakdown in the body are available in humans, rats, and monkeys: the molecule is metabolized by cleavage of the peptide backbone and breakdown of the fatty acid chain; in humans, about 66% of the labeled substance was found in urine and about 33% in feces, whereas unchanged tirzepatide was found in neither (Martin 2024, PMID 39243911).

As with all long-acting GLP-1 agonists, tumors of the thyroid C cells occurred in long-term studies in rodents. Whether this finding is transferable to humans is unclear, because rodents carry considerably more GLP-1 receptors on these cells. This animal finding is the basis for the warnings in the prescribing information (see below).

### Risks and side effects

**Established from studies:**

- **Gastrointestinal complaints** are by far the most common: nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation, decreased appetite. In SURPASS-3, 12–24% reported nausea, 15–17% diarrhea, and 6–10% vomiting (Ludvik 2021, PMID 34370970). The complaints occur mainly during the dose-escalation phase and usually subside. They are the most common reason for discontinuing therapy.
- **Gallbladder and bile ducts:** A meta-analysis of nine trials with 9,871 participants found an increased risk for the composite category of gallbladder or biliary disease compared with placebo or insulin (relative risk 1.97; 95% CI 1.14–3.42), but no statistically significant increase for pancreatitis (Zeng 2023, PMID 37908750). Rapid weight loss itself promotes gallstones.
- **Hypoglycemia:** rare on its own. The risk increases when insulin or sulfonylureas are taken at the same time – in SURPASS-4, hypoglycemia without a sulfonylurea occurred in 1–3% (Del Prato 2021, PMID 34672967).
- **Mental health:** A follow-up analysis of SURMOUNT-1 to -3 with 4,056 people without known serious mental illness found no indication of increased depression; suicidal thoughts were reported by 0.6% in both groups (Wadden 2026, PMID 41537305). Sufficient data are lacking for people with serious mental illness.
- **Loss of fat-free mass:** about a quarter of the weight lost (Look 2025, PMID 39996356). Strength training and adequate protein intake are recommended by experts as countermeasures; hard study data on the functional benefit are limited.

**From post-marketing data:** An analysis of the US adverse event database FAERS found disproportionately many reports of nausea, pancreatitis, diabetic retinal disease, and medullary thyroid cancer – but no increased risk compared with other GLP-1 agonists (Caruso 2024, PMID 39141075). Such reporting data show signals, not causes: known risks are reported more often. In a large cohort study with more than 46,000 pairs in routine care, the risk of serious gastrointestinal events (pancreatitis, biliary disease, bowel obstruction, gastroparesis, severe constipation) did not differ substantially between tirzepatide and semaglutide (hazard ratio 1.07; 95% CI 0.90–1.26) (Crisafulli 2026, PMID 41183330).

**Theoretical or derived from animal data:** medullary thyroid cancer (animal finding, see above). For this reason, the prescribing information includes a warning or contraindication in the case of a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma and in MEN 2 syndrome.

**Important for practice (according to the prescribing information, not to be understood as instructions):** Delayed gastric emptying can affect the absorption of tablets, which is relevant, for example, for the contraceptive pill. Tirzepatide is not used during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Before operations under anesthesia, delayed gastric emptying must be taken into account. The prescribing information and the treating physician provide all details.

**Copycat products:** During the shortages of 2022 to 2024, tirzepatide preparations were made in pharmacies in the USA ("compounding"), and at the same time a large online gray market emerged. A 2025 investigation found 33 preparations containing semaglutide or tirzepatide in dosage forms that are not approved – such as lozenges or mixtures with vitamins; data on efficacy and safety are lacking for these products (Belcourt 2026, PMID 41689811). This applies to an even greater degree to "research" products without a marketing authorization (see Part I and Part IV).

### Legal status

- **EU / Austria / Germany:** Tirzepatide is centrally authorized in the EU as Mounjaro (2022 for type 2 diabetes, since late 2023 also for weight management). It is prescription-only. Whether health insurance covers the costs depends on the indication: for type 2 diabetes, reimbursement is possible in principle; in Germany, weight-reduction drugs are largely excluded from reimbursement by law, and in Austria separate, restrictive rules apply. Details change – please check for current information.
- **USA:** Approved as Mounjaro (2022, type 2 diabetes) and as Zepbound (2023, chronic weight management; in 2024 additionally obstructive sleep apnea in obesity). Since the end of the official shortage, the FDA has severely restricted the production of copies in pharmacies.
- **Sport:** Tirzepatide is not on the WADA Prohibited List (status as of 2026; please check for current information).
- **Gray market:** Trading in unapproved tirzepatide products ("for research use only") is not a legal route to a medicine in the EU; the products are not subject to any quality control.

### Ongoing studies and outlook

**Established (registered studies, status as of October 2026):**

- **SURMOUNT-MMO** (NCT05556512): outcomes trial with about 15,000 people with obesity without diabetes and high cardiovascular risk, tirzepatide versus placebo. The primary endpoint comprises myocardial infarction, stroke, revascularization, heart failure events, and death from any cause; planned completion in 2027. According to the design publication, it is the first incretin outcomes trial to investigate both primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease (Lam 2025, PMID 40545827).
- **SYNERGY-Outcomes** (NCT07165028): phase 3 master protocol on fatty liver disease with a planned 4,500 participants.
- **Type 1 diabetes with obesity** (NCT06914895, NCT06962280): two phase 3 trials.
- **Adolescents with obesity** (NCT06439277, SURMOUNT-ADOLESCENTS-2) and children (NCT07817875).
- **Direct comparison with the successor retatrutide** (NCT06662383, see the chapter Retatrutide).
- Numerous studies on knee osteoarthritis, psoriasis, kidney disease, and stroke.

**Assessment:** In 2026, tirzepatide is the benchmark against which new weight-loss medications must be measured. The next big question is whether SURMOUNT-MMO will show a cardiovascular benefit in people without diabetes similar to that shown by the SELECT trial for semaglutide. Competition is coming from triple agonists (retatrutide), amylin combinations (CagriSema, amycretin), and tablets (orforglipron). When the patents expire and cheaper copycat products become possible is covered in Part V.

### Conclusion

Tirzepatide is a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist that has been studied in one of the largest trial programs in diabetes and obesity medicine. In SURMOUNT-1, body weight at the highest dose decreased on average by about 21%, and in the direct comparison with semaglutide (SURMOUNT-5) by 20.2% versus 13.7%. Against dulaglutide, it was noninferior for cardiovascular events, and favorable effects were seen in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and in sleep apnea. Side effects mainly affect the gastrointestinal tract and the gallbladder; after discontinuation, much of the weight returns. Treatment belongs in the hands of a physician – with the original product, not with gray-market goods.
